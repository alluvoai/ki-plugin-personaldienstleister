# Approve-Stundenfreigabe (Review Stundenzettel)

## Purpose

View submitted hours (Stundenfreigabe) per employee and billing month and prepare them for approval.

> **No longer read-only on hours.** Two period-level actions now write: `approve_by_operator`
> releases a period on the client's behalf, and `revoke_approval` takes an approval back — both
> via `manage-record-action` on `0-426` (see *Releasing and un-releasing the whole period*).
> Alongside them: `revoke_release` on a single released day (`0-427`, mandatory reason),
> `correct_signer` on a granted Freigabe (`0-435`, mandatory reason) when the wrong client
> representative was named, delegating an approval task via `manage-task`, setting an employee's go-live date via
> `manage-record-settings`, filing a Beleg for an employee (`submit_beleg` on `0-2`), and the
> reimbursement actions further down — each two-stage (preview → confirm).

## What MCP reaches — and what it still does not

**Nothing here confirms a period on the client's or the employee's behalf as if they had, and
nothing rejects one.** Their confirmation is their own act, and a contested period is resolved in
the Stundenklärung flow, which has no MCP write. What the operator can do without leaving MCP has
grown, though: a period the client will not sign can be released by the Verleiher under its own
channel, and an approval given in error can be taken back.

- **Read** `Timesheet` (`0-426`), `TimesheetDay` (`0-427`) and `TimesheetRelease` (`0-435`) with
  the generic query tools.
- **Write, through `manage-record-action` only** — four actions, each two-stage
  (`confirmed: false` preview → confirm → `confirmed: true`):
  - `approve_by_operator` on the period (`0-426`) — release it on the client's behalf.
  - `revoke_approval` on the period (`0-426`) — take a whole approval back to `open`.
  - `revoke_release` on a released day (`0-427`) — undo one premature or wrong client sign-off.
  - `correct_signer` on a sign-off (`0-435`) — move a standing release's attribution to the
    client representative who actually signed. It takes nothing back.
- Two narrow tools alongside: `manage-custom-field` *defines* custom fields on the period, and
  `manage-task` can hang a task on it.

Everything else about a period — its status, minutes, confirmations and corrections — is still
refused by `manage-model`; those are service-owned and never a direct write. See "The operator's
Stundennachweis list" below for the exact reach.

> **Three action names, two identical German labels — pick by model type *and* status.** Both
> `revoke_approval` and `revoke_release` are labelled "Freigabe zurücknehmen"; running the wrong
> one fails with a state error rather than doing the other thing.
>
> | Action | On | Only while | Does |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | `approve_by_operator` ("Ohne Kunden freigeben") | period `0-426` | `open` | releases the whole period without the client |
> | `revoke_approval` ("Freigabe zurücknehmen") | period `0-426` | `approved` | takes the whole period's approval back |
> | `revoke_release` ("Freigabe zurücknehmen") | day `0-427` | `open` (and the day released) | undoes **one** signed day of an open period |
>
> When an operator says "Freigabe zurücknehmen", ask which: one day the client signed too early,
> or the whole approved period.
>
> **`correct_signer` ("Unterzeichner korrigieren", on the Freigabe `0-435`) is not part of that
> collision** and is not an undo — it changes *who* a standing release names while leaving the
> release itself in place. "Die falsche Person hat unterschrieben" means one of two very different
> things: the wrong person *was asked to sign at all* (→ `revoke_release` / `revoke_approval`), or
> the right person signed and the **wrong name was picked** for them (→ `correct_signer`). Ask
> which before you write.

## Prerequisites

- Employee ID (`0-2`) or Assignment ID (`0-401`) known, or a period (month) provided for a broad search.
- Exclusively via `mcp__alluvo__*`.

## Steps

### 1. Search for Time Entries

Use `search-model` or `query-model` for the time entry model type (`TimeEntry`, resolve via
`list-model-types`). Filterable fields:

- `employee_id`, `assignment_id`
- `type` — enum, `work` | `break`
- `start` — datetime range for the month / period

> **There is no approval status on a time entry.** `TimeEntry` carries no `status` field —
> approval state lives on a separate `TimeEntryApproval` record, which has **no MCP model
> type**. A filter like `status: submitted` therefore matches nothing; scope by employee /
> assignment / period instead and read approved-vs-open in the web app (step 4).

### 2. Prepare Entries — a worked day is several rows, not one

> **Sum the day; never read a single entry as the day.** Submitting a shift writes
> work → break → work, so one ordinary "06:00–14:12 with a 30-minute break" day is **two
> `work` rows plus one `break` row**. Group entries by calendar day, keep `type: work`, and sum
> `duration_hours` (DB-derived, already net). Taking one row per day is how a 15,4 h weekend
> gets reported to an employee as 6,73 h.

Per employee / assignment:
- Total of submitted hours (summed as above)
- Breakdown by day (date · first start – last end · summed work hours · comment)
- Anomalies: days > 10h (ArbZG §3), missing comments, deviations from planned Schicht

> **The day's break is derived, not read off a column.** It is the span (first start → last
> end) minus the summed work minutes — which also captures paused timers and untracked gaps,
> not just an explicit `break` row. Compare *that* figure against the § 4 ArbZG break, not the
> `break` entry's own duration.

> **The §3 10h day is the employee's whole day, not this Einsatz's.** Sum every Schicht the
> employee worked on that date — a second Teildienst the same day, and hours under a *different*
> Einsatzvertrag, count too. Two separately harmless 6h entries at two clients are a 12h §3
> breach. Check the employee across assignments before signing off a day as unremarkable.

> **A planned Schicht's hours are net of the § 4 ArbZG break** (span − break; minimum 0 up to
> 6h00 span, 30 min over 6h00 up to 9h30, 45 min beyond). A 9h00–9h30 Schicht therefore plans
> 30 minutes, not 45 — see `→ build-dienstplan`. Compare submitted hours against that net figure
> before calling a 15-minute gap a deviation.

> Two facts about the plan you compare against: the employee app now displays exactly this
> net-of-break figure in its Dienstplan wizard, so "the app showed a different number" no
> longer explains a gap — and employees can add, retime, or cancel single Schichten on their
> own running Einsatz without operator involvement (`→ build-dienstplan`, step 6b), so compare
> against the plan as it is *now*, not as it was last planned by the operator.

> **Since 2026-09-02 the Soll of an open day can still move.** From the Zeiterfassung the employee
> may change („Dienst ändern“) or cancel („Dienst entfällt“) the planned Schicht itself — including
> one that has already started or lies on a past day — for as long as the day is **open**: not
> closed by them, not signed off by the client. So a deviation you looked at yesterday may be gone
> today because the *plan* moved rather than the hours, and the Tagesabschluss is what freezes both
> (see *Changing a closed day* below). Re-read the day before writing to the operator, and read a
> Plan/Ist match on a re-opened day as "the roster was corrected", not as a suspicious rewrite —
> the change carries the AÜG § 11 change-log row and the employee digest either way
> (`→ build-dienstplan`, step 6a).

> **"Die App sagt mir X h unter Soll" is a different Soll from this one.** The Dienstplan
> wizard's review step shows a month-wide comparison against the employee's **salary** Soll,
> summed across all their Einsätze (`→ build-dienstplan`, step 6b). The Soll on the Stundenzettel
> and the Tätigkeitsnachweis is the frozen plan of each timesheet day. The two are supposed to
> differ — do not reconcile them against each other, and do not go looking for the gap inside the
> submitted Zeitraum.

### 3. View Individual Entry Details

Use `get-model` for a specific time entry if the operator wants to check details (e.g. employee's note, attached documents).

### 4. Approval — the client's signature first, the operator release as the exception

The normal route to Approved is still the client signing: in the Kundenportal, on site on the
employee's device, or — where the company has deadlines enabled — by the Frist lapsing. None of
those is something you run.

**Where the client will not sign at all**, the period is releasable over MCP with
`approve_by_operator` on `0-426` (see *Releasing and un-releasing the whole period* below). Treat
it as the exception it is: it releases hours for billing that the customer never agreed to, it
demands every day be closed first, and the Tätigkeitsnachweis it produces states outright that no
Entleiher confirmation exists. Ask what the operator has actually agreed with the client before
proposing it.

For everything else, direct the operator to the web app. The navigation is **HR & Payroll →
Stundennachweise** (EN "Timesheet Records", `/objects/timesheets`) for the periods themselves, and
**HR & Payroll → Stundenklärung** (EN "Timesheet Mediation") for the ones in `mediation` — that
flow has no MCP write. Do not quote the old path "Zeiterfassung → Freigaben" — no such menu exists.

Alternatively: use `manage-task` to assign an approval task to a colleague — hang it on the period
itself with `taskable_type: "timesheets"` and the period's id, so the colleague opens the task on
the record they have to act on rather than a title that merely describes it.

## The operator's Stundennachweis list — readable, not writable

Until recently an approval period had **no** internal surface: the Stundenklärung queue lists only
periods in `mediation`, so a pending or already approved period was visible to the
client and to the employee but nowhere to an operator. There is now a **Stundennachweis** record
list — read-only by design, in the web app and over MCP alike.

**Where it is.** HR & Payroll → **Stundennachweise** (`/objects/timesheets`), deliberately
placed between *Zeiteinträge* (raw tracked time) and *Stundenklärung* (the exception queue), because
the queue used to be the only entry and made the exception look like the main record.

> **A missing menu entry is a permission, not a bug.** The item is gated on the
> `timesheets.view` permission (families `timesheets.*`, `timesheet_days.*`,
> `timesheet_day_corrections.*`). Those exist but are not granted to any role by default, so a
> Disponent who does not see the entry needs the permission — do not diagnose it as a rollout or
> activation problem.

**What it shows.** One row per approval period: Zeitraum (the `period_label`), Mitarbeiter,
Einsatzbetrieb, **Status**, **Phase**, Soll, Ist, Abweichung, Kundenfreigabe — plus Vereinbart, Von,
Bis, Diensttage gesamt and Freigegebene Diensttage available as hidden columns. It filters on
employee, Einsatz, status, `has_deviation`, `date_from` / `date_until`, `client_confirmed_at`,
`employee_confirmed_at` and `deadline_at`, and carries one saved view per status — **five** of them
(Offen, Wartet auf Mitarbeiter, In Klärung, Freigegeben, Abgerechnet). The record page
hangs under the Mitarbeiter and opens the period's Tage, **Freigaben**, Zeiteinträge, Korrekturen
and Dokumente.

> **The list lands on "Alle".** The first tab is **Alle** and it is what the list resolves to when
> nothing else is chosen — there is nothing left to hide, because a period with nothing to release
> is not a row any more (it is in the trash, see below). The other views are **Dieser Monat** /
> **Letzter Monat** (both by period *overlap*, so a week straddling a month boundary appears in
> both) and one tab per status. There is no **Aktiv** tab and no **Kann sich nicht selbst heilen**
> tab — do not send an operator looking for either.

### The status vocabulary — five values, and three things the status does not say

`TimesheetStatus` has **five** values, and nothing else is valid on `0-426`. A filter or saved
view sending anything outside this list matches nothing and silently reads zero:

| Value | Label | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| `open` | Offen | the period exists and the approval process has not finished — see below, this covers two situations |
| `pending_employee` | Wartet auf Mitarbeiter | the employee's own counter-confirmation is outstanding |
| `mediation` | In Klärung | the figures are contested and an operator resolves it in the Stundenklärung |
| `approved` | Freigegeben | signed off, billable |
| `invoiced` | Abgerechnet | picked up by a billing run |

> **`closed` / "Ohne Einsatz" is gone too, one release after it shipped.** There is deliberately no
> status for "nothing to certify": such a period is **not created**, and one that becomes so is
> **soft-deleted** (see *Which periods the employee actually sees* below). Never name `closed` to an
> operator and never send it in a filter — the API rejects the value.
>
> **`upcoming`, `pending_client`, `disputed`, `escalated`, `mediating` and `draft` are gone as well.**
> `upcoming` and `pending_client` both became `open`; `disputed`, `escalated` and `mediating` all
> became `mediation`. Do not filter, report or quote any of them — a `query-model` on `0-426` with
> one of those values returns an empty set and a backlog figure built that way reads zero.
> (This is the timesheet status only: the Dienstplan-Periode `SchedulePeriod` (`0-110`) and
> Reimbursement still have a genuine `draft` status — `→ build-dienstplan`.)

**`open` covers two different situations, and the status alone cannot tell them apart.** "Nothing
has been sent to the client yet" and "submitted, the Frist is running" are both `open`; the
distinction lives in the **`submitted_to_client_at`** stamp, not in a status. So when an operator
asks "liegt die Woche beim Kunden?", read that column — never infer it from `open`. It is stamped
when the nightly deadline run promotes an ended period into the client queue, and on the first
full client confirmation; `revoke_approval` deliberately leaves it standing (the week genuinely
was submitted).

**Three derived reads sit beside the status; keep them apart.**

- **`lifecycle_phase`** — a materialised column and its own **Phase** table column: `upcoming`
  (Anstehend) ▸ `in_approval` (In Freigabe) ▸ `approved` (Freigegeben) ▸ `invoiced` (Abgerechnet).
  Four stations, and it only ever moves forward. `open` maps to
  *Anstehend* while nothing is submitted and no day is released, and to *In Freigabe* once either is
  true; `pending_employee` and `mediation` both map to *In Freigabe*. Note the collision: `upcoming`
  is a valid **phase**, never a status.
- **Awaited party** — "whose move is it", derived per request and **never stored**: `client`
  (Kunde), `time_tracking` (Zeiterfassung), `employee` (Mitarbeiter), `mediation` (Klärung),
  `nobody` (Niemand). An `open` period yields *Kunde* only when at least one day is signable right
  now; where nothing is, it yields *Zeiterfassung* — the ball is with the employee even though the
  status says the period is open toward the client. Quote this, not the raw status, before telling
  an operator to chase a customer. There is **no filterable column** behind it, so it cannot be
  used in a `query-model` filter.
- **Release progress** — `none` / `partial` / `complete`, folded from each day's own release state
  (`released`, `signable`, `pending`, `awaiting_closure`, `gap`, `exempt`). A period awaiting the
  client can additionally be *partial*: after a Teilfreigabe the client may sign some days while
  the rest are still stuck on the employee, which the operator surfaces render as "Kunde
  (teilweise)". Report the partial case as such — a plain "wartet auf Kunde" over-states it.

> **There is no "Unterzeichner" column and no `client_channel` filter any more.** Both read
> week-level columns that nothing writes since Teilfreigabe shipped — a period can carry several
> sign-offs by different people through different channels, which one set of columns cannot hold.
> Do not send an operator looking for them, and do not filter a `query-model` call by
> `client_channel` on `0-426`. "Wer hat unterschrieben" is answered on the **Freigaben** tab, or
> over MCP on `TimesheetRelease` (`0-435`) — see the next section.
>
> `client_confirmed_at` on the period survives and now means exactly one thing: **every** day of
> it is released. On a partially signed period it is empty, and that is correct, not a lost
> signature — read `Freigegebene Diensttage` / `Diensttage gesamt` for how far along it is.

**Two actions, both in the app, both link-only:**

- **"Tätigkeitsnachweis öffnen"** — the signed Tätigkeitsnachweis PDF, generated on the spot if the
  background job has not produced it yet. Offered only on an `approved` or `invoiced` period, since
  that is all the endpoint serves. This is what replaces hand-assembling the PDF URL.
- **"Zur Vermittlung"** — jumps to the Stundenklärung queue. Offered only on a period in
  `mediation`, because on any other period the record does not appear in that queue.

> **Readable over MCP, never writable through a generic tool.** Three of the five types are on
> the MCP allowlist and appear as usable in `list-model-types` (the other two are listed too,
> flagged `not_available`):
>
> | Type | MCP reach |
> |---|---|
> | `Timesheet` (`0-426`) | **read** — `search-model`, `query-model`, `get-model`, `count-model`, `get-model-schema` |
> | `TimesheetDay` (`0-427`) | **read**, plus the one record action `revoke_release` (below) |
> | `TimesheetRelease` (`0-435`) | **read** — the sign-off events of a period (below) — plus the one record action `correct_signer` |
> | `TimesheetDayCorrection` (`0-428`) | **not on the allowlist** — every generic tool refuses it |
> | `TimesheetDayRelease` (`0-436`) | **not on the allowlist** — read the per-day snapshot off the period's Freigaben tab in the app |
>
> **A day carries the deviation reason as two fields, and the classification is filterable.**
> Beside the free-text `deviation_reason` ("Abweichungsgrund"), `0-427` has
> `deviation_reason_code` ("Abweichungsgrund (Kategorie)"). It is sortable **and filterable** —
> alongside `deviation_flag`, the only two filterable fields the day has — and that is the whole
> reason it exists: "wie oft mussten Leute länger bleiben?" is one `query-model` on `0-427` with
> `deviation_reason_code: "stayed_longer"` plus `aggregate: {function: "count"}`, and cannot be
> answered off a free-text column at all. Confirm the exact field list with `get-model-schema`
> (`context: "list"`) before building any other filter, and expect **older days to carry no code**
> — only days closed since the pair shipped have one.
>
> **The code is no longer a closed enum — it points at a tenant-editable catalogue.** The former
> fixed list is gone; the categories are rows of **`ShiftDeviationReason`**, which the tenant may
> add to, relabel and deactivate (see *Abweichungsgründe — a tenant-editable catalogue* below).
> The column itself did not change — it still stores the row's `code` string, with **no foreign
> key** — which is why every historical day keeps resolving. Two consequences for anything you
> filter or report:
>
> - **Never present the eight seeded codes as "the" list.** `stayed_longer`, `handover_delayed`,
>   `understaffed`, `left_early`, `sent_home_early`, `started_late`, `break_differs` and `other`
>   are the platform defaults; this tenant may have added its own and deactivated some. Read the
>   catalogue before naming categories to an operator.
> - **A code on an old day may no longer be in the catalogue at all**, and its label may have been
>   rewritten since the day was closed. Resolve a code against the catalogue for display, and treat
>   a code you cannot resolve as a historical value, not as bad data.
>
>
> **A stored `deviation_minutes: 0` is not always a verdict.** The builder writes `0` with
> `deviation_flag: none` for an *excused* day — one whose planned end has not passed yet, or one a
> declared absence covers — and on the column that zero is indistinguishable from a computed one.
> The period's **Tage** card no longer renders it as a green ✓ „Keine Abweichung": a stored `0` on
> a day with no binding Ist (nothing tracked, no agreed correction) now shows a dash there. Only
> the card's payload changed — the stored column is untouched, so `query-model` on `0-427` and the
> Tätigkeitsnachweis still read `0`. Never report "keine Abweichung" off a zero without checking
> whether the day is due at all and carries an Ist; a real shortfall — day due, nothing worked —
> is a negative number and stands.
> **`search-model` on `0-426` actually filters now — it used to silently return everything.**
> The period had no searchable column at all, and a search with no searchable column does not
> filter: it was a no-op that handed back the full (paginated) list with no hint that the `q` was
> ignored. Never build on the old behaviour, and never treat a Timesheet result set as
> "everything there is" because a `q` came back full. The search matches, tokenized and
> case-insensitive:
>
> | What you can type | Path it matches |
> |---|---|
> | Mitarbeiter-Vorname | `employee.contact.first_name` |
> | Mitarbeiter-Nachname | `employee.contact.last_name` |
> | Personalnummer | `employee.employee_number` |
> | Einsatzbetrieb | `assignment.assignmentContract.company.name` |
> | Einsatzbetrieb-Alias | `assignment.assignmentContract.company.alternative_names` |
>
> Every token has to hit somewhere, so `q: "Sabine Vogel"` matches across first and last name, and
> `q: "UKD"` finds the Uniklinik through its alias — the same semantics as global search. So the
> entry point for "welche Zeiträume hat X" is one call: `search-model` on `0-426` with the name or
> the Personalnummer. Do **not** resolve the Employee (`0-2`) first only to re-query `0-426` by
> `employee_id` — that detour is obsolete. (An `employee_id` filter is still the right tool when
> you already hold the id, e.g. after a `get-model`.)
>
> **One caveat on the bare quick search.** `q` alone returns id + label pairs, and a period's label
> is `Tätigkeitsnachweis <Name>` — it does not carry the Zeitraum, so several periods of the same
> employee come back looking identical. Add `limit` / `fields` / `filter_groups` (list mode) and
> read `period_label`, `date_from` / `date_until` and `status` before you name a period to the
> operator.
>
> **`manage-model` create/update is refused on all five.** None of them is in the tool's managed
> types, so a generic write comes back rejected — a period's status/minutes/confirmations are
> lifecycle state owned by `TimesheetConfirmationService` and the mediation flow, a day's
> Ist/Soll/agreed spans belong to the builder, and a release row is written only by
> `TimesheetReleaseService`. That denial is the guard working; no `confirmed: true` and no
> broader permission gets around it. Bypassing it would move hours out from under a client
> signature — the same integrity problem as fabricating a release (see the go-live section below).
>
> The three readable types **can** be an automation-workflow trigger (`trigger_model_type` accepts
> any MCP-readable type) — `0-428` cannot. The two record actions above ("Tätigkeitsnachweis
> öffnen", "Zur Vermittlung") are reachable through `manage-record-action`, but both are
> **link-only** — executing one hands back the URL and writes nothing, so they remain things you
> *tell an operator to click*. The one action you can run that changes state is `revoke_release`
> on `0-427`.
>
> **The period's one state-changing action, `push_open_days_to_next_period`, is web-app-only.**
> "Offene Tage in die Folgeperiode schieben" (below) genuinely writes — it moves a period boundary
> — but it carries no `McpExposed` marker, so `manage-record-action` refuses it on `0-426` however
> it is named. Explain it and send the operator to the period record; never offer to run it.
>
> **The one type-specific exception: custom fields.** `manage-custom-field` accepts
> `model_type: "0-426"` — `create`, `list`, `update`, `archive` and `reorder` all work on the
> Stundennachweis, two-stage like everywhere else (`confirmed: false` preview → `confirmed: true`).
> Only the period itself: `0-427` and `0-428` are not supported, so there are no day-level custom
> fields. This defines the field, it does **not** let you fill it — `manage-model` refuses `0-426`
> like every other write, so a `cp_*` value is written in the web app only. Do not promise the
> operator you can fill the field you just defined.
>
> **A note does attach to a Stundennachweis now.** `manage-activity` with `activity_type: note`,
> `action: create`, `subject_type: "0-426"` and the period's id logs a note **on the Zeitraum** —
> two-stage like every write (`confirmed: false` preview → `confirmed: true`), and readable back
> with `get-timeline` on the same `subject_type` / `subject_id`. It is the same note the record page
> shows, so there is no longer a reason to park a period remark on the linked Kontakt. The accepted
> note subjects are **derived** from the models that carry notes, not a hand-kept list, so the set
> widens on its own: confirm an id with `list-model-types`, and a rejected `subject_type` names the
> accepted ones back to you. The gate is `view` on the record through its own policy
> (`timesheets.view` plus your row-level scope) — not a `*.edit` permission, because the note hangs
> off the period without changing a field on it. `follow_up_task` works here too, since `0-426` also
> carries tasks — but noteable and taskable are two different lists, so on a noteable record without
> tasks the call is rejected up front rather than half-succeeding.
>
> **Calls and meetings still do not.** They stay on `0-105` (Contact), `0-3` (Company), `0-30`
> (Rahmenvertrag) and `0-31` (AÜV) — a communication happens with a person, an organisation or
> against a contract. A callback with the Entleiher about this week belongs on the Kontakt, not on
> the period.
>
> A note does not replace the two purpose-built texts: a remark about **one day** is still the day's
> **Bemerkung**, and a clarification is still `review_note` (both below). Use a note for a remark
> about the whole Zeitraum that has no field of its own — and never to record hours, a signature or
> an approval, none of which a note carries.
>
> A task attaches as well: `manage-task` takes `taskable_type: "timesheets"` with the period's id —
> which is what the delegation step above uses.
>
> The read-only stance is not a gap to route around: the sanctioned writes stay the confirmation,
> correction and revocation paths; the list has no edit form, no create button and no bulk delete.

And the label rule the list itself follows: the Zeitraum column is `period_label`. **Never rebuild
it as "KW {iso_week}"** — a period is a segment of the tenant's period scheme and routinely spans
two ISO weeks (see *Abrechnungszeitraum* below).

## A Kundenfreigabe is an event, not a checkmark on the period (Teilfreigabe)

A client sign-off is a **`TimesheetRelease` (`0-435`)** — one person released a set of days, at a
moment, through one channel, with one signature — and a period can carry **several**. The client
signs Monday to Thursday today and Friday tomorrow, possibly a different person through a different
channel. Each event stores a per-day snapshot of the values that were actually signed.

This replaces "the week has a signature". Four consequences change what you tell an operator:

- **A partial release no longer locks the rest of the period.** Only the *released days* are
  closed to the employee; days the client left open stay fully writable, so the employee can go on
  tracking and correcting them. Previously the first Teilfreigabe locked the whole week — a day
  missed before that signature was uncorrectable, and the period could never complete. Do not
  repeat the old rule.
- **The lock is per Einsatz.** An employee on two assignments can have the same date signed off at
  company A and still open at company B. A locked day at one client says nothing about the other.
- **A day whose shift has not ended is not releasable — *unless the employee has closed it*.**
  "Tag abschließen" is a stronger statement about the day than the plan's end time is, so a day
  the employee has declared finished is releasable straight away: someone who finishes at 11:35
  against a plan running to 19:00 releases those hours at 11:35, not after 19:00. A day **nobody
  has closed** keeps the old behaviour and stays blocked until its planned end has passed — not by
  hand on the employee's device, and not by the deadline run either, so a client still cannot sign
  a running Friday at 09:16 and freeze it at 0 h against a 7h42 plan. Withdrawing the closure
  re-blocks the day. Read "Der Kunde kann den heutigen Tag nicht abzeichnen" as a Tagesabschluss
  question first (gate below), not as a clock question — telling an operator "das geht erst nach
  Feierabend" as an unconditional rule is wrong.
- **A release can be taken back** — operator-only, with a mandatory reason, at two levels: one
  day of an open period (`revoke_release` on `0-427`) or the whole approval of an approved period
  (`revoke_approval` on `0-426`). Both below.
- **A release's signer can be corrected without taking the release back** — `correct_signer` on
  `0-435`, operator-only, mandatory reason. The signature, the moment, the channel and the signed
  days stay exactly as they are; only the name attached to them moves. Below.
- **Fristablauf never overwrites a human signature any more.** Deemed approval releases exactly
  the days nobody signed, as its own event with no signer, and leaves an existing on-site or portal
  signature standing. See *Automatische Freigabe*.

### Reading who signed what — `TimesheetRelease` (`0-435`)

Read-only, on the MCP allowlist. One row per sign-off, carrying `released_at`, `channel`
(`portal` / on-site signature / offline / `auto_approved`), `signer_name`, `released_by_contact_id`,
the number of days it covered (`day_releases_count`) and how many of those still stand
(`active_day_releases_count`). The two counts differ exactly when an operator has revoked a day
out of it. `signer_label` is the readable "who": it fills in for a deemed approval, where nobody
signed and `signer_name` is deliberately empty — it is an appended accessor, so **do not filter on
it** (a computed field is rejected, not silently ignored).

**Discover the filter surface before you scope a query.** `0-435` exposes no filter schema of its
own, so run `get-model-schema` (context `list`) and filter on what it advertises; the dotted
relation path (`timesheet.id`, `timesheet.employee_id`) is the reliable way to scope releases to a
period or an employee. In the web app the same history is the **Freigaben** tab on the period.

The per-day snapshot (`TimesheetDayRelease`, `0-436`) is **not** MCP-reachable — "welche Stunden
genau wurden gezeichnet" is read on that tab, or off the Tätigkeitsnachweis.

### Correcting who a release names — `correct_signer` on `TimesheetRelease` (`0-435`)

The only write on `0-435`, and the sanctioned repair for a wrong § 17c AÜG attribution. The case it
exists for: the employee holds out the phone on site, the client's representative is picked from a
list of dozens of contacts, and the wrong one is tapped — so a person who was never in the room
stands under the signature. Everything else the release records stays immutable; this moves the
**attribution only**. It is a real MCP write via `manage-record-action`, two-stage like the rest:

```
manage-record-action(model_type: "0-435", record_id: <release id>,
                     operation: "execute", action: "correct_signer",
                     data: { contact_id: <contact id>,
                             reason: "Mitarbeiter hat auf dem Gerät den falschen Ansprechpartner ausgewählt" },
                     confirmed: false)   # preview first, then confirmed: true
```

- **`contact_id` is required and must be a `Contact` of the Einsatz's own Entleiher** — the company
  on the assignment contract behind the period. Anyone else is refused with a validation error
  naming that company: a § 17c signature belongs to that client and to nobody else. Resolve the
  candidate off *that company's* contacts before calling, not off a tenant-wide name search.
- **`reason` is required** and is written to the provenance log together with the previous and the
  new signer (event `timesheet_release_signer_corrected`). A blank one is refused.
- **Keep `confirmed` at the top level and out of `data`.** Top-level `confirmed: false` runs the
  action's own write-free before/after; `confirmed: true` executes. **The top-level switch alone
  decides preview vs. write:** top-level `confirmed: false` (or omitted) is *always* write-free —
  a `data: { confirmed: true }` sent alongside it is forced back to `false`, echoed as
  `confirmed: false` under `## Data to submit`, and the response says the key was ignored.
  `data.confirmed` is only honoured on the top-level `confirmed: true` branch (where an explicit
  `data.confirmed: false` still yields the write-free before/after). So to actually correct the
  signer, send **top-level `confirmed: true`** — `data.confirmed` is not a substitute for it.
- **The preview is a real before/after**, rendered under `## Action Preview`: `preview_only`, plus
  `previous_contact_id` / `previous_signer_name`, `new_contact_id` / `new_signer_name`, the client
  `company`, how many `days` the release covers, and `regenerates_activity_report`. Read both names
  and the day count back to the operator before confirming — this reassigns a signature.
- **Not available on a deemed approval** (`channel` = `auto_approved`). Nobody signed it, so there
  is no attribution to correct, and the action says exactly that instead of failing blankly. Do not
  present it as a temporary state that will clear.
- **It needs the `timesheet_releases.correct_signer` permission**, which is its own grant on top of
  reading Freigaben. A Disponent who sees the action listed as unavailable is missing the
  permission — check that before concluding the release cannot be corrected.
- **What it does not touch:** the drawn signature, `released_at`, the `channel` and the per-day
  snapshots all stay. Somebody really did sign, at that moment, on that mark — only the name was
  wrong. It also carries the correction into the superseded week columns on the period
  (`client_confirmed_by_contact_id` / `signer_name`) **only** while they still name the old signer;
  a week naming somebody else records a different sign-off and is deliberately left alone.
- **It re-renders the Tätigkeitsnachweis itself** when the period already has one — the PDF is
  rendered at signature time and would otherwise keep printing the wrong name. The superseded copy
  is archived, not deleted, so what the client actually saw stays findable. **Do not trigger a
  regeneration afterwards**: a second run only archives another copy for nothing. On a period that
  has not produced its Nachweis yet nothing is rendered and the next render already reads the
  corrected name — `regenerates_activity_report` in the preview tells you which case you are in.

Wrong **hours** are not this action: that is `revoke_release` on the day (below) or a correction.
Wrong **person on an otherwise correct sign-off** is this one.

### What the client sees in the portal — per-day checkboxes, and two flags not to confuse

The client releases days, not weeks: the portal's confirm call takes an optional day selection, and
the old week-level precondition ("the last day of the week must be closed") is gone — the per-day
gate is strictly stricter, and the old one blocked a whole week's releasable days over one open
day. Each day in the Dienstplan payload carries whether it is selectable, why not if it is not
(`already_released`, `future`, `running`, `not_closed`, `unsettled`, `missing_time`,
`nothing_to_release`), whether it blocks the week, and whether it is already confirmed.

> **`running` means "today, still open" — not "the clock has not passed the planned end".** It
> fires only when the day is today, its planned end is still ahead *and* the employee has not
> closed it. A closed day today comes back `selectable` with no blocked reason. Never explain
> `running` as "wait until after the shift ends"; the fix is the Tagesabschluss, and only a day
> without one waits for the clock.

> **Two week-level flags that sound alike and are not.** `canRelease` means "this week can
> **complete**" — no day is blocking it — and it gates nothing on its own. `hasReleasableDay`
> means "at least one day is signable **right now**", and *that* is the condition behind the
> release control. Confusing them hides the Freigabe for an entire week because one day is still
> open — the exact failure the day-level model exists to prevent. When a client reports "ich kann
> die Woche nicht freigeben", ask which days the screen offered before assuming the week is
> blocked.

Two more rules on that screen:

- **Sammelfreigabe is whole-week-or-nothing.** It releases only weeks where **no** day blocks the
  week; a week with one unclosed day is left out entirely rather than partially signed. A week
  that cannot be released one-by-one must not slip through the bulk path — so "warum war Woche X
  nicht in der Sammelfreigabe" is answered by the blocking day, not by a permission.
- **A day the employee has not closed yet delivers no hours to the client at all**, and is left
  out of the week's deviation figure. Its hours are a work in progress, not a shortfall the client
  could act on — so a client seeing no Ist on such a day is reading correct behaviour. The masking
  follows the same three verdicts (`not_closed`, `unsettled`, `running`), so **closing the day
  un-masks it immediately** — a client asking "warum sehe ich für heute keine Ist-Zeiten" is asking
  whether the employee has hit Feierabend yet, not whether the shift is over.

### Taking a day's release back — `revoke_release` on `TimesheetDay` (`0-427`)

The sanctioned undo when a client signed a **single day** of a still-open period prematurely or
with the wrong hours. It is a real MCP write, via `manage-record-action` on `0-427`, two-stage
like every other action:

> **Not the same action as `revoke_approval`**, which shares its German label and undoes a whole
> *approved* period on `0-426`. Day + open period here; week + approved period there.

```
manage-record-action(model_type: "0-427", record_id: <day id>,
                     operation: "execute", action: "revoke_release",
                     data: { reason: "Kunde hat Freitag mittags gezeichnet, Schicht lief bis 20:00" },
                     confirmed: false)   # preview first, then confirmed: true
```

- **`reason` is mandatory** and is kept on the release history. A blank one is refused.
- **Only while the period is still `open`** (and the day itself released). From `PendingEmployee`
  onwards the period is in somebody else's hands — the employee's counter-confirmation, mediation, an
  issued invoice — and each has its own route (a correction, the Stundenklärung queue, a credit
  note). The action reports which case it is rather than failing blankly; do not retry it against
  an approved or invoiced period. **An `Approved` period is undone with `revoke_approval` on the
  period instead** (below) — that one takes the whole week back, not one day.
- **Needs the `timesheet_days.revoke_release` permission.** Like the rest of the
  `timesheets.*` / `timesheet_days.*` families it exists but is granted to no role by default — a
  Disponent who cannot run it needs the permission, it is not a rollout gap.
- **The original signature is never erased.** The release row stays and is stamped revoked, with
  who took it back, when and why. The client did sign; a later correction must not make that
  unfindable — the same integrity rule as everywhere else in this skill.
- **What it moves:** the day becomes editable again for both sides, and if the period was fully
  released its `client_confirmed_at` is cleared (it is no longer true that every day is released).
  The period's **`status` deliberately does not move** — an `open` period simply has one
  more open day, and `submitted_to_client_at` is untouched. Do not report a status change that
  will not happen.

State the day, the client's original signature and the reason to the operator before confirming —
this reverses something a named person put their name to.

### Splitting a period so the signed days can be billed — "Offene Tage in die Folgeperiode schieben"

The second lever on a period that will not complete, and the right one when `revoke_release` is
not. The case it was built for: the client signs Mon–Thu on site because Friday's shift has not
ended yet, and nobody comes back for Friday. The period then holds four signed days it can never
certify — no `client_confirmed_at`, so no Tätigkeitsnachweis, no signature receipt and nothing to
bill — and where the company has `deadlines_enabled` off, nothing resolves it on its own.

**It is no longer limited to stragglers.** The old "the period must be over" gate is gone (product
decision, 28.08.2026): the action used to require every planned shift of the period to be worked,
and refused otherwise with *„Diese Periode wird noch gearbeitet — die offenen Tage sind geplante
Dienste, keine Nachzügler."* That refusal no longer exists. A **running** period may be split the
moment one day carries a release: the signed days become billable at once, and the open days —
planned future shifts included — move into the following period and are worked and released there.
Do not tell an operator to wait for the last shift of the period.

`push_open_days_to_next_period` on the period (`0-426`) moves the **boundary** between the two
periods rather than orphaning a day: this period's `date_until` moves back to the day before the
first open one, the open day rows are re-pointed, and the following period's `date_from` moves back
to meet them. The shortened period is then fully released and completes through the **same cascade
a final signature runs** — its Tätigkeitsnachweis is generated and the receipt goes to whoever gave
the last standing release.

**You cannot execute it — direct the operator to the record.** It carries no `McpExposed` marker,
so `manage-record-action` refuses it (see the capability box above). It needs the `timesheets.edit`
permission, and it is hidden while impersonating.

**The button appears only when all of these hold.** One evaluation drives its visibility, its
confirmation text and the write, so a refusal is never a display bug:

- the period is **still open** — from the employee's counter-confirmation (`PendingEmployee`)
  onwards the boundary is somebody else's, and the action refuses naming the status it found
  (*„Nur eine offene Periode kann Tage abgeben — diese steht auf …"*);
- **at least one day is still open**, counting only days that have something to release: an off
  day and an absence-covered day are not "open" and never block;
- **at least one day is already released.** Pushing every releasable day away would leave a period
  with nothing to release, and such a period can never complete — so it is refused;
- the open days form a **contiguous trailing run**. A blocker sitting *between* two signed days is
  refused, because moving it would tear the period into two halves that do not touch. That one is
  structural, not a product decision — it did not move when the readiness gate did.

**The following period decides the shape, not whether it runs.** Where the successor is the
contiguous next period, still open, not yet put in front of the client, and can absorb the days
without reaching past the end of the month the open days fall into, they **merge** into it. In
every other case — a successor already submitted, a gap in the chain, no successor at all — the
tail becomes a **period of its own** and the successor is not touched. So a successor sitting with
the client no longer blocks the carryover; it only decides which of the two happens. Neither shape
ever crosses a month boundary: billing buckets a period by its `date_from`, and days slid into
another month would be billed nowhere.

**What to say before they click.** The confirmation names the period's new end date, the exact days
that leave, the target range and **which of the two shapes** this is — merging into the existing
period, or splitting off a new one; the two sentences differ, and an operator confirming a
cross-period write needs the right one. Two facts worth stating with it: the moved days keep their
row ids, so their corrections and field history travel with them — and every day that leaves is
unreleased by construction, so **no signature is disturbed**. When a guard bites, the action names
which one rather than failing blankly, and each has a different next step.

This is not the tool for a period **nobody** has signed any part of — that is a different problem,
answered by chasing the Freigabe or, where the company has deadlines enabled, by the Frist.

### What the Tätigkeitsnachweis actually puts in front of the client

This is the sheet the Entleiher signs (§ 17c Abs. 1 AÜG). Read it off this description rather
than from memory — an operator arguing with a client about a number is usually arguing about one
of these columns. A period that is not yet `approved` or `invoiced` prints an **"Entwurf"** badge.

**The order on the page**, top to bottom: title and the § 17c subtitle, the two-column meta block,
the daily table, the operator's **Anmerkung** where one was written, the **Freigaben** table where
it earns its place, then the signature block. That is the whole sheet.

> **There is no § 17c confirmation paragraph between the table and the signatures.** The box that
> used to sit there is gone: it restated the subtitle and then repeated employee, Entleiher and
> documented range — all of which the header and the meta block already print — to say what the
> signature block says with the signer, the date and the note under the line. So never send an
> operator to "dem Absatz über den Unterschriften"; the one statement that box alone carried
> (an operator release is no client confirmation) now prints **under the signature line**, see
> *Unterschriftenblock* below.

**When the stored PDF exists.** Generation no longer waits for `approved`: the PDF is written
after **any full client confirmation** — including a period that lands in `PendingEmployee`
because the agreed hours deviate from the tracked ones. Previously such a period had no PDF at
all until the employee reacted, sometimes days later. The badge rule above is unchanged, so that
early PDF prints "Entwurf" until the period reaches `approved`; reaching `approved` re-runs the
generation and replaces the file, so the stored document is always current. A **Teilfreigabe**
produces nothing — a partial day-selection leaves the period open, and no PDF exists until the
rest is released. The operator-side gate is also unchanged: "Tätigkeitsnachweis öffnen" in the
Stundennachweis list is still offered only on an `approved` or `invoiced` period.

**Freigaben.** Once generated, the document carries a **Freigaben** table listing every sign-off
the period collected — which days it covered (a contiguous run collapses to "from – until",
anything else lists each date), who signed, through which channel, when, plus that event's
signature image where it has one. That replaces the single week-level signature. Three things
follow:

- **A deemed approval appears as its own clearly marked row with no signer**, beside — never on
  top of — a human signature that came earlier. "Der Kunde hat unterschrieben, auf dem Nachweis
  steht Fristablauf" is no longer a thing that happens.
- **A release whose days were all revoked is dropped from the table**, since it no longer says
  anything true about the period. A partially revoked one still appears, covering its remaining
  days.
- **The closing signature block still shows one signer** — the most recent standing release — so
  the Freigaben table, not that block, is where a period signed by two people is read.

**Unterschriftenblock — ein Name ohne Handzeichen ist kein leeres Feld mehr.** The closing block
renders the most recent standing release. Where that release carries a **drawn** mark, the image is
printed. Where it carries a **name but no stored mark**, the name is typed in a cursive face where
the mark would be, and a one-line note underneath says what actually stands behind it:

| The last standing release | What prints under the signature line |
|---|---|
| Portal, with the login on record | "Freigegeben von \<Name\> am \<Datum\>, angemeldet als \<E-Mail\>" — unchanged |
| Portal, without a login on record | "Digital im Kundenportal freigegeben" |
| Fristablauf (deemed approval) | "Freigegeben per Fristablauf — keine Unterschrift". Nobody signed, so there is no name to type either — the note stands alone |
| Freigabe durch den Verleiher (`operator_release`) | "Eine Bestätigung des Entleihers liegt hierzu nicht vor." Deliberately **not** the neutral line below it: nothing is missing from an operator release, there was never a client signature to store |
| Any other channel with no stored mark | "Freigabe erfasst ohne gespeicherte Unterschrift" |

Previously any non-portal signature-less release printed a **blank box above a printed name**,
which reads as an unsigned document — the opposite of what happened. Two things follow for what you
tell an operator:

- **A cursive name is not a claimed signature**, and the line beneath it says so. "Warum steht da
  eine Unterschrift, die niemand gezeichnet hat" is answered by reading that note: it is a typed
  name plus its provenance, not a captured mark.
- **The neutral wording is deliberate wherever the channel is not evidenced.** Releases the
  backfill reconstructed carry `onsite_signature` as a migration default, not as an observation —
  so the document says "ohne gespeicherte Unterschrift" rather than asserting a channel. This sheet
  is evidence under § 11 AÜG; never "correct" that neutral line into a channel claim, and do not
  promise a client it proves *how* they signed.

**Kopf.** The meta block under the title carries five facts and no more: Mitarbeiter/in with the
Tätigkeit in parentheses behind the name, Entleiher, **Einsatzort**, **Abteilung** and the
**AÜV-Nummer** (the last three printed only when the Einsatz resolves one — the department comes
from the Einsatz and falls back to the Einsatzvertrag, and an Einsatzvertrag without a contract
identifier prints no AÜV row).

> **It runs in TWO columns, not as one stacked list**, filled left-to-right and split balanced, so a
> full sheet reads Mitarbeiter/in · Entleiher · Einsatzort on the left and Abteilung · AÜV on the
> right, and a four-fact sheet reads 2/2 rather than 3/1. An operator told to look "unter Entleiher"
> for the Abteilung or the AÜV-Nummer will not find them there — they are **to the right**. Which
> fact lands in which column moves with how many of them exist, so describe the block by what it
> contains, never by a fixed position on the page.

**Kalenderwoche is not one of them** — every row sits under a KW group header that names its own
week (see *Tabelle*), so "wo steht die KW" is answered by pointing at the table, not at the meta
list.

**Seitenkopf.** The running header on *every* page carries the document title **"Tätigkeitsnachweis"**
(bold), the employee's name and the documented range, plus the **"Entwurf"** marker while the period
is not yet approved. It names the sheet, so a loose printed page says what it is — on page two as
well as page one.

> **The AÜV-Nummer is NOT in the page header** — it is a standing fact of the Einsatz and sits with
> the others in the meta block (right-hand column on a full sheet). "Zu welchem Vertrag gehört
> dieses Blatt" is answered off the meta block, not off the running header. A loose page from the
> middle of a long Nachweis therefore names the document and the person but not the contract.

> **The documented range is in the page header ONLY.** It was once repeated in the meta block, which
> put it on a one-page sheet twice, four lines apart; it was removed from the meta list rather than
> printed in both places — so never send an operator looking for an "Einsatzzeitraum"-Zeile under
> Entleiher. It is not there, and that is not a rendering fault.

> **The range is not the Einsatz's term.** What the header prints is the period's **first and last
> actually worked day** — not the approval period's bounds and not the contract term. A period can
> begin days before the Einsatz does, and since the § 17c-Bestätigungskasten was removed the header
> is the only place the range appears at all, so it has to be the truth. Never tell an operator they
> can read the Vertragslaufzeit off this document; "Einsatzzeitraum (Gesamt)" was removed
> deliberately. It is derived from the days themselves, so the empty-rows setting below never moves
> it.

**Tabelle.** How many columns it has is a **per-company setting**, not a fixed layout — see *Wie
viel vom Plan der Kunde sieht* below. The default (`delta_only`) prints
Tag · Datum · Von · Bis · Pause · **Std.** · **Abw.** · Bemerkung: what was worked, plus the one
deviation figure. Under `full` the planned block is printed beside it and the header becomes two
rows — **Soll** spanning Zeit · Pause · Std., **Ist** spanning Von · Bis · Pause · Std. — **eleven**
columns in total, not twelve: Soll's Von and Bis share one cell.
Under `none` the Abw. column is dropped as well. So never assert "der Nachweis hat eine
Soll-Spalte" without reading that client's setting first.

- **Rows are grouped per Kalenderwoche.** Each group opens with a "KW 32" header and closes with
  its own "Summe KW 32" line carrying that week's Ist and Abw. — plus that week's Soll wherever the
  planned block is printed; the grand total
  ("Gesamtstunden des Zeitraums") stays at the bottom. A period is four to five weeks long under
  any month-aligned scheme (see *Abrechnungszeitraum* below), which is why the flat single-total
  list was replaced. Groups are keyed by ISO year **and** week, so a period crossing New Year does
  not merge KW 1 into KW 53.
- **Soll spans THREE columns, not four — its Von and Bis share one `06:00–14:12` cell.** The block
  is **Zeit · Pause · Std.**; only **Ist** keeps Von and Bis apart (Von · Bis · Pause · Std.). The
  asymmetry is deliberate, so never tell an operator to look for a "Soll-Von-Spalte" — there is
  none, and they will read the wrong cell in exactly the comparison `full` exists for. Say
  *"die Soll-Zeit-Spalte"* and they land on the right one. The planned times themselves are still
  printed: a lone planned hour figure beside a full Ist row hid *how* the day diverged ("7,70
  geplant / 06:00–14:12 gearbeitet" cannot tell a client whether the person started late or left
  early), and the difference is what they sign off. Collapsing the pair is what frees the width the
  Ist **Pause** column needs for the clocked break windows below. A planned start, end or break
  that does not exist prints "—".
- **The Ist Pause cell names *when* the break was taken, not only how long it lasted.** Under the
  duration ("30 Min.") it prints the clocked window as a second, smaller line — `09:51–10:21` —
  stacking one line per window where several were clocked. That is § 4 ArbZG evidence on the sheet
  the Entleiher signs, so "wann war die Pause?" is answered by the document itself; do not send an
  operator into the Zeiterfassung for it. It is printed under **all three** disclosure values, not
  only under `full`.
  - **A window exists only where the break was clocked as its own time entry** (a `break`-type
    entry with a real start and end). The **live stopwatch's pause path stores no window** — it
    accumulates paused seconds only — so a day paused with the timer, or one whose break was
    entered as a plain duration (manuelle Erfassung, Dienstplan übernommen), prints the duration
    alone. That is the normal case, not a gap in the document. Windows are also scoped to this
    Nachweis's own Einsatz: an employee placed at two clients in one week never has the other
    Entleiher's breaks on this sheet.
  - **The windows are shown only when they add up to the duration printed above them.** The agreed
    break can be corrected in the client-portal approval flow, and a signed § 17c document must not
    pair a stated "45 Min." with windows spanning 30. Where the two disagree the times are withheld
    and the cell prints an italic *"korrigiert"* instead (EN: *"corrected"*). Read that word as
    **"breaks were clocked, but the stated duration is no longer the one they add up to"** — it is
    *not* "no break was recorded", and it is not a fault to fix. The same applies when the agreed
    break was corrected down to zero: the cell then reads "—" with *korrigiert* under it.
- **Soll is the frozen plan, not today's Dienstplan.** It is read off the timesheet day's own
  `soll_*` values, which were stamped when the period was built. Editing or cancelling the Schicht
  afterwards does not move the printed Soll — that is on purpose, since the Abweichung was measured
  against exactly that plan (`→ build-dienstplan`).
- **Abw. is the day's stored `deviation_minutes`, never recomputed.** The document and the app
  therefore cannot disagree about a day. It is **suppressed on an `extra` day** — work with no
  planned Schicht has nothing to deviate from, and printing the whole worked duration as a delta
  would invent a plan that never existed. So an empty Abw. next to real hours is correct, not a
  missing number.
- **A planned-but-not-worked day is marked as a gap**, highlighted rather than left to read as a
  blank line, and carries the Bemerkung **"Nicht gearbeitet"** when the day is flagged `missing`.
- **Bemerkung prints the automatic reason first, the operator's own note after it**, separated by
  " · ". The manual note is appended, never replaced — "Krank · AU liegt vor ab 03.08." is the
  expected shape, not a duplication.
- **A deviating day prints its explanation as `Abweichung: <Kategorie> – <Satz>`** — the
  classification the employee picked at Feierabend, an en dash, then their own sentence (see
  *Tagesabschluss* below). The two are one line but never merged with the operator's Bemerkung,
  which keeps its own line underneath, so the client can tell which of the two the system
  demanded. A day closed before the category shipped has only the sentence and prints just that —
  a missing "Kategorie –" on an old sheet is not a defect. The same
  `Kategorie – Satz` shape appears under "Grund der Abweichung" in the Kundenportal (both the
  plan detail and the sign-off dialog) and in the employee's own week, so all three read
  identically.

> **On a default tenant the absence day is not printed at all.** Everything in this box describes
> the row *when it is printed* — and `taetigkeitsnachweis_show_absence_days` is **off by default**,
> so a day the employee was absent on and tracked nothing is dropped from the sheet before any of
> it applies (see *Abwesenheitstage* below). Read that setting for the client in question before
> telling an operator what their Nachweis says about a Kranktag.
>
> **A declared Krankmeldung prints exactly one word: "Krank".** All three incapacity categories —
> `SICK_LEAVE`, `SICK_LEAVE_WITHOUT_PAY` and `CHILD_CARE` — collapse to that single label. This is
> deliberate and must not be "corrected" to the actual category: the sheet documents **attendance,
> not the wage type**, and "Kind krank" tells the client something about the employee's family it
> has no business knowing. Two consequences worth stating to an operator:
> - **A *submitted* Krankmeldung already counts — approval is not the gate here.** Every absence
>   the employee actually declared prints the label: `submitted`, `pending`, `needs_revision` and
>   `approved` all count. Only a never-submitted `draft`, a `rejected` and a `cancelled` absence
>   print nothing — those are the explicit statement that the day was NOT covered. Approval still
>   decides the **pay**; it no longer decides what this document says about attendance
>   (`→ record-absence`). When a submitted and an approved absence cover the same day, the
>   approved one is the one the document names.
> - The label is derived **when the PDF is generated**, from the covering absences overlapping the
>   period. It is not stored on the day, so nothing about it can be edited in the timesheet.

### Wie viel vom Plan der Kunde sieht — `document_planned_disclosure`

How much of the planned shift the sheet discloses beside the hours worked is one
`time_tracking_hours_approval` setting with three values. The tenant default is `delta_only`,
so on an untouched tenant the document shows **no** planned times:

| Value | What the table prints | Columns |
|---|---|---|
| `none` | worked hours only — no planned times, no deviation | 7 |
| `delta_only` | the worked block plus the single **Abw.** figure — **the default** | 8 |
| `full` | the **Soll** block (Zeit · Pause · Std.) beside **Ist** (Von · Bis · Pause · Std.), plus Abw. | 11 |

```
manage-settings(group: "time_tracking_hours_approval", action: "get")
manage-settings(group: "time_tracking_hours_approval", action: "update",
                fields: { "document_planned_disclosure": "full" })
```

`time_tracking_hours_approval` is the canonical group key — the Stundenfreigabe settings are
now the *hours-approval* feature of the Zeiterfassung app, and `manage-settings` derives the
key from that. The old group key `timesheet` is still accepted for the moment but is slated
for removal; do not use it in new calls. The per-company override keys on
`manage-record-settings` (`timesheet.document_planned_disclosure` etc.) are a different
surface and keep their `timesheet.` prefix.

In the web app it is **Einstellungen → Apps → Zeiterfassung → "Soll-Ist-Darstellung im
Tätigkeitsnachweis"**, a dropdown directly under the empty-days switch (the former
*Einstellungen → Stundenfreigabe* page redirects there). An operator usually
quotes the option label rather than the enum value, so map it before you write:

| Operator says | Value |
|---|---|
| "Nur Ist-Stunden (kein Soll, keine Abweichung)" | `none` |
| "Ist-Stunden + Abweichung (ohne geplante Zeiten)" | `delta_only` |
| "Vollständiger Soll-Ist-Vergleich (Soll-Block + Abweichung)" | `full` |

Picking `none` in that dropdown raises an inline warning on the screen itself — the same portal
caveat as below, so an operator quoting it is reading the app correctly, not hitting a fault.

- **Overridable per client company**, like `taetigkeitsnachweis_show_empty_days` and
  `period_scheme` — `manage-record-settings`, `model_type: "0-3"`, key
  `timesheet.document_planned_disclosure` (`describe` first, `clear` reverts to the tenant
  default). So "nur dieser Kunde soll die geplanten Zeiten sehen" is a real answer, not a
  tenant-wide trade-off.
- **Presentation only.** Totals, the derived break and everything billed are computed from the days
  and do not move with it — the same plain no to "ändert das die Rechnung?" as the empty-rows flag.
- **`full` is the layout for a client disputing *how* an hour went missing**, not *that* it did. It
  is the most transparent and the least readable; eleven columns on A4 is the price, and it is why
  it is not the default.

> **Say this out loud before setting a company to `none`.** The client portal's approval screen
> always shows the full planned-vs-worked comparison, and the signature is given on that screen.
> Under `none` the document then omits the very deviation the client approved — the sheet says less
> than the screen that was signed. `delta_only` and `full` both keep it. Treat `none` as a
> deliberate choice the operator confirms, never as a tidy-up.

### "Der Tätigkeitsnachweis ist voller leerer Zeilen" — a layout setting, not a billing one

The PDF lists every calendar day of the period by default, including the ones with no hours.
Since a period is normally a month segment (see *Abrechnungszeitraum* below), a weekend-only
Einsatz prints five dash-filled rows for every two real ones. Switch the empty rows off and the
document gets shorter:

```
manage-settings(group: "time_tracking_hours_approval", action: "get")
manage-settings(group: "time_tracking_hours_approval", action: "update",
                fields: { "taetigkeitsnachweis_show_empty_days": false })
```

`taetigkeitsnachweis_show_empty_days` (bool, default `true`) is the tenant default; in the web
app it is **Einstellungen → Apps → Zeiterfassung → "Leere Tage im Tätigkeitsnachweis
anzeigen"**.

- **It is overridable per client company**, like the Fristen and `period_scheme` above — so "nur
  für diesen Kunden kürzer" is a real request, not a tenant-wide compromise. Set it on the
  company with `manage-record-settings`, `model_type: "0-3"`, key
  `timesheet.taetigkeitsnachweis_show_empty_days` (`describe` first, `clear` reverts to the
  tenant default).
- **A day carrying a Bemerkung is never dropped**, even with zero hours — the note is the only
  thing that row says. That now includes the automatic ones, so a day with a declared
  Krankmeldung ("Krank" — submitted counts, not just approved) survives *this* filter too. So "der Tag mit der Notiz fehlt jetzt" is not
  this setting; look for a missing time entry instead — or at the absence filter below, which is
  the one that does drop a Kranktag, and by default.
- **A gap row is never dropped either** — a day that was planned and not worked has zero hours but
  is the opposite of an uneventful day, and hiding it would remove the one thing the client should
  see. Only genuinely empty, unremarked days go.
- **Nor is a day carrying an Abweichungsgrund** — the explanation the employee was compelled to
  write at Feierabend is exactly what that row exists to say (see *Tagesabschluss* below).
- **Presentation only.** Totals, the derived break and everything billed are computed from the
  days themselves and do not move with this flag. An operator asking "ändert das die Rechnung?"
  gets a plain no — the only thing that changes is which rows the PDF prints.

### "Wo ist der Kranktag hin?" — `taetigkeitsnachweis_show_absence_days`, **off by default**

Whether days the employee was **absent** on (krank, Urlaub — every category, not just the three
incapacity ones) appear on the client's sheet at all. **Default `false`** — the opposite of its
empty-days sibling above. Do not carry the sibling's default over to it: an operator told "das
steht standardmäßig drauf" is being told the wrong thing.

```
manage-settings(group: "time_tracking_hours_approval", action: "get")
manage-settings(group: "time_tracking_hours_approval", action: "update",
                fields: { "taetigkeitsnachweis_show_absence_days": true })
```

In the web app: **Einstellungen → Apps → Zeiterfassung → "Abwesenheitstage im Tätigkeitsnachweis
anzeigen"**, in the same document block as the empty-days switch and the Soll-Ist dropdown.

- **Why off is the default, and why that is not a display bug.** The Entleiher is asked to confirm
  the hours worked **at their site** (§ 17c Abs. 1 AÜG); an absence day is not one of those, and
  "Krank" on a document that goes to the client discloses a health fact they have no business
  knowing — the same reasoning that already collapses "Kind krank" into the neutral label. Say
  this before switching it on for a client: turning it on puts the employee's absences in front of
  the Entleiher. Do it where a client contractually expects a gap-free calendar, not as a tidy-up.
- **It runs after — and independently of — the empty-days filter.** An absence row survives that
  one (it carries the "Krank" note, and where a Schicht was planned it counts as a gap), so this
  is the filter that actually removes it. An operator reporting "der Kranktag fehlt auf dem
  Nachweis" on an untouched tenant is reading correct behaviour; check *this* key before
  investigating anything else.
- **It can never hide worked hours.** Only a row with zero net minutes is dropped, so a part-day
  absence that still carries tracked time stays — those minutes are exactly what the client signs.
  A dropped row therefore carried nothing, and no total can move with this flag: **presentation
  only**, like both settings above.
- **Overridable per client company**, like the empty-days flag, `document_planned_disclosure` and
  `period_scheme` — `manage-record-settings`, `model_type: "0-3"`, key
  `timesheet.taetigkeitsnachweis_show_absence_days` (`describe` first, `clear` reverts to the
  tenant default). So "nur dieser eine Kunde will einen lückenlosen Kalender" is a real answer
  without exposing every other client's sheets.

### "Wann soll mein Mitarbeiter die Unterschrift holen?" — the four day-mode nudge settings

Only for a company running **day mode**: `timesheet.approval_granularity` = `day` **and** the
`DayLevelHoursApproval` flag active for that company. Outside day mode all four are inert — do not
offer them as a lever on a normal period-mode client.

In day mode the employee is given no deadline at all. Instead, on a day they are on site, the app
tells them to collect the client's signature **today**, with the reason — the assignment ends
today, the next shift falls in the next Abrechnungszeitraum, there is no shift here for the next
few days, or enough open days have piled up on this assignment. The last two are what these
settings tune:

| Key (prefix `timesheet.`) | Default | What it moves |
|---|---|---|
| `signature_gap_trigger_days` | 2 | Days of gap to the employee's next shift at this Einsatzbetrieb before they are told to collect today |
| `signature_accumulation_trigger_days` | 5 | Open days on **one** assignment before they are nudged regardless of the gap |
| `signature_client_ask_cadence_days` | 3 | Minimum days between two asks at the same (employee, company) — the client-fatigue cap |
| `signature_prewarn_days` | 3 | How far ahead the employee is warned that a signature day is coming |

- **Two routes, same four keys.** Tenant-wide over `manage-settings`, group
  `time_tracking_hours_approval`; per company over `manage-record-settings`, `model_type: "0-3"`,
  prefix `timesheet.`. `describe` first on either; `clear` on the company reverts it to the tenant
  default. The Zeiterfassung settings page is generated from the same tenant-wide field list, so
  what the operator can set on that screen is exactly what the tool can set.
- **Per company is the answer to almost every real question.** "Klinik X wird zu oft gefragt" and
  "meine Leute im Tagesmodus lassen zu spät unterschreiben" are both one client, not the tenant.
  Only change the tenant default when day mode is the house standard.
- **The cadence cap is deliberately breakable.** "Assignment ends today" and "last shift before the
  period is cut" ignore `signature_client_ask_cadence_days` — raising it does not silence those,
  and it should not, because they are the expensive ones.
- **The prewarn is not the point; what it enables is.** An unclosed or unsettled day cannot be
  fixed on the day the client representative is standing there — the employee reads "4 Tage müssen
  zuerst geschlossen werden" at Feierabend and goes home without a signature. Shortening
  `signature_prewarn_days` removes their room to clear those days first.

## Releasing and un-releasing the whole period — two operator-only actions on `0-426`

The two period-level writes. Both go through `manage-record-action` on `0-426`, both are
two-stage, and both are weighty enough that the operator should hear exactly what will happen
before you send `confirmed: true`.

### Releasing without the client — `approve_by_operator`

For the client who will not confirm: the hours were agreed by phone, nobody signs, and the period
would otherwise sit until the Frist deems it approved — or, where the company has
`deadlines_enabled` off, until nothing resolves it at all. This is the Verleiher releasing the
period itself. Before this existed the only operator-driven route to Approved was mediation, and
mediation needs a *dispute* — a client who simply never answers produced none.

```
manage-record-action(model_type: "0-426", record_id: <period id>,
                     operation: "execute", action: "approve_by_operator",
                     data: { client_note: "Stunden am 02.06. mit Fr. Schulz telefonisch abgestimmt",
                             internal_note: "PDL seit drei Wochen nicht erreichbar" },
                     confirmed: false)   # preview first, then confirmed: true
```

- **Only while the period is `open`** — whether or not it has already been submitted to the
  client. `Approved` / `Invoiced` are already released, `PendingEmployee` is waiting on the
  employee, and a period in `Mediation` is resolved through the Stundenklärung queue instead.
- **Every day must be releasable first** — closed by the employee, its closure still valid, no
  unexplained gap, nothing still running. Already-released, absence-covered and genuinely empty
  days are exempt. This is the same day gate the client's own release passes, deliberately: an
  operator signing in the client's stead clears at least the bar the client would have had to.
- **A refusal names the blocking days**, as `2026-05-27 (not_closed)` — the same reason vocabulary
  as the portal (`not_closed`, `unsettled`, `running`, `missing_time`, …). Quote those dates to the
  operator rather than a generic failure: they say which day to chase, and the fix is the
  Tagesabschluss (gate below), after which the action succeeds.
- **Two notes, two audiences, both optional.** `client_note` is printed on the Tätigkeitsnachweis
  the client receives; `internal_note` never leaves the operator surfaces and is never on the
  document. If it is at all unclear which one the operator meant, read it back before confirming —
  the internal reasoning ("erreichen die PDL seit drei Wochen nicht") is one field away from being
  printed for the customer.
- **It does not always land on `Approved`.** Where the agreed hours differ from what the employee
  tracked, the period goes to **`PendingEmployee`** for their counter-confirmation instead: an
  operator may release the *client's* side, but nobody may bind the employee to hours they did not
  record. **Read the resulting status back before reporting "freigegeben"** — the deviating
  periods are exactly the ones where saying it wrongly matters most.
- **Needs the `timesheets.approve_by_operator` permission** — its own, separate from
  `timesheets.view`, and like the rest of the `timesheets.*` family granted to no role by default.
  Releasing hours for billing without the customer having agreed to them is a capability a role may
  well be denied while still running the Stundenfreigabe day to day.
- **The document must never claim a client signature.** The release carries the channel
  `operator_release`, which is explicitly *not* a client confirmation. On the Tätigkeitsnachweis the
  signature line reads "Datum, Freigabe durch den Verleiher" over the tenant name and the acting
  user, the Freigaben table names the channel "Freigabe durch den Verleiher", and **directly under
  that signature line** stands the sentence *"Eine Bestätigung des Entleihers liegt hierzu nicht
  vor."* Point an operator at the signature block, **not** at a paragraph above it — there is no
  box there any more (see *what the Tätigkeitsnachweis actually puts in front of the client*). That
  document is § 11 AÜG evidence — never describe an operator release, to a client or to an operator,
  as "der Kunde hat freigegeben".

### Taking a whole approval back — `revoke_approval`

The undo for a period approved in error, above all one signed by the **wrong person** — the case it
was built for is an employee signing the client's line on her own device, leaving the period
approved, locked and billable on a signature the client never gave.

```
manage-record-action(model_type: "0-426", record_id: <period id>,
                     operation: "execute", action: "revoke_approval",
                     data: { reason: "Von der Mitarbeiterin selbst unterschrieben statt vom Kunden" },
                     confirmed: false)   # preview first, then confirmed: true
```

- **`reason` is mandatory** and is written onto every revoked release row. A blank one is refused.
- **Only while the period is `Approved`.** An `Invoiced` period has left the approval process and
  is money now — reverse the invoice with a credit note first; the action says so rather than
  failing blankly. A period that is not yet approved has no approval to take back: there the
  day-level `revoke_release` (`0-427`) is the right tool.
- **Needs the `timesheets.revoke_approval` permission**, again its own and granted to no role by
  default.
- **What it moves:** `Approved → open`, along the state machine's one sanctioned backward edge.
  Every standing day release is stamped revoked with the reason, the week's completeness marker,
  signer, drawn signature, channel, login email, employee counter-confirmation and deemed-approval
  stamp are cleared, and the days become editable again. **`submitted_to_client_at` is deliberately
  left standing** — "was already sent to the client" is carried by that stamp, not by a status, and
  the week genuinely was submitted before it was wrongly signed. So the period reads `open` *and*
  submitted, not `open` and untouched.
- **No signature is erased.** The release rows stay and are stamped revoked, with who took them
  back, when and why. The signature *happened*; erasing it would destroy the evidence of exactly the
  thing being corrected. The process history (`mediated_at`, `disputed_at`, `escalated_at`) stays
  too — a revocation does not un-happen it.
- **The stored Tätigkeitsnachweis is dropped**, because it certifies the revoked signature. It is
  regenerated when the period is signed again. Say this before confirming: anyone holding that
  document or a link to it loses it until then.
- **A fresh client deadline is issued** and the sent-reminder stamps are cleared. This is not
  cosmetic: a revoked week would otherwise land back in `open` carrying a deadline that
  elapsed long ago, and — since it still counts as submitted — the next deadline run would deem it
  approved again, with no signature at all, within the hour.
- **The route back to `Approved` is the normal one**: the correct signer signs in the portal or on
  site, the new Frist lapses, or — where the client will not sign at all — `approve_by_operator`
  above.

State the period, who originally signed it and the reason to the operator before confirming.
Both of these actions reverse or stand in for something a named person put their name to.

## How the client is notified (don't call it "an email")

When a period reaches the client approver — ready to confirm, the deadline reminder, and the
auto-approved notice — delivery depends on whether that Ansprechpartner has a **client-portal
account**:

- **With an active portal account** — the notice lands in the portal's notification centre
  (bell) and as web push, *in addition to* the mail, and follows that person's own channel
  preferences.
- **Without one** — mail only. This is still the common case, so never assume a client saw a
  bell.

Either way an outbound Email activity is logged on the contact's timeline, so
`get-timeline` on the Contact (`0-105`) is how you check what actually went out before
telling an operator the client "was never informed".

### The on-device signer gets a receipt mail — one per signature, and only with an email

After a client representative signs the Stundenfreigabe **on the employee's device**, the signer
receives a receipt mail (`timesheet.signature_receipt`): **one mail per signing gesture**, naming
every period that signature released — not one mail per period. Like the other Stundenfreigabe
mails it is transactional (the mail cannot be switched off; only push and in-app can), it is
localized to the contact, and it logs an outbound Email activity on the contact's timeline — so
`get-timeline` on the Contact (`0-105`) is again how you verify what went out.

Three qualifications before you promise anyone a copy:

- **Only a signer with an email address gets it.** The on-device flow deliberately accepts a new
  signer with nothing but a name — a ward manager signing on the employee's phone often has no
  more — and an existing company contact may have no email either. In both cases the send is
  simply skipped: that is the ordinary case, not a fault, and the Tätigkeitsnachweis PDFs are
  generated regardless. So never tell an operator "the signer automatically gets a copy" — read
  the contact's `email` first, and name the missing address as the reason when there is none.
  **The employee can supply the address in the app, but only before signing.** Next to a signer
  they typed in by hand the app now offers **"Angaben bearbeiten"** (EN "Edit details"), which
  reopens the entry form with what was typed still in it — the address can be added without
  retyping the name and phone. ("Ändern" / "Change" sits beside it and does something else: it
  drops the selection and returns to the search field.) So "warum hat XY keine Bestätigung
  bekommen" has a new, workable answer for next time: have the employee add the e-mail in the
  app before the client signs. Two limits — the button appears **only for a hand-typed signer**,
  never for an existing company contact (that is master data; fix its `email` in alluvo with
  `manage-model` `update` on `0-105`), and it changes nothing retroactively: once the signature
  is submitted the contact is created with whatever was entered, and adding the address later
  does not re-send the receipt for that signature.
- **A Teilfreigabe sends nothing.** Only fully released periods enter the receipt; a partial
  day-selection leaves the period open and produces neither a PDF nor a mail until the rest is
  released (see *When the stored PDF exists* above).
- **The mail is meant to carry the Tätigkeitsnachweis PDF of every released period — but
  currently arrives without the attachments.** A known defect on the alluvo side (the mail reads
  the PDF from the wrong storage location) strips them; the mail itself, with the period list,
  still arrives, and the PDFs are stored correctly. Until the fix lands, a signer who needs the
  document gets it from the operator — "Tätigkeitsnachweis öffnen" on the approved period — not
  from their inbox.

### The signer picker — a personal shortlist first, then a company-wide name search

Before signing, the employee picks the confirming person in a picker with **two zones**, and the
two answer different questions. It is the same picker in week mode and in day mode. Know the
split when an operator asks how to make the right person easy to find, why an employee's list is
empty, or where a duplicate contact came from.

- **"Meine Ansprechpartner" is shown before anything is typed — and it is that employee's own
  list.** It holds up to **five** contacts at that client whom *this* employee has already had
  sign (shown with the date of that last signature) or whom they created in the wizard
  themselves, most-recently-signed first. It was previously called "Zuletzt bestätigt von" and
  was **company-wide**, so it proposed — and date-stamped — people who had only ever signed for
  a colleague. Two consequences worth saying out loud to an operator: **nothing an operator sets
  in alluvo puts a contact into this list** (not the `timesheet_approver` label, not a portal
  role — only the employee's own signing history or their own hand-created contact does), and
  the dates now come from the **standing Freigaben** (the rows behind the Freigaben-Reiter), not
  from a week-level signer column. That column stopped being filled reliably when Teilfreigabe
  shipped, so the old shortlist could come back empty for an employee who had had the same
  person sign many times — that is fixed, and an employee reporting it can simply be told so.
- **There is no fallback: an empty shortlist is a normal state, not a broken link.** An employee
  who has had nobody sign at this client yet, and has created nobody there, sees no suggestion
  group at all — only the hint to type a name. Deliberate. Do not go hunting for a missing
  company-contact association when an employee says "die Liste ist leer"; the answer is that
  they have not signed with anyone here yet.
- **The name search stays company-wide.** Every contact of the company is reachable by typing,
  on purpose — narrowing the search to the employee's own people would have every new temp on
  the ward mint a duplicate Stationsleitung. So the fix for "the employee cannot find X" is
  always *type the name*, never *add the contact again*.
- **The badge is a designation, it has one exact spelling, and it acts on the search results.**
  A contact whose (company, contact) membership carries the pivot label **`timesheet_approver`**
  sorts to the top of the **typed search results** and is marked there with an
  **"Ansprechpartner"** badge (EN "Contact person"). It has no effect on the pre-typing
  shortlist above. The badge is keyed on that literal string, so "mark the official
  Ansprechpartner and the employee will find them" only works if the label is written exactly
  that way — and only once the employee starts typing. `manage-association` writes `label`
  verbatim, so the friendly `label: "Ansprechpartner"` shown in the association example below
  puts the person in the client's Kontakte list but does **not** produce the badge or the sort
  priority — pass `label: "timesheet_approver"` when that is what an operator is asking for.
- **alluvo writes that label itself, and it overwrites.** Whoever signs on the employee's device
  is tagged `timesheet_approver` on that company afterwards — new contact or existing one alike —
  and the write replaces any label already on the membership. So a label an operator set by hand
  can be silently replaced by the last on-site signer; read the current one rather than assuming
  yours survived. The same label is also the **fallback tier** for who receives approval
  correspondence, used only when no contact holds an approver role at the week's Einsatzbetrieb.
  It authorises nothing on its own — see *Portal access is opt-in* below.
- **The picker's search field no longer autocorrects.** iOS used to rewrite a surname as the
  employee typed, the search then found nobody, and the employee added a contact that already
  existed — a real source of duplicate contacts on client companies. Fixed going forward, but it
  does not clean up the duplicates already created: those are an ordinary contact merge
  (`→ merge-duplicate-companies`, which covers Contact `0-105` as well as Company `0-3`).

### Portal access is opt-in — nobody gets it by being a contact

There are **two independent stages**, and they are routinely confused — keep them apart in
anything you tell an operator or a client:

1. **Linked to the company** — the (company, contact) membership row. It is the CRM link *and*
   the entry in the client portal's **Kontakte** list in one: every linked contact is shown
   there. There is no separate "listing" designation on top of it (the old
   `is_contact_person` / Ansprechpartner-Listing concept was abolished).
2. **Login-capable** — an **active** membership carrying **at least one role-bearing grant**.

Being linked to the company makes someone a CRM contact and puts them in the client's Kontakte
list — **not** a portal user. Logging in requires **both** halves of that person's portal
membership at the company: the membership must be **active**, *and* it must carry at least one
grant that actually names a role. Either half missing means no login. A freshly created or
freshly linked Ansprechpartner has neither, so they cannot sign in and nothing is sent to them
automatically. Inviting is always its own explicit step. Appearing in the Kontakte list
therefore says nothing about access, in either direction. (Contacts who had actually signed in kept the role they held; the bulk `viewer` grants
handed out by the rollout backfill to contacts who never had a login were revoked, so a
long-standing Ansprechpartner may well have no access today.)

> **One exception, and it is not an invitation:** attaching a contact as a **contract
> recipient** provisions the membership plus a **`viewer`** grant they need to open the contract
> link — unless they already hold a role-bearing grant, or their membership was deliberately
> disabled. It grants authentication only, sends no invitation mail, and never touches an
> existing role. It is *not* enough for Stundenfreigabe: confirming hours needs **`approver`**,
> which is still an explicit assignment. `→ manage-contract-lifecycle`.
>
> **That `viewer` lands on the person's existing position, not company-wide.** If they were
> already placed at an Einsatzbetrieb (as an Einsatz-Ansprechpartner), the Viewer is written
> *there* and the placement row is dropped — one node, now role-bearing, at the same spot. Only
> a contact with no grant at all gets a company-wide Viewer. So a recipient's reach can be
> narrower than "the whole company", and it never gives them a second Organigramm node.

> **Standing in the Organigramm is not access.** A membership can hold a *placement* — a grant
> attaching the person to an Einsatzbetrieb (and Abteilung) with **no** role — which shows them
> in the Organigramm but grants **no** portal login at all. So "has a row at the company" never
> answers "can they confirm the hours"; only a role-bearing grant on an active membership does.
> Read the membership (below) instead of inferring access from the org chart. The client's
> **Kontakte** list is no help here either: it shows every linked contact, placement or not.

> **Placements can also be written directly.** `send-portal-invitation` / `assign-portal-roles`
> with an Einsatzbetrieb and an **empty `roles` list** places the person there without any portal
> access — the deliberate way to answer "der soll im Organigramm stehen, aber sich nicht
> anmelden". See *The position + the roles that hold there* below.

> **Where placements come from: naming an Ansprechpartner vor Ort on an Einsatz.** Setting an
> Einsatz's `contact_id` places that contact automatically, at the Einsatz's Einsatzbetrieb and
> Abteilung — following the §11 correction chain (`client_site_correction_id` / department
> correction first, then the contract), the same waterfall as the Einsatzmitteilung. It gives
> them **no role**, so:
>
> - **"Ansprechpartner eingetragen" never implies "kann sich anmelden."** Anyone who needs to
>   confirm hours still needs an explicit **`approver`** assignment — placing them is not a step
>   toward that. `→ manage-contract-lifecycle` sets `contact_id`; the actions below grant roles.
> - **Nothing is mailed.** Placing sends no invitation; only `send-portal-invitation` does.
> - **Swapping the contact leaves the old placement standing.** The new person is placed, the
>   previous node stays. So an Organigramm node is not evidence that someone is the *current*
>   Ansprechpartner of an Einsatz — read the Einsatz for that.
> - **Someone who already holds a role is not placed at all.** The role *is* their position, so
>   naming a role-holder as Ansprechpartner vor Ort adds nothing to the Organigramm — they keep
>   the single node they already had, wherever it sits. Do not read "kein zweiter Knoten" as a
>   failed write.
> - **Placing never moves anybody.** A contact already placed at another Einsatzbetrieb (or
>   company-wide) stays where they are; the new Einsatz's site is ignored for them. Only when the
>   existing placement is at the *same* Einsatzbetrieb do the Abteilungen change — and they are
>   **added**, not replaced, so a person named on Einsätze at Station A and Station B ends up at
>   both.
>
> This is also why a long-standing Einsatz-Ansprechpartner may have **lost** access: the `viewer`
> grants the rollout backfill had handed this group were revoked, except where the person was a
> contract recipient or actually had an account.

Two actions on the Contact (`0-105`), both via `manage-record-action` `operation: "execute"`:

- **`send-portal-invitation`** — the entry point for someone who has **never** signed in. Takes
  the contact's **position** at that company (`position_client_site_id`, optional
  `position_department_ids`) plus the `roles` that hold there — applied *before* the mail goes
  out, see the shape below — and `company_id`, required as soon as the contact hangs off more
  than one portal company. So this single call grants the first role, **switches the membership's
  access on when it was off**, stamps the invitation on the membership, and sends the mail. It
  only fails when the contact has no email address, or belongs to no portal-enabled company.
  Re-running it re-sends the invitation.
- **`assign-portal-roles`** — adjusts that position and its `roles` afterwards, plus a `portal_access_active` flag that
  is the membership's **access switch**: `false` sets it to `disabled`, which blocks sign-in
  while leaving **every role intact**, and `true` restores access with that reach unchanged. It
  is only offered for a contact who **already has a portal account** (`has_portal_login`) at a
  portal-enabled company; before that it does not appear at all — use `send-portal-invitation`.
  Note that "has an account" is a weaker condition than "has signed in" (see the `0-430` table
  below), so the action can appear for someone who has never opened the portal.

> **Inviting *is* granting access — including to someone who was deliberately locked out.** The
> invitation activates a `disabled` membership before the mail goes out, because a disabled
> membership cannot establish a session at all: the OTP endpoint issues **no** code (deliberately,
> so it leaks nothing about which addresses have accounts) while still showing the recipient the
> "Code gesendet" screen. Inviting a disabled membership therefore used to be silently worthless —
> the mail arrived, the code never did. Two consequences:
>
> - **It repairs the classic "eingeladen, aber es kommt kein Code" case.** Re-running
>   `send-portal-invitation` re-enables the membership *and* sends a usable invitation, in one
>   call. Read `status` on `0-430` first so you can tell the operator what it will change.
> - **Never reach for it as a shortcut to re-enable someone.** A `disabled` membership is a
>   deliberate decision — lockout, offboarding in progress, disputed account — and inviting
>   reverses it. If `status` is `disabled`, ask before sending. The flip is written to the
>   timeline as `client_portal_access_toggled`, and only when the switch was actually off: a
>   normal re-send to an active membership writes no toggle entry.

> **Picking a role in the portal's own Ansprechpartner form is not a reliable invitation path —
> use the action.** The client administrator's contact form inside the portal has a role field,
> and on **create** a submitted role does trigger the invitation. On **edit** it deliberately
> does not whenever the contact already held a role before that save, or was invited before —
> they are in the portal already, there is nothing to invite them to. Worse, the edit path
> currently sends **no** invitation at all, even for a never-invited contact: the send is
> refused silently, with no error in the form and nothing on the timeline. That leaves a contact
> holding `approver` who never received a link and cannot confirm a single Zeitraum. So never tell a
> client "set the role and the mail goes out" for an existing Ansprechpartner — run
> `send-portal-invitation` instead, which grants the role and sends the mail in one step no
> matter what the contact held before. (The silent refusal is a known defect on the alluvo side
> and is being fixed; the action is the correct path either way.)
>
> **Contacts created through that form before 2026-08-11 were born with access switched off.**
> Its "Portal-Zugang aktiv" toggle carried no default, so the create sheet submitted `false` and
> the membership was disabled a second after being created — leaving a contact who holds a role,
> was mailed an invitation, and still cannot sign in. The toggle defaults to **on** now, so the
> create path can no longer produce that state; older ones are repaired by re-running
> `send-portal-invitation`, which activates the membership as part of sending.

Roles are `administrator`, `approver`, `booker`, `commercial`, `viewer` and `site_admin` — six,
not five. For Stundenfreigabe the relevant one is **`approver`** — that is the person who
confirms the hours in the portal.

#### The position + the roles that hold there — three parameters

Both actions ask for the **position once** and then the roles that apply there. Three
parameters, not a repeater:

- **`position_client_site_id`** — **null or omitted** means **company-wide**, every
  Einsatzbetrieb; an **Einsatzbetrieb id** means the person sits at that one site only. The
  ClientSite must belong to the same company, or the call is refused. For Stundenfreigabe that
  is the whole point: a site-scoped `approver` reaches the Wochennachweise of that
  Einsatzbetrieb and no other, so "Haus 1 soll Haus 2 nicht sehen" is a position, not a
  workaround.
- **`position_department_ids`** — Stationen (**CompanyDepartments**) *of that same
  Einsatzbetrieb*, narrowing the position further. **Empty or omitted means the whole
  Einsatzbetrieb**, and none are accepted for a company-wide position — a department that hangs
  off another site is refused (`department_wrong_site`).
- **`roles`** — a plain list of role **values** (`["approver"]`, `["approver", "booker"]`).
  Every one of them applies at that one position; there is no per-role scope any more.

Two scope rules the tool enforces — check them *before* you write, they are refusals, not warnings:

- **`administrator` must be company-wide.** Passing a `position_client_site_id` with it is
  rejected; the role *is* the company-wide scope.
- **`site_admin` must be site-scoped** — it *requires* a `position_client_site_id`, and it must
  carry **no** `position_department_ids`. It is the per-Einsatzbetrieb counterpart of
  `administrator`: the same authority at that one site, including inviting colleagues and
  assigning roles there, but **without** billing management — payment terms and billing
  addresses stay company-wide Administrator/Commercial territory. So "der Leiter von Haus 2 soll
  dort alles dürfen, aber nicht an die Rechnungsdaten" is `site_admin` at that site, not
  `administrator`. Combining it with Stationen is refused on the `position_department_ids` field
  itself: *"Der Rolle „Einsatzbetrieb-Admin" können keine einzelnen Abteilungen zugeordnet
  werden…"*. Read that as "drop the Stationen, this role covers the whole Betrieb" — it is a
  scope rule, not a transient failure, and retrying unchanged will fail again.

Duplicates in `roles` are collapsed. A role value that is not in the catalog is **silently
dropped** rather than rejected — and since the write is a full replace, a single typo'd value
can leave the list empty and wipe every grant. Read the resulting position back instead of
assuming the write landed.

> **The old `assignments` repeater still parses, but do not send it.** A payload carrying
> `assignments` (rows of `{ role, client_site_id, department_ids }`) and **none** of the three
> keys above is still understood: alluvo takes the position from the first row and the roles
> from all of them. That fallback exists so an outdated caller does not silently wipe a
> contact's grants — it is not the contract. It cannot express a placement (`roles: []`), and
> its per-row `client_site_id` is a fiction now that every row has to agree anyway. Use the
> position parameters.

> **A position with no roles is a placement, and that is a real, useful write.** Sending an
> Einsatzbetrieb with **`roles: []`** puts the person in the Organigramm at that site (Stationen
> included) with **no portal access at all** — the same kind of node that naming an
> Ansprechpartner vor Ort creates. Use it when an operator wants somebody visible in the client's
> org chart without a login. **Company-wide with `roles: []` is the opposite**: there is no
> position to place them at, so it clears every assignment for that company — see the
> full-replace warning below.

#### One position per Ansprechpartner — every row must agree on *where*

**A contact holds exactly one position at a company:** company-wide, one whole Einsatzbetrieb, or
some Abteilungen of one Einsatzbetrieb. All their roles apply there. That is exactly why the two
actions ask for **one** `position_client_site_id` + `position_department_ids` and a flat `roles`
list: the shape itself can no longer express a mixed membership, so the classic refusal is not
something an operator can walk into from here any more. Two refusals still exist, both 422s,
both refusals rather than warnings:

| Refusal | When | Message the operator sees |
|---|---|---|
| `position_conflict` | a payload names more than one position — no longer reachable through these two actions, but still raised for a scope-restricted portal actor whose own reach sits at a different position than the one being written | "Ein Ansprechpartner hat genau eine Position: unternehmensweit, ein ganzer Einsatzbetrieb oder einzelne Stationen eines Einsatzbetriebs. Alle Rollen gelten dort." |
| `position_conflict_existing` | a role is *added* at a position the person does not currently occupy — this is the **`approve-portal-role-request`** path, not the two actions here, which replace the position outright | "Diese Person ist bereits zugeordnet: <Position>. Bearbeite die bestehende Zuständigkeit, um sie zu verschieben." |

Consequences worth stating to the operator *before* you write:

- **"Haus 1 soll Haus 2 nicht sehen" is still a site-scoped `approver`** — that has not changed.
  What is no longer possible is one person being `approver` at Haus 1 *and* something else
  elsewhere. If the operator wants both, they want two different people, or one company-wide role.
- **`administrator` and any site-scoped role are mutually exclusive.** `administrator` must be
  company-wide and `site_admin` must be site-scoped, so nobody can hold both — and a company-wide
  `administrator` cannot additionally sit at an Einsatzbetrieb.
- **Moving somebody means moving all their roles at once.** Because the two actions here are a
  full replace, the move is one call: send the new `position_client_site_id` /
  `position_department_ids` together with **every** role they should end up with. There is no MCP
  way to move one role and leave the rest. Say out loud that the whole membership moves before
  confirming.
- **Assigning a role removes their placement.** A placement (a position with no role) only exists
  while the membership holds no role at all, so the first role you grant absorbs the org-chart
  node — including one at a different Einsatzbetrieb. Expect the Organigramm to show them at the
  role's position afterwards, not at both. The reverse also holds now: sending an Einsatzbetrieb
  with `roles: []` turns a role-holder back into a plain placement there, dropping their access.
- Existing memberships that mixed positions (typically an `administrator` who also sat at an
  Einsatzbetrieb, or a placement next to a role at the same site) were normalised on the alluvo
  side when this shipped, always in the direction of the **wider** reach — nobody lost a
  permission. If an operator says "der stand doch auch bei Haus 2", that is the normalisation, and
  the company-wide role they kept already covers Haus 2.

Nothing was migrated in the other direction: a membership that was already company-wide still
reads back with an empty `position_client_site_id` and behaves exactly as it always did. A tenant
that never scopes a role sees no change at all.

> **Known defect — on-site signing fails for a signer who already holds a role elsewhere.** When a
> client signs the Stundenzettel on the employee's device, alluvo grants that signer an `approver`
> **scoped to the Einsatz's Einsatzbetrieb**. If they already occupy a different position — the
> company-wide `administrator`, or a role at another Haus — that grant is refused with
> `position_conflict_existing` and **the whole signature fails**, not just the role write. The
> station manager sees an error on the phone and no Freigabe is recorded. Workaround until it is
> fixed: let a colleague who holds no portal role sign on the device, or take the release through
> the portal, or release without the client — see *Releasing without the client —
> `approve_by_operator`* above, with its own conditions. Reported to alluvo as api#4605; do not
> tell the client their device is broken.

> **The write is a full replace — an omitted `roles` wipes the roles.** Whatever you send *is*
> the contact's complete position and role list for that company; anything you leave out is
> deleted, placements included. Two ways to trigger that by accident, both of which cost the
> person their login:
> - **Calling `assign-portal-roles` with only `portal_access_active`** and no position/`roles` —
>   the omitted list is read as "no roles at no position", not as "leave them alone", and clears
>   every grant.
> - **A typo in a role value.** Unknown values are dropped silently, so `["approvre"]` submits an
>   empty list and removes everything.
>
> So always read the contact's current position and roles first, send them back **in full** plus
> your change, and state the result to the operator before writing.

> **Taking access away has its own action — do not improvise it out of these two.** Three
> distinct operations, one intent each:
> - **"Der soll sich nicht mehr anmelden können", permanently** is
>   **`revoke-portal-access`** on the membership (`0-430`) — the sanctioned path, documented
>   under *Portalzugang entziehen* below. It deletes the role grants and nothing else.
> - **Blocking sign-in temporarily** is `portal_access_active: false` on `assign-portal-roles`.
>   The membership goes `disabled`, the person cannot establish a session, and every role they
>   hold survives. It is the reversible one: flipping it back to `true` returns their exact
>   previous reach. This is the right tool for a lockout you expect to undo — leave of absence,
>   offboarding in progress, a disputed account.
> - **Clearing the roles by hand is destructive and is not a no-op.** There is no fallback to
>   `viewer`: an empty `roles` list deletes every grant for that company, so the person loses
>   the login *and* the site/department scoping behind it — and, unlike `revoke-portal-access`,
>   it takes their **placement** with it, dropping them out of the Organigramm.
>
> Never clear roles as a way of locking someone out — `revoke-portal-access` is the version of
> that intent that keeps the placement and refuses to strand a company. And never let a wipe
> happen as a side effect of a "tidy up the roles" step: state what the list will be, get a
> yes, and only then write.

The mail carries the portal link only — the recipient signs in with the normal email code,
there is no magic link, so forwarding it grants nobody else access.

> **The last-administrator guard reaches you on some paths and not others — check before you
> assume.** On the **`portal_access_active` switch** the guards (last active Administrator, and
> a portal contact locking themselves out) apply to *client* administrators acting inside the
> portal only; an internal operator is exempt and **can** leave a company with no administrator.
> On **`revoke-portal-access`** and on **`archive`** (`0-430`) the last-administrator guard
> applies to **you** as well — the action simply reports itself unavailable with that reason
> rather than throwing. The self-lockout half still never trips for an operator (you are nobody's
> portal contact). Either way, check who else holds `administrator` before removing the last one.

#### The Ansprechpartner list IS the membership — there is nothing to "list"

There is no listing step and no listing action. The **`list-as-portal-contact-person` /
`unlist-as-portal-contact-person` actions no longer exist**, and neither does the
`is_contact_person` flag behind them — do not offer either to an operator. The client portal's
**Kontakte** list simply shows every contact linked to the company, so the link itself is the
entry.

That makes putting someone on (or taking them off) the list an ordinary association write on
the Contact (`0-105`) ↔ Company (`0-3`) pair, preview first:

```
Tool: manage-association
action: "attach"          # "detach" to take them off again
source_type: "0-105"
source_id: <contact_id>
target_type: "0-3"
target_id: <company_id>
label: "Ansprechpartner"
confirmed: false          # preview, then repeat with true
```

`label` is free text written to the pivot verbatim, and on a (company, contact) membership the
only value anything reads back is the literal string `timesheet_approver` — it is what marks the
contact with the "Ansprechpartner" badge at the top of the employee's signer picker and what the
fallback tier of approval correspondence looks for (see *The signer picker* above). Use
`"timesheet_approver"` when the operator means "designate the official Ansprechpartner"; anything
else, including `"Ansprechpartner"`, is a descriptive note that no surface acts on.

Attaching creates the membership row (`0-430`); detaching deletes it. `function_title` — the
person's **job** ("Pflegedienstleitung"), never a portal role — is set afterwards with
`manage-model` `update` on `0-430` (writable there: `status`, `function_title`, `notes`,
`sort_order`; grants deliberately are not).

> **The link grants nothing — say so plainly.** Attaching sends **no** mail, creates **no**
> user and grants **no** role: the fresh membership starts with no grant, so it cannot sign in.
> Access lives solely in `send-portal-invitation` / `assign-portal-roles`. The old UI button was
> labelled "Im Portal freischalten", which wrongly suggested access — never repeat that reading
> to an operator or a client.
>
> Conversely, **detaching is not a lockout** — it removes the whole membership, roles and all,
> and takes the person out of the client's Kontakte list. To revoke a login while keeping the
> record, use `revoke-portal-access` on the membership (permanent, keeps the placement) or
> `assign-portal-roles` with `portal_access_active: false` (a reversible pause that keeps the
> roles) — both below.

### Reading portal state — PortalMembership (`0-430`)

Never guess who can log in. `mcp__alluvo__query-model` on **PortalMembership (`0-430`)**,
filtered by `company_id`, is the read-only answer to "who reaches this client's portal, and as
what". One row per (company, contact), carrying:

| Field | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| `full_name`, `email`, `phone` | the person (read through from the Contact) |
| `function_title` | their **job** ("Pflegedienstleitung") — **not** a portal role |
| `portal_roles_label` | the roles they hold; **empty means no login**, however active the row |
| `portal_scope_label` | where those roles apply — Einsatzbetrieb · Abteilungen, or company-wide |
| `status` | `active` / `disabled` — the access switch, independent of the roles |
| `archived_at` | set = the customer retired this person from their Kontakte list; **always `disabled` too** (see below). Not a default display column — request it explicitly |
| `portal_invitation_status` | `never_invited` / `invited` / `logged_in` — how far along the access path; read off the membership's own `invited_at` |
| `has_portal_login` | a portal **account exists** — *not* proof of a sign-in (see below) |

**Filter it, don't search it.** `0-430` filters on `company_id`, `contact_id`, `status` and the
role through the `grants` relation as **`grants.role`** (two-hop dotted path — a membership holds
one grant per role/scope combination, so this asks "does this membership hold role X"), plus the
usual `created_at` / `updated_at` / `record_source`. "Wer hat bei Firma X Portalzugang", "welche
Zugänge sind deaktiviert" and "wer ist irgendwo `administrator`" are therefore direct queries —
do not fall back to free-text search or to parsing the `portal_scope_label` prose.

**`has_portal_login` and `logged_in` answer different questions — keep them apart.**
`has_portal_login` is true as soon as a portal user account exists, and an account can come into
being **with no login ever happening**: the on-device Stundenfreigabe signing flow provisions one
in advance purely to carry a signature. Only `portal_invitation_status = logged_in` means the
person demonstrably completed a sign-in. Use `has_portal_login` for "is there an account to
adjust", `logged_in` for any statement about actual portal usage.

> **The `logged_in` signal only exists going forward.** It reads a login timestamp that alluvo
> only began stamping with this change, so for contacts whose last sign-in predates it the
> underlying field is empty and they read as `invited`. An empty one is therefore **not**
> evidence that nobody ever logged in — never tell a client "da hat sich nie jemand angemeldet"
> on the strength of an older record. Report what you actually know: invited yes/no, and that
> usage before the changeover is not recorded.

> **`never_invited` on an older record is likewise not proof that no invitation was sent.** Until
> 2026-08-11 a successful `send-portal-invitation` stamped only the legacy contact-company pivot
> and left the membership's `invited_at` null, so every surface that reads the membership — this
> field, the "Einladung" entry on the record, the Organigramm, the Ansprechpartner table — kept
> reporting "Nie eingeladen" after a send that really went out. The action stamps the membership
> now, so anything sent from that date on reads correctly. For an older contact, check the
> timeline (`client_portal_invitation_sent`) before telling a client nobody was ever invited —
> and note that re-sending is harmless and fixes the stamp.

So "can this person confirm the hours?" is `status = active` **and** `portal_roles_label`
contains `approver` (or `administrator`). A `disabled` row with roles, and an `active` row with
none, both mean no. Do not read `function_title` as a permission — the job title and the portal
role share nothing, and conflating them is exactly what this model type exists to prevent.

#### Portalzugang entziehen — `revoke-portal-access` on the membership (`0-430`)

The sanctioned way to take a client contact's portal access away. It runs on **PortalMembership
(`0-430`)** — not on the Contact — via `manage-record-action`, two-stage as always:

```
Tool: manage-record-action
operation: "execute"
model_type: "0-430"
record_id: <membership_id>
action: "revoke-portal-access"
confirmed: false          # preview, then repeat with true
```

It takes no parameters: there is nothing to compose and nothing to send back in full, which is
exactly what makes it safer than the `assign-portal-roles` wipe.

- **It deletes every ROLE grant of that membership and nothing else.** The contact, the
  membership row, its history, its `function_title` and any **role-less placement** — the
  person's position in the Organigramm, including its Abteilungen — all stay. Compare the
  `assign-portal-roles` empty-`roles` wipe, which takes the placement too.
- **One membership = one (company, contact) pair, so there is no `company_id` to get wrong.**
  A contact who is an Ansprechpartner at three portal companies has three memberships; revoking
  one leaves the other two untouched. Name the company to the operator before you confirm.
- **Role-less means no login — there is no fallback to `viewer`.** Portal access is opt-in, so a
  membership left with no role cannot sign in even though `status` stays `active`. Do not report
  the revoke as "deaktiviert": `status` is unchanged and `portal_roles_label` is now empty.
  `→ Reading portal state` above for how the two fields differ.
- **It does not need a portal user.** Unlike `assign-portal-roles` — which only appears for a
  contact with `has_portal_login` — this works for someone who never signed in, which is the
  whole population of backfilled company-wide `viewer` grants.
- **Hidden when there is nothing to revoke.** A membership holding no role grant at all (a
  placement-only Einsatz-Ansprechpartner never had portal access) does not offer the action.
  `list` reports it as `Available: No`; that is a correct answer, not a failed lookup.
- **Refused for the company's last active Administrator**, with the reason "Der Zugang kann
  nicht deaktiviert werden — diese Person ist der letzte Administrator dieses Unternehmens."
  Read it out and have a second administrator appointed first; the guard binds an internal
  operator too (see the callout above). It never throws — the action reports itself unavailable.
- **It grants nothing, ever.** There is no "revoke and re-add a smaller role" mode. Going the
  other direction is `send-portal-invitation` (first grant, mails) or `assign-portal-roles`
  (adjust an existing one) — and after a revoke the contact has no role left, so restoring
  access means re-sending the position and roles in full.
- **Needs `portal_memberships.edit`.** It is logged to the timeline as
  `client_portal_roles_changed`, recording each revoked grant with its role, Einsatzbetrieb and
  Abteilungen — so "was genau wurde entzogen" is answerable afterwards — plus the placements it
  kept. It sends **no** mail: the person is not told, so tell them yourself if that matters.

**Cohorts — `execute_bulk` over membership ids.** The same action over up to **100** memberships
in one call, which is how you clear out a group (e.g. every backfilled company-wide `viewer`
whose contact never signed in). Find them first with `query-model` on `0-430` — filter
`company_id`, `status`, `grants.role` and read `portal_invitation_status` — then:

```
Tool: manage-record-action
operation: "execute_bulk"
model_type: "0-430"
record_ids: [<membership_id>, …]     # max 100
action: "revoke-portal-access"
confirmed: false                     # preview, then repeat with true
```

The preview returns a per-record table — available yes/no with the reason per row — so an
Administrator protected by the guard and a placement-only membership show up **before** you
write. Run it, read the summary to the operator, get a yes, and only then repeat with
`confirmed: true`; the confirmed run acts on the available rows and skips the rest, reporting
both counts. Never send a cohort straight to `confirmed: true` — the preview is the only place
the per-row refusals are visible.

#### Archivieren — the customer's own "remove this person" (`archive` / `unarchive`)

`0-430` carries the `archive` / `unarchive` pair on `manage-record-action` (two-stage as always:
`confirmed: false` preview → operator yes → `confirmed: true`). It is the *only* removal the
portal offers a customer for a contact, and it is registered on the operator side too so both
sides see the same list. Three things the generic archive description does not tell you:

- **Archiving revokes access.** It sets `archived_at` **and** forces `status` to `disabled`,
  stamping who did it. So it is never merely "aus der Liste nehmen" — say plainly that the
  person loses their login before you confirm it. Do not reach for it when the operator only
  wants a tidier list — and not when they only want the *access* gone either: that is
  `revoke-portal-access` above, which leaves the person on the customer's Kontakte list.
- **`unarchive` restores half.** It clears `archived_at` and nothing else; access stays
  `disabled`, because the status the membership held *before* archiving is recorded nowhere and
  guessing `active` would silently re-open a login the customer had closed. Restoring someone
  fully is therefore two calls: `unarchive`, then `assign-portal-roles`
  (`portal_access_active: true`, with their position and full `roles` list) or
  `send-portal-invitation`.
  Never report an `unarchive` as "wieder freigeschaltet".
- **It is refused for the last active Administrator.** Archiving the only remaining active
  `administrator` at a company would leave nobody able to unarchive anyone, so the action comes
  back **unavailable with that reason** rather than throwing. Read the reason out and grant a
  second administrator first. (A second guard — a portal contact archiving themselves — exists
  only for actors inside the customer portal; running as an operator you never trip it.)

Since an archived contact is still returned by your reads, an archived membership is a live
answer to "wo ist der Ansprechpartner hin?" — check `archived_at` before concluding a contact
was deleted or never existed, and before counting a company's Ansprechpartner (`→ head-of-sales`,
`→ account-research`).

**`CompanyContactPerson` (`0-361`) is gone** — the model type was removed, so `query-model`,
`count-model`, `search-model` and `manage-model` against `0-361` all fail. `0-430` absorbed it:
one row per (company, contact) that is the CRM link, the Kontakte-list entry and the portal
membership at once, so it is now also the type for counting a company's Ansprechpartner in CRM
work (see `→ account-research`, `→ head-of-sales`). Keep the two readings apart all the same: a
`0-430` row merely *exists* for every linked contact — only `status = active` plus a
role-bearing grant means they can log in.

### Rollenanfragen — PortalRoleRequest (`0-434`)

A role can also be **asked for from the portal**. Today one surface raises it: the contract
signing step, when the customer names a person who holds no signing right (`→
manage-contract-lifecycle`). That writes a **PortalRoleRequest** (`0-434`) — a request, not a
grant. Until it is decided the named person has a Contact at the company and nothing else: no
membership, no role, no login, no mail. Read the queue with `query-model` on `0-434`, filtered
by `status` (`pending` / `approved` / `rejected`), `company_id` or `contact_id`; there is no
create path, for an operator or over MCP, because a request is a question the *customer* asked.

Decide it with `manage-record-action`, `operation: "execute"` on `0-434`, preview first:
**`approve-portal-role-request`** (`role`, `context_only`, `decision_note`) grants the role —
and, when the request came from a contract, hands that contract over so the person gets the
signing mail; **`reject-portal-role-request`** (`decision_note`) closes it, grants nothing and
mails nobody. Only a `pending` request can be decided, and both need `contacts.edit` +
`client_portal.manage_access`. `role` defaults to the requested one, normally `commercial` —
the role carrying `contracts.sign`. Leave `context_only` at `false`: the "nur dieser eine
Vertrag" path currently closes the request without granting or handing anything over
(alluvo#3721).

Two things follow for anyone reading portal state here. A customer administrator
(`ContactsManage` — `administrator`, `site_admin`) who names a signer **decides in the same
move**, so a request from one of them is already `approved` and never appeared as pending.
And an approval is a real role grant like any other: it lands on the membership (`0-430`) and
shows up in `portal_roles_label`, so the roles you read there may have come from an approved
request rather than from an operator's `assign-portal-roles` call.

The mail itself is transactional and cannot be switched off; only push and in-app can — by
the client, in the portal under Einstellungen → Benachrichtigungen. If an operator needs to
check or change a portal user's channels, `get-notification-preferences` /
`manage-notification-preferences` take a `user_id` (requires permission to view/update that
user; preview → `confirmed: true`). Do **not** promise a client that you can silence a
deadline mail for them.

### The Stundenfreigabe surface is activated per company — a portal role alone shows nothing

There is a **second, independent gate** in front of the client's Stundenfreigabe. A role makes
someone a portal user; whether that portal *shows* Stundenfreigabe at all is decided **per
company** (per Einsatzbetrieb), not per tenant. Default is off, and there is deliberately **no
tenant-level inheritance** — a sibling client of the same tenant having it proves nothing about
this one.

While a company is not activated, that customer sees:

- **no "Dienstpläne/Stundenfreigabe" entry** in the portal menu (EN: "Shift Plans/Hours
  Approval"),
- **no "offene Freigaben" card** on the portal dashboard,
- **no `create-plan` / `shift-plan` buttons** on their Einsatz and Einsatzvertrag pages,
- and the hub URL answers **404** — not 403, not "keine Berechtigung". That is on purpose (a
  customer must not be able to probe which surfaces another client has), so **never read the
  404 as a permission or role problem** and never go fix roles because of it. An old
  `/time-entries/approval` link behaves the same way: it lands on the hub only for an activated
  company, otherwise on the dashboard.

> **The fingerprint: the mail link works, the menu entry is missing.** The reminder and
> "bereit zur Freigabe" mails deep-link to the weekly Freigabe-Kalender, which is **not** behind
> this gate. So a client can receive the mail, follow the link, and release hours, while finding
> nothing named Dienstpläne/Stundenfreigabe anywhere in the portal. "Der Link aus der Mail geht,
> im Menü finde ich nichts" is the exact signature of a company that was never activated — not a
> broken mail and not a missing role.

> **There is no separate "Stundenfreigabe" menu entry — the hub is it.** An activated client has
> exactly one entry, **"Dienstpläne/Stundenfreigabe"**, and it is where the Wochennachweise are
> released; it carries a badge with the number of weeks that actually offer them a signable day
> right now — `open`, tracked, already started, at least one day releasable — no badge at zero.
> It is not a plain status count, so a week sitting with the client whose remaining days are stuck
> on the employee correctly does not raise it. The same label names the shift-plan button on a won AÜV detail page. So for a
> client who *can* see the entry, "ich finde die Stundenfreigabe nicht" is answered by pointing
> at that one item — don't go looking for a second surface, and don't quote the old menu name
> "Dienstpläne" back at them.

**You cannot switch it on.** No MCP tool and no portal or Einstellungen screen activates it;
it is set per company by alluvo. Say that plainly, note which company it is about, and hand it
over — do not promise the client it will be live in this session.

At rollout it was activated for the companies holding a Rahmenvertrag in a
**Rahmenvertragsgruppe**. Every other company is off until enabled individually, including ones
that had the surface before and have live Stundenfreigaben — that is the intended rollout, not a
regression. Report it as "für diesen Kunden noch nicht freigeschaltet".

> **Activating or deactivating it sends nothing and changes no deadlines.** The reminder /
> escalation / Auto-Freigabe pipeline runs off the Fristen settings and ignores this gate
> entirely. So the mails keep going out to a non-activated client, and turning the surface on
> for a client neither triggers a mail nor moves a Frist.
>
> Those Fristen resolve **per company**: a company's own `timesheet.*` override wins, the tenant
> setting is only the fallback. `deadlines_enabled` is the master switch — with it off for that
> company, none of its weeks are promoted, reminded, escalated or auto-approved, whatever the
> tenant default says. Read the *client's* effective values before telling anyone when their
> hours lapse.

### What the client's "gemäß Zeiterfassung" column actually shows

The hub puts the planned Schicht next to the tracked day. The **Ist** ("gemäß Zeiterfassung") is
the shift date's **own whole tracked day, collapsed into one span** — earliest start → latest end
of that calendar day's `work` entries, net minutes summed, break derived as span − worked minutes.
It is the same collapse the Wochennachweis is built from, so the hub and the Nachweis cannot
disagree about a day.

Consequences to hold on to when a client disputes a number:

- **A minus in front of the client is a real shortfall.** The hub used to pick the ONE entry
  closest to the shift's start within a ±1-day window, which produced three wrong answers: a
  split day (Arbeit 13:09–16:50 / Pause / Arbeit 17:20–21:00) reported only its first block —
  3h41 of a 7h21 day, shown as a −3h40 deficit on a fully worked day; a `break` row could win the
  match and be displayed as worked time; and a day with **no** tracking borrowed the neighbouring
  day's entry. All three are gone. Stop offering them as the explanation for an Abweichung —
  investigate the tracking instead.
- **A day with no work entries has no Ist at all** — the cell stays empty and the delta is null.
  An empty Ist means nothing was tracked, not a shortfall to be explained away.
- **`break` rows never count as worked time.** The collapse takes `work` entries only; the break
  is what the derived figure measures, not something added to it.
- **A geteilter Dienst shows the same day on both rows.** Two Schichten on one calendar day each
  display that day's single collapsed span, and the period total counts the **date** once. Never
  add the visible rows up by hand — you would bill the day twice.

**Not-yet-submitted period headers are refreshed when the client opens the hub.** A Nachweis'
Soll/Ist rollups are a snapshot from when it was last built and nothing recomputes them when a
`TimeEntry` arrives afterwards — which is why such a period could read "gemäß Zeiterfassung:
0h 0min" above rows that plainly showed times. Opening the hub now rebuilds every `open` period in
scope **that has no `submitted_to_client_at`**, so that mismatch is fixed and not worth diagnosing
further.

> **A period already submitted to the client is deliberately not refreshed** — `open` with
> `submitted_to_client_at` set. Those numbers are exactly what the
> client is being asked to approve; a late time entry must not move them underneath the request.
> The bound is the stamp, not the status: `open` alone would sweep in the submitted weeks too.
> If a submitted period is genuinely wrong, it changes through the correction flow (see "Released
> or approved hours are locked" below), never by waiting for a refresh — and never by editing the
> `TimeEntry` rows under it. Approved and invoiced periods are refused by the builder outright.

### The hub's cards are approval periods, not calendar weeks

Every card in the client's Dienstpläne/Stundenfreigabe hub is **one approval period**, cut by the
assignment's resolved Abrechnungszeitraum (next section) — under the default `month_segments`
that is 1.–7., 8.–14., 15.–21. and 22.–Monatsende, never a Monday–Sunday week. The card title is
the same label every other surface uses, `01.08.–07.08.2026 (KW 31/32)`, not "KW 32 / 2026".

- **A materialised Wochennachweis keeps its stored bounds.** The scheme only fills what the
  existing Nachweise leave uncovered — the same clamping the employee side writes with — so the
  card and the Nachweis behind its Freigabe button always cover the identical days. Right after a
  scheme switch a card can therefore be shorter than the scheme's segment (see "A scheme change
  never re-cuts existing periods" below); that is correct, not a rendering fault.
- **Two periods starting in the same ISO week are both shown.** Cards used to be keyed by
  (ISO-Jahr, ISO-Woche), so a clamped 10.–14. plus the full 15.–21. collapsed onto one key and one
  of them silently disappeared — the client could never release it. That is fixed: a client who
  reported a missing Freigabe around a segment boundary is not to be re-diagnosed as a role,
  activation or notification problem.
- **A card with no Wochennachweis yet is planning-only** — status "Geplant", no Freigabe button,
  Soll/Ist read off the plan and the tracked days.
- **A period with nothing in it is not rendered at all** — no Schicht rows, `Soll = 0` **and**
  `Ist = 0`. This is not limited to planning-only cards: a *materialised* Wochennachweis is
  dropped by the same rule. They arise for real — the scheme's first segment clamped against an
  Einsatz that starts mid-segment writes a Nachweis with zero of everything (production:
  `01.08.–02.08.2026`), which used to render as "Noch keine Dienstpläne vorhanden · 0h 0min ·
  0h 0min" beside a Freigeben button that released nothing.
  - **A period already freigegeben or abgerechnet stays visible even when empty** (`Approved`,
    `Invoiced`) — an approval is never hidden from the person who gave it.
  - So a client seeing **fewer cards than the month has periods** is expected, and the answer is
    "in dem Zeitraum ist nichts drin" — not "die Freischaltung fehlt", not a missing role and not
    a lost Freigabe. Nobody is being chased for it either: the nightly deadline run only promotes
    periods with *tracked* hours, so an empty one is never submitted to the client, and the
    employee's own release list excludes it too.
- **The plan list's "Offene Freigaben" filter counts the cards that are actually rendered**, not a
  second query, so the filter and the list can no longer disagree — a plan the filter shows really
  does hold a releasable period. Keep it apart from the hub's top KPI **"Offene Wochen"**, which is
  still a plain count of `open` Wochennachweise with any hours across the company and therefore
  includes ones whose card is hidden. The two figures differing is not a bug.

**The KPI is "Aktueller Zeitraum", not "Diese Woche".** It sums Soll/Ist of the period
**containing today** across all of the company's Einsätze. It used to match on ISO year/week, i.e.
"the period that *starts* in the current ISO week" — under any month-aligned scheme that is a
different period, or none: the 01.–07.08. segment starts on a Saturday in KW 31, so on Wednesday
the 5th (KW 32) the card reported 0h. "Der Kunde sieht 0 Stunden in der Übersicht" no longer has
that explanation.

**The deemed-approval footer is only stated where it is actually true.** The sentence "Nicht bis
[Frist] freigegeben oder beanstandet? Gilt als automatisch freigegeben." appears on a period
only when the company has `deadlines_enabled` on **and** `deadline_behavior: auto_approve` **and**
that period has a materialised Nachweis with a stamped `deadline_at` — and it names that real
deadline. The old copy promised a hardcoded "Montag, 19:00 Uhr" of the previous Kalenderwoche to
every client, including those the whole flow was switched off for, and no month segment ends on a
Sunday anyway. So never quote Monday 19:00 to a client: read the Frist off the period. A client
with deadlines off correctly sees no such sentence, and the "Auto-Freigabe" KPI shows "—".

**What the Genehmigungsfiktion actually approves: the still-open days, and only those.** When the
deadline elapses, the days nobody signed are released as their own event, channel *Automatisch
freigegeben (Fristablauf)*, with no signer — and a day someone had already signed on site or in
the portal **keeps that signature**. Only once every day is covered, part by a person and part by
the deadline, does the period itself go `Approved`. Two things to stop saying: that auto-approval
stamps the whole period as unsigned (it used to overwrite the week's channel, which erased the
human signature and left the unsigned days with no release at all — and those days were billed at
zero), and that a period with a Teilfreigabe is somehow exempt from the deadline. **A shift that is
still open — running and not closed — is never auto-released either**: the deadline run reads the
same per-day gate as a human signer, so a day it may not release makes it escalate rather than deem
the period approved.

> **Unchanged:** a period with no reachable approver is escalated, never auto-approved (silence
> from someone who was never asked is not consent), and a period with nothing in it is never
> submitted in the first place. **What "escalated" now means:** the deadline run moves such a
> period to `mediation` and stamps `escalated_at` — there is no separate `escalated` status any
> more, so look for it in the Stundenklärung queue and read `escalated_at` to tell a deadline
> escalation apart from a client/employee dispute.

## Abrechnungszeitraum — an approval period is not an ISO week any more

Hours are released **per period per Einsatz**, and how the period is cut is a contract setting
(**Abrechnungszeitraum**), not a fixed Monday–Sunday week. Four schemes exist:

- `month_segments` — day 1–7, 8–14, 15–21, 22–end of month (the last segment flexes between 7
  and 10 days). The tenant default. No period crosses a month boundary, so invoicing and
  payroll can run as soon as the month's last segment is approved.
- `half_month` — day 1–15 and 16–end of month.
- `monthly` — one period per calendar month, day 1 to end of month; the client approves at the
  same rhythm as the invoice they receive.
- `calendar_week` — legacy ISO Monday–Sunday. Still present on old periods, never set on a new
  contract.

The effective scheme resolves most-specific-first: **assignment contract → framework contract →
company override → tenant setting `timesheet.period_scheme`** (default `month_segments`). Set
it on a contract via `timesheet_period_scheme` (`→ manage-contract-lifecycle`).

**Labels.** A period reads as `01.08.–07.08.2026 (KW 31/32)` — date range plus the ISO week(s)
it spans, since a month segment usually straddles a week boundary. Never rebuild a "KW x" label
from a week number alone; it misdescribes every non-`calendar_week` period. Quote the date
range back to an employee or client, not just a KW.

That one label is what **every** surface the counterparty sees already says: the client portal's
Dienstpläne/Stundenfreigabe cards, the Tätigkeitsnachweis PDF and all twelve Stundenfreigabe
notifications (ready-for-approval, both client and employee reminders, deviation, dispute,
correction requested/escalated, escalation, mediation-SLA, both deemed-approved notices, the
internal digest). So if you quote a KW on the phone, it will not match the portal, the mail or
the PDF in front of the client — read the date range off the period and use that. An employee or client who says "I can't find KW 32" is almost always
looking at a segment label; ask for the dates on their mail rather than searching for a week.

**A scheme change never re-cuts existing periods.** Stored periods keep their `date_from` /
`date_until` forever — including already-approved ones — and newly created periods are clamped
past them. So right after a switch you will see a short transitional period (a legacy ISO week
Jul 27–Aug 2 followed by an Aug 3–7 segment instead of a full Aug 1–7). That is expected, not a
bug — do not try to "repair" it.

## Which periods the employee actually sees

The employee releases hours **per period per Einsatz** (see Abrechnungszeitraum above), not per
entry. Two layers decide what they see, and mixing them up is the usual reason a "missing
period" gets escalated: **whether a `Timesheet` (`0-426`) row exists at all**, and — for the
rows that do — **whether the row is offered to the employee**. Work through both before calling
a missing period a bug.

**Layer 1 — three gates decide whether the period is materialised at all.** A period that fails
any of them has no row: no `0-426` record, no stamped `deadline_at`, no badge count, nothing for
`manage-record-action` to act on, and nothing a `query-model` can find.

- **Periods are clamped to the Einsatz's own start/end.** A period lying entirely outside the
  assignment is never materialised. A blanket six-week look-back on an Einsatz that started
  last Monday yields one period, not six. An Einsatz with no recorded start (or no end) is not
  clamped on that side — an Einsatz missing its own `end_date` falls back to its
  AssignmentContract's `valid_until`.
- **A period with no *releasable* day is never created.** Creation requires a day somebody could
  actually release: an **effective Schicht that nobody is signed off sick for**, or a **closed
  tracked entry**. Neither → no row. "Effective" is the same
  predicate as the Soll (`draft`, `published`, `locked` count; `superseded`, `cancelled`,
  `provisional`, `pending_approval`, `rejected` deliver nothing), and a still-running Stoppuhr
  entry with no end time does not count yet. This is what stops a **dead Einsatz** announcing
  weeks: a cancelled or never-signed contract whose `end_date` still runs into the future used
  to spawn empty periods (0 Soll / 0 Ist) each carrying a Frist nobody could act on. It is also
  why a period whose **every** planned day is covered by a declared Krankmeldung
  (`→ record-absence`) is not built at all — there is nothing in it for anybody to certify. **A
  tracked entry always counts, even on a covered day**: real hours worked despite an absence
  record still reach a period, get released and get billed. This is **not** a `Soll = 0` rule — an
  all-sick period keeps its Soll, the Krankmeldung neutralises the deviation and not the plan.
  Note the gate is *creation only* — a row that already exists, and above all one already signed,
  is still refreshed.
- **Nothing before the employee's alluvo go-live is materialised.** A go-live date
  (`employee_app.go_live_date`, see below) floors the window for **every** writing path, not
  just the employee's own list — the employee app, the client-portal hub and the internal
  materializer all stop at it. A period that *straddles* the go-live is still built **in full**,
  because it carries real tracked hours from after the cutoff. No date set (the default) means
  nothing changes.

**Layer 2 — of the rows that exist, only one with actually TRACKED hours is offered.** The
employee's approvable list *and* its badge require tracked or agreed minutes (Ist > 0 **or**
`agreed` > 0). Planned (Soll) minutes alone do not qualify: a period that is nothing but a
Dienstplan has zero hours to sign, and it used to list as a fully selectable period with a
"0,00 h" badge — an employee could tick it and walk a ward manager through signing nothing.
`agreed` is in the rule because a client or operator correction writes that span and it is what
gets billed, so a period carrying only an agreed time is still real. Note the asymmetry with
layer 1: a planned-only period **does** get a row (it has a Soll, a Frist and a `0-426` record
you can query) but is **not** offered for release.

So "the employee doesn't see period X" is expected when X lies outside the Einsatz, holds
no releasable day (no Schicht, no tracked entry, or every planned day covered by a
Krankmeldung), ended before their go-live, or carries only planned
hours. **Treat "no period" as a valid answer meaning *there is nothing to account for* — not as
a data error to escalate.** Check for a time entry in that period (`→ build-dienstplan`) and
read the employee's go-live before escalating it as missing. A planned Schicht on its own is not
enough to release — if the period holds only a Dienstplan, the answer is "nobody tracked any
time in it", and the fix is tracking (or an agreed correction), not chasing the employee. Also
confirm you are looking at the right cut: under `month_segments` there is no "KW 32" to find, so
an operator asking after a calendar week may be asking about two periods, or about a segment
that spans parts of two weeks.

> **Nothing here needs repairing, and nothing is lost by a period not existing.** A `Timesheet`
> is derived data — it is rebuilt from the Schichten and time entries the moment the period is
> asked for again. So an Einsatz that is revived, a Schicht planned late, or a go-live moved
> backwards brings the period straight back with its hours intact. Never offer "recreate the
> Zeitraum" as a fix; there is no MCP write on `0-426` anyway (`→ head-of-disposition`).

> **A period that exists and then empties out is not closed — it is gone, and it comes back by
> itself.** Layer 1 above is about a row that was **never created**; a row that already exists and
> then loses every releasable day (every Schicht cancelled or deleted, or every day covered by a
> declared Krankmeldung) is **soft-deleted** — it is in the trash, not in a status. Say it that way
> to an operator: *der Zeitraum ist nicht geschlossen, er ist weg — und er kommt von selbst zurück.*
> Five things to get right:
>
> - **It is not in the list, the board, the queues, the deadlines or the client portal** — a row
>   that is not there is absent from all of them. It is also out of the monthly
>   Stundennachweis-Checkliste, and the nightly deadline run sends it no reminder and no escalation.
> - **It is recoverable, and nothing is lost.** Same row, same id, same days, same history — find it
>   with `search-model` on `0-426` and `trashed: "only"` (or `"with"` alongside the live ones).
> - **The way back is the real fix, not a restore.** Plan a Schicht back into the period, or
>   reject/cancel the Krankmeldung, and alluvo restores that very row with its days rebuilt. Never
>   offer "restore the Zeitraum" (`delete-model` with `action: "restore"`) as the answer — restoring
>   a period whose days are still all covered only puts it straight back in the trash.
> - **Never diagnose it from `soll_minutes = 0`.** An all-sick period **keeps its Soll**: 23,10 h
>   Soll against 0,00 h Ist is exactly this case. Read the absences, not the figures.
> - **A period carrying evidence is never pruned** — a client or employee confirmation, any
>   **Freigabe** row (`0-435`), a day the client already signed, an `invoiced_at` stamp, or an
>   unresolved Korrektur (`pending_employee` / `pending_client` / `escalated`) all keep it. So does
>   any status other than `open`: a week in `pending_employee`, `mediation`, `approved` or
>   `invoiced` stays where it is, however empty its days have become.

> **A Dienstplan still in `draft` is not the reason.** A Dienstplan-Periode (`0-110`) sitting in
> Entwurf is easy to reach for as the explanation, and it is the wrong one: `draft` counts as
> effective, so its Schichten are visible in the employee app, they book the employee's
> availability, and they produce the period's **Soll** on the Stundenzettel exactly like a
> published plan's. What blocks the employee's release is tracked hours (Ist or `agreed`), not
> the plan's status — so publishing the Dienstplan will not make a missing period appear. Do not
> offer it as the fix here.
>
> Where the draft status *does* bite is the **customer** side: the client portal's Dienstplan
> list and the "Nächste Schichten" table skip a `draft` Periode entirely. So "the customer can't
> see the Dienstplan / can't check the released hours against the plan" is the symptom that
> really points at an unpublished month — publish it (`→ build-dienstplan`, step 7), and note
> that publishing sends the employee nothing.

> **A `superseded` Periode delivers no Soll at all — and that is the correct number.** The
> Stundenzettel's Soll is built only from **effective** Schichten, so five statuses contribute
> exactly zero: `superseded` ("Ersetzt" — a plan replaced by a newer one for the same range),
> `cancelled` (terminated Einsatz), `provisional` (pre-signature wizard plan), `pending_approval`
> and `rejected`. Only `draft`, `published` and `locked` deliver Soll.
>
> This matters most right after a **Umplanung**. A replaced plan used to be demoted to `draft`,
> which *is* effective — so the old plan kept counting toward the period's Soll alongside the new
> one, and the Soll came out roughly doubled. It is now inert. If a period's Soll dropped after a
> Dienstplan was replanned, that is the correction landing, **not** hours going missing: read the
> Soll off the *replacing* Periode and compare against that. Never "repair" it by recreating
> Schichten on the superseded plan — it is closed to new Schichten anyway
> (`→ build-dienstplan`, step 3), and it is terminal.
>
> An **already approved or invoiced** period is never affected: alluvo refuses to supersede a plan
> whose Stundenzettel are approved or invoiced, so a period you have signed off cannot lose its
> Soll to a replanning (`→ build-dienstplan`, step 7).

**The nightly deadline run applies the same hours rule as the employee's own list.** Automatically
submitting an open period to the client — stamping `submitted_to_client_at`; the status stays
`open` — requires **tracked or agreed** minutes (`Ist > 0` or `agreed > 0`) — planned minutes alone are not enough — and additionally waits until the period's
last planned Schicht lies in the past. So a planned-only period reaches the client neither
manually nor by deadline: it used to be promoted like any other, mailing the client "bitte
freigeben" for a period with nothing on it and then deeming that nothing approved. This is also
why an empty period card can be hidden from the portal without anyone losing a Freigabe — it
could never have been submitted to the client in the first place. When asked "why is this period
not with the client?", check both: no tracked time blocks it outright, an unelapsed deadline blocks
only the automatic route — and read `submitted_to_client_at`, not the status, for the answer.

### A visible period is not a releasable one — the three urgency states

The employee's start page carries an **"Ausstehende Aufgaben"** card with **one row per approval
period**, labelled by its date range ("Zeitraum 21.07. – 27.07.2026"), and classifies each into
one of three states:

| Badge the employee reads | State | When |
|---|---|---|
| `überfällig seit <Datum>`, grouped under **Überfällig** | Overdue | the period's `deadline_at` has elapsed |
| `bis <Datum>`, grouped under **Anstehend** | Open | the period has ended, its deadline is still ahead |
| `ab <Datum> freigebbar` plus "Läuft noch — noch nichts zu tun", under **Anstehend** | Waiting | the period is still running (its `date_until` is today or later) |

**The card used to read "1 Zeitraum bereit zur Freigabe" for the period the employee was still
working.** It no longer does, and that changes the answer to a common report: *"der Mitarbeiter
sieht keine Stundenfreigabe / kann nicht freigeben"* about the **current** period is now the
designed state, not a fault to diagnose. Establish which period they mean first — for a running
one the answer is the date it opens (the day after it ends), not a Tagesabschluss chase, not a
go-live check and not a portal-activation check.

Three details that decide what you tell them:

- **Running beats overdue.** The still-running test is applied first, so a period whose
  `deadline_at` already lies in the past is still shown as *Waiting* while it runs — a period
  can never be reported überfällig before it has ended.
- **A period with no stamped `deadline_at` carries no badge and is never überfällig** — it falls
  back to *Open* on the period end alone. "Da steht keine Frist" is that case, not a lost
  deadline.
- **The card's header badge counts only the überfällige rows**, and at most three period rows are
  listed before the rest collapse into a "N weitere Zeiträume" entry. So no red number does not
  mean nothing is waiting, and a short list is not a missing period.

**The candidate pool itself is unchanged.** The two layers above — Einsatz clamp, substance and
go-live floor deciding whether a row exists; tracked (or agreed) hours deciding whether it is
offered — still decide what reaches this card. Urgency only ranks what is already there, so none
of the "period X is missing" diagnoses above are affected.

### A fourth row: "Danach: <Zeitraum>" — the next period, previewed, not a task

Below the rows above the card now also shows the **next** period the employee will have to
release, one per Einsatz: **"Danach: 08.08. – 14.08.2026"** with the hint **"Freigabe ab 08.08.
möglich"** (EN: "Next up: …" / "Can be released from …"). The same preview appears in the
Freigabe-Wizard, step 1, under the heading **"Kommt als Nächstes"** — a dashed card below the
selectable periods, checkbox greyed out, a "Geplant <h>" badge and a lock line "Freigabe ab
TT.MM." So when an employee says "unter meinen Zeiträumen steht noch einer, den ich nicht
anklicken kann", that is this, and it is designed.

What it is *not* — this is the part that decides your answer:

- **It is not a task and not a backlog item.** No `Timesheet` row exists behind it; the
  period is projected from the Einsatz's resolved Abrechnungszeitraum. Materialising a row is
  exactly what stamps a Frist, so the preview carries **no `deadline_at`**, is **not** in the
  card's red badge (that still counts only überfällige rows), is never submitted to the client,
  and the nightly Fristen-/Mahnlauf ignores it. Nobody is being chased for it.
- **It is not queryable.** Nothing exists in the data, so a `query-model` on the period finds
  nothing and no operator-side backlog figure can contain it. "Der Mitarbeiter sieht einen
  Zeitraum, den ich nicht finde" is expected for this row (`→ head-of-disposition`).
- **It is not clickable** — no link, no button, no chevron — and it always sits under
  **Anstehend**, never under Überfällig.
- **It never makes the card appear on its own.** The card renders only when there is a real
  approvable period; an employee with a clean slate sees no card and no preview.

When it shows at all:

- only if the projected period has **planned Schichten** with net minutes > 0 — the same
  `effective()` rule as the Soll, so `draft`, `published` and `locked` Dienstpläne count while
  `superseded`, `cancelled`, `provisional`, `pending_approval` and `rejected` deliver nothing.
  No preview therefore means "im nächsten Zeitraum ist nichts geplant", not a fault.
- **clamped to the Einsatz.** A period reaching past the Einsatzende previews only the covered
  days; once the Einsatz has ended there is no preview at all.
- it starts **after the last period the Einsatz already has**, not on the next scheme boundary —
  so right after an Abrechnungszeitraum switch the preview follows the transitional period (see
  "A scheme change never re-cuts existing periods" above) instead of contradicting it.

Its dates, KW-Span and planned hours are cut with the same primitives as the real period, and it
uses the same `01.08.–07.08.2026 (KW 31/32)` label — the preview cannot name the period
differently from the card that later replaces it. The "Freigabe ab" date is the previewed
period's **own start day**; where periods run back-to-back that is the same day the currently
running period becomes releasable, so the two rows naming one date is correct, not a duplicate.

**"Bis wann muss der Mitarbeiter freigeben?" is a per-client answer.** The date behind the
`bis <Datum>` badge is the period's own stored `Timesheet.deadline_at` — computed per company
as **period end (`date_until`) + `deadline_days_after_week_end` days, at `deadline_time`**, in the
tenant timezone. Both values resolve company-override-first with the tenant setting as fallback,
exactly like the client-side Fristen above. Two employees working the same period at two clients
therefore have two different deadlines: read the Frist off *that* employee's period for *that*
company rather than quoting a tenant default.

### "Ist meine Unterschrift angekommen?" — the employee now has a place to look

After a client representative signs on the employee's device, the employee has **three** surfaces
that say the release is in — none of which existed as an answer before, when the only feedback was
a confirmation that vanished and a wizard that shows nothing but what is still open. So an operator
asked "die Mitarbeiterin hat unterschreiben lassen und weiß nicht, ob es durch ist" no longer has
to look it up for them; walk them to one of these instead.

**1. "Meine Freigaben" in the employee-app menu.** A permanent nav entry under **Stunden &
Auslagen**, pointing at the hours-confirmation page. That page existed all along but was reachable
*only* by typing the URL or following a deadline-mail deeplink, so nothing in the app led back to
it — that is what changed. It sits next to the **Stundenfreigabe** entry in the same group, and the
two are not the same thing: Stundenfreigabe opens the batch-sign dialog (what is still to release),
"Meine Freigaben" is the periods themselves. Both appear only when the employee-app **Zeiterfassung**
feature is active for that user; an employee without it sees neither.

Two limits before you promise an employee a complete history:

- **Six weeks back, not forever.** The page materialises and lists periods from the same ~42-day
  look-back as the approvable list. "Ich finde meine Freigabe vom Mai nicht" is the window, not a
  lost release — read that period off the operator-side Stundennachweis list instead (above).
- **It lists only periods in `PendingEmployee`, `Approved` or `Mediation`** (plus any period
  holding a client correction awaiting the employee's accept/reject). An `open` period is not on
  it — submitted to the client or not, and it is not a read-only archive either — confirm, dispute and
  on-site signing all run from that page.

**2. The confirmation after the batch sign names what was signed.** It used to read "Die
Unterschrift ist hinterlegt" and auto-close after 3 seconds. It now lists **number of periods ·
number of days**, the **date span** (`Zeitraum <von> bis <bis>`) and **Unterschrieben von <Name>**,
carries a **"Zu meinen Freigaben"** button into the page above, and stays up **6 seconds**. The
figures come from the selection that was submitted, so on a **Teilfreigabe** the day count is the
days actually released, not the week — an employee reading back four days on a five-day week is
correct, not a dropped day.

**3. The wizard's empty state distinguishes "nothing to do" from "already released".** Reopening
the Stundenfreigabe quick action with nothing outstanding used to show one blank screen for both —
exactly the screen an employee reaches when they come back to check. It now reads **"Alles
freigegeben"** with **"Zuletzt freigegeben am <Datum, Uhrzeit>"**, the period (`Zeitraum KW …`),
the signer's name and the same archive button, whenever a last release exists; with none it still
reads "Aktuell warten keine Freigabezeiträume auf eine Kundenfreigabe." and offers no button. Three
things decide what stands there, and they matter when an employee
disputes it:

- It is the employee's most recent **standing** `TimesheetRelease` (`0-435`) — a release counts as
  standing while **at least one** of its days is un-revoked. Revoke every day of it
  (`revoke_release`, above) and the next-most-recent release takes its place.
- It is scoped through the **employee's own** periods, not by contact — the name shown is the
  **client representative** who released those hours, whoever that was. "Da steht ein fremder Name"
  is that, not a mix-up.
- **Freigaben per Fristablauf are included deliberately**, with `signer_label` supplying the "who"
  where nobody signed. From the employee's side deemed approval is still "die Stunden sind durch";
  hiding it would put the blank screen back for exactly the case where nobody signed.

None of this is MCP-reachable — it is what the employee sees, so it is the answer you give, not a
call you make. To verify it yourself, read the release off `TimesheetRelease` (`0-435`) as above.

### The go-live cutoff hides requests — it approves nothing

The go-live date is when an employee starts working in alluvo. Before it they are asked neither
to release hours, to confirm Einsatzmitteilungen, nor to hand in old AU-Bescheinigungen — that
period still ran in the tenant's previous system. Resolution order: **per-employee override → tenant-wide
`employee_app.go_live_date` → none** (no cutoff).

Read or change the per-employee value with `manage-record-settings` — as of this surface it is
no longer a Company-only tool:

- `action: "describe"`, `model_type: "0-2"`, `model_id: <employee id>` — lists the applicable
  keys with resolved value, tenant default, and whether an override is stored. **Describe
  first**; keys are model-scoped, and Employee (`0-2`) currently carries only this one.
- `action: "set"`, `key: "employee_app.go_live_date"`, `value: "YYYY-MM-DD"` — stores the
  override (needs `update` permission on the Employee). `action: "clear"` removes it and reverts
  to the tenant default.

> **Never call it "approving" or "closing off" the past.** It only decides what the employee is
> *asked* about. No `Timesheet` is marked approved, no Einsatzmitteilung acknowledgement is
> stamped, nothing is signed on anyone's behalf — clearing the date or moving it backwards
> rebuilds every untouched Zeitraum straight from its Schichten and time entries. The distinction
> is legal, not cosmetic: a released Zeitraum carries a real approver contact and signature image,
> and a pre-go-live one has none. Fabricating one would put false evidence into an AÜG-relevant
> record — which is also why the cutoff **never** removes a Zeitraum that carries a client or
> employee confirmation, a release or an invoice, however far it predates the go-live.

It settles **Einsatzmitteilung confirmations** by the same rule: an Einsatz whose most recent
§11 notification was *sent* before the employee's go-live is no longer surfaced for confirmation.
A receipt with no recorded send date is never auto-settled — it stays pending rather than being
silently dropped.

It settles **overdue AU-Bescheinigungen** the same way: an AU whose submission deadline
(`medical_certificate_due_at`) fell before the go-live disappears from the employee's app
entirely — the absence itself stays visible, but with no overdue badge, no deadline line and no
upload button, the app's certificate counters skip it, and the automatic employee reminder mail
is floored at the go-live too. An AU with **no** recorded deadline is never auto-settled. The
certificate was almost certainly handed in in the tenant's previous system and simply never
reached alluvo; asking the employee to re-upload old paper helps nobody. Operator surfaces are
deliberately unchanged — Disposition keeps seeing the AU as overdue (`→ record-absence`).

**Most of your MCP figures do not see the cutoff — the Zeiträume now do.** Split the two cases:

- **Abrechnungszeiträume (`Timesheet`, `0-426`) are gone, not hidden.** The cutoff is now a
  materialisation gate, so no period ending before an employee's go-live is created any more, and
  the unconfirmed ones already on disk were removed in a one-off cleanup. A `query-model` on
  `0-426` therefore agrees with what the employee is shown, and "ich finde die alten Wochen im
  Backend nicht mehr" is that cleanup, not lost data — those hours were tracked in the tenant's
  previous system (zvoove/Landwehr), which stays the record for them. **Signed or invoiced
  periods were never touched**, on either side of the cutoff, so a pre-go-live Zeitraum someone
  actually released is still there.
- **Everything else still counts the pre-go-live period.** A `search-model` count of time entries
  (`TimeEntry`), an Assignment search on `assignment_notification_acknowledgement_state:
  pending`, or an AbsencePeriod (`0-13`) query for missing AU-Bescheinigungen all include it —
  the cutoff is applied on those employee-app surfaces, not to the data. So those operator-side
  backlog figures can legitimately exceed what the employee is shown; check their go-live before
  telling them they are sitting on three pending weeks.

When you report on hours from before a tenant's go-live, say what bounds the answer: alluvo
holds the Zeiträume from the go-live onwards, and the period before it lived in the previous
system. Read the employee's own cutoff (`manage-record-settings` `action: "describe"`,
`model_type: "0-2"`) rather than assuming the tenant default — waves differ per employee.

**Counter-confirmation is triggered by a client edit, not by a break.** Once the client
releases a week, the employee is asked to counter-confirm only where the agreed values differ
from what they actually tracked — start, end, **or** break. A scheduled break on the Schicht
alone does not trigger it. The employee's objection window is measured from the moment the
client confirmed, independent of whatever was left of the client's own deadline. If an
employee reports a counter-confirmation request, a day in that week really was edited —
compare agreed vs tracked start/end/break for that week rather than dismissing it.

> **A day with no times on either side is skipped before the break is compared** — so a week
> whose only difference was the inherited break of a day nobody worked now goes straight to
> `approved` on the client's signature, with no counter-confirmation and no "die vereinbarten
> Stunden weichen von Ihren erfassten Stunden ab" mail. That case was every reported sick day:
> a day with no tracking inherits the Schicht's 30-minute break as `agreed_break` while
> `ist_break` stays 0, and start/end are null and equal on both sides. Two consequences for how
> you read a queue: **a pure Krankheitswoche no longer appears in `PendingEmployee`** — its
> absence there is the fix, not a stuck transition — and a `PendingEmployee` week is now much
> more likely to be a real edit worth looking at. The fix is in the comparison, not in the data,
> so it applies to weeks already stored; nothing was backfilled and nothing needed to be.

## "Einstempeln geht nicht" — the Stoppuhr can be switched off tenant-wide

The second-precise live timer (Einstempeln / Pause / Fortsetzen / Ausstempeln) is a tenant
setting, `live_timer_enabled`, and it is **off by default** — the planned and manual capture
paths cover the normal case. It used to only hide the button while the API still accepted the
call; **starting a timer is now refused server-side**, with a 422 (`TIMER_STATE`) reading *"Die
Stoppuhr ist für dein Unternehmen nicht aktiviert. Erfasse deine Zeiten bitte über „Zeit
erfassen" oder übernimm den Dienstplan."*

- **Never answer a time-capture question with "tap Einstempeln" before checking that toggle.**
  With the timer off the two working answers are the ones the message names: log the hours
  manually ("Zeit erfassen") or take them from the Dienstplan.
- **Only clock-IN is gated.** Ausstempeln, Pause and Fortsetzen still work on purpose, so a
  timer that was already running when the setting was switched off can still be closed instead
  of stranding an open entry. An employee reporting "ich kann nicht mehr ausstempeln" is
  therefore *not* this — look elsewhere.
- **It is an MCP setting now.** `manage-settings` has a `time_tracking` group:
  `action: "get"` shows `live_timer_enabled`, `action: "update"` flips it — tenant-wide, no
  per-company override. The tool writes immediately (no `confirmed` preview), so state the key
  and the new value and get the operator's go-ahead before calling it. In the web app it is
  **Einstellungen → Apps → Zeiterfassung → "Live-Timer aktiviert"** (the former *Einstellungen
  → Zeiterfassung* page redirects there).
- **Turning it off does not clean up what it let through.** A stray timer entry booked while the
  button was still reachable stays on the day and keeps widening that day's span on the
  Tätigkeitsnachweis — correct it as a normal operator day correction (see the sections below),
  and mind that a released or approved day is locked.

## Tagesabschluss — the employee must close each day before the week can be released

Releasing hours is gated on the employee having **closed every day being released**. "Tag
abschließen" (Feierabend) is an explicit act in the employee app — it is no longer a question
asked after each logged shift.

- **Closing a day also lifts the clock on it.** An active closure makes the day due for release
  regardless of its planned end, so a day finished early releases at once instead of sitting behind
  "Schicht läuft noch — kann erst nach Feierabend freigegeben werden" for the rest of the shift
  window. This only lets the employee's *own declaration* override the plan, never the absence of
  one: an unclosed day still waits for its planned end, and withdrawing the closure puts it back
  there. Nothing downstream is loosened — the § 4 ArbZG break check and the plan-deviation check
  below run after this, so a closure that no longer settles its day is still refused.
- **The release fails with a 422 naming the unclosed dates** ("Diese Tage sind noch nicht
  abgeschlossen: …"). Only the days *being* released are checked, so a Teilfreigabe of closed
  days is not blocked by an open remainder of the week. Days with no completed time entry are
  skipped, and days before the employee's go-live are floored out (see the cutoff above). An
  employee reporting "ich kann meine Stunden nicht freigeben" is almost always this — ask which
  dates the message named. Those dates are also the ones you can now close yourself when the
  employee cannot (see *Closing a day for the employee*, below).
- **A day covered by a DECLARED Abwesenheit owes no Tagesabschluss at all** — it is not merely
  unblocked, it is never asked for. The employee's "noch abzuschließen" prompt and this release gate
  read the same definition, so the two cannot disagree about what is outstanding. **A *submitted*
  Krankmeldung already counts** — `submitted`, `pending`, `needs_revision` and `approved` all
  discharge the day; only a never-submitted `draft`, a `rejected` and a `cancelled` absence do not.
  Closing a day is the employee confirming what they worked, and on a day their own Krankmeldung
  covers there is nothing to confirm — whether anyone has approved it yet is a pay question, not an
  attendance one. So an employee stuck on a sick day no longer needs the approval to release;
  a *rejected* absence, on the other hand, puts the day back on their list (`→ record-absence`).
  The case this covers is the day where hours **were** tracked and the AU arrived afterwards — the
  normal order for a late Krankmeldung — which previously stayed on the employee's list forever and
  took the whole week's release with it. Deliberate consequence: such a day now releases without the
  employee having confirmed those hours; the hours themselves are untouched and still reach the
  timesheet. A clean sick day with no time entries never prompted in the first place.
- **A `replacement_needed` Schicht needs no time tracking — but it IS Soll on the timesheet.**
  Where an approved absence blocked the Schicht (`→ build-dienstplan`, *the Schicht's own status*),
  the employee's app shows it flagged *"Entfällt — Krankmeldung"* with no log button, and logging
  it is refused — that is unchanged. What changed: the blocked Schicht **does count into the
  week's `soll_minutes`** again, so the day keeps its planned times and the Tätigkeitsnachweis can
  print "geplant 06:00–14:12 · Krank" instead of a blank line. An absence week's Soll is therefore
  **no longer 0** — do not report a non-zero Soll on a Krankheitswoche as a defect, and do not
  expect the Soll to drop when a Krankmeldung is approved. No deviation arises from it: the covered
  day's `deviation_minutes` is neutralised (see *a Kranktag is not a deviation*, below). The
  employee's **salary** target is a separate figure — the Arbeitszeitkonto derives its own Soll
  from salary targets and absence periods and never reads the timesheet's `soll_minutes`, so
  nothing is double-counted.
- **Historical days were backfilled.** Every past day that was already break-compliant carries a
  seeded closure, so migrated and zvoove-imported weeks are not walled off. A past day whose
  break fell short stays open on purpose — the employee closes it with a § 4 ArbZG reason,
  exactly as the old break gate required. For existing data the gate is no stricter than before.
- **Closing a short-break day is a § 4 ArbZG declaration**, not a checkbox: the employee
  acknowledges the Belehrung, gives a reason, states whether it was a one-off and — if it
  recurs — what was done about it. The owners of every Einsatz worked that day are notified,
  once per distinct declaration (re-closing with the same reason and the same minutes does not
  notify again). If the employee asks for support, each Einsatz owner also gets a high-priority
  task due the next working day. Never coach an employee into a reason to clear the gate — the
  declaration is the ArbZG record, not a formality.
- **A day that deviates from its plan needs a reason too — a category *and* a sentence, both
  mandatory.** The Feierabend is refused until the employee answers both halves:
  `deviation_reason_code` (a `code` from the tenant's Abweichungsgrund-Katalog, below) and
  `deviation_reason` (their own words). A submit carrying only one half is asked again, never
  half-stored — the code makes the deviation countable, the sentence is what the Entleiher reads
  on the § 17c AÜG Tätigkeitsnachweis, and neither substitutes for the other.
  - **When it fires:** the day has a plan *and* tracking *and* the two differ by **more than 5
    minutes** (`DayDeviation::TOLERANCE_MIN` — the same definition `deviation_flag` uses, so the
    gate and the timesheet cannot disagree). An `extra` day (worked with no planned Schicht) and a
    `missing` day (planned, nothing tracked) are **never** asked: the first has nothing to deviate
    from, the second has no Feierabend to hang the question on.
  - **And it is suppressed when the § 4 question already covers the same minutes.** A day whose
    *gross* attendance matches the plan (same 5-minute tolerance) and whose only net delta is a
    missing break is asked **once**, in the § 4 dialog — not a second time as "warum weicht dein
    Tag ab?". Such a day closes with a `no_break_reason` and **no** `deviation_reason` /
    `deviation_reason_code`, even though its `deviation_minutes` is non-zero. Only while the break
    gate actually fires: a break *longer* than planned is no § 4 case, is explained nowhere else,
    and keeps its own question. Two consequences: never read a missing `deviation_reason` on a
    deviating day as a hole in the data (`→ head-of-disposition` for the counting caveat), and do
    not expect the deviation fields when you close such a day yourself — the operator action builds
    its form from the same gates and simply will not offer them.
  - **The options are direction-filtered by the server**, from each catalogue row's `direction`.
    With the seeded rows a day that ran long offers *Länger geblieben (Absprache)*,
    *Übergabe/Dokumentation hat länger gedauert*, *Unterbesetzung oder Notfall auf Station*; a day
    that ran short offers *Früher gegangen (Absprache)*, *Vom Einsatzbetrieb früher entlassen*,
    *Später angefangen*. *Pause weicht vom Plan ab* and *Sonstiges* apply either way. A code from
    the wrong direction is refused ("Bitte wähl einen Grund aus, der zu dieser Abweichung
    passt."), so a long day can never be filed as "früher gegangen". Never re-derive that mapping
    yourself or read a stored code as direction-free — and since the tenant maintains the
    catalogue, read the row's own `direction` rather than assuming it from the wording of a label.
    Deactivated rows are not offered at all.
  - **The free text has a quality bar: at least 10 characters and at least two words of two or
    more letters.** A lone "." used to reach a signed client document ("Abweichung: ."), so
    "nicht leer" is no longer the test — `..........`, `----------` and `1234567890` are all
    refused ("Bitte beschreib die Abweichung in mindestens 10 Zeichen …"). It is a shape check,
    not a content check. **Never draft, suggest or auto-fill a placeholder that merely clears
    it** — the sentence is the employee's own account of their day and goes to the client.
  - **Both gates come back in one round-trip.** A day that breaks § 4 *and* deviates is asked for
    the break declaration and the deviation reason together, not one after the other.
  - **Re-closing a day that no longer deviates clears both fields.** After you correct the hours,
    a stale explanation is not left behind — and conversely, an edit that *creates* a deviation
    leaves the closed day without one until it is withdrawn and re-closed.
  - **You can file both halves yourself when you close a day for the employee** — they are the
    `deviation_reason_code` / `deviation_reason` fields of `close_tracked_day_for_employee` (see
    *Closing a day for the employee*, below), under exactly the same rules. What you can also do,
    without writing anything, is read the stored code on the day — see `0-427` above.
- **A second 422 blocks release even on a day that IS closed** ("An diesen Tagen wurde die
  Pausenzeit nachträglich geändert und liegt jetzt unter der gesetzlichen Vorgabe: …"). It fires
  when the closure carries **no § 4 reason** and the day's breaks fall short *as the entries
  stand now*. The only way to reach that state is an operator editing a closed, compliant day
  into a violation — the employee's own writes are refused while the day is closed. Remediation
  is **withdraw the closure and close the day again with a reason**, never closing it again on
  top. A closure that already carries a reason stays settled regardless of later edits: the
  employee acknowledged a short break for that day, and re-asking is pointless. Read the message
  before diagnosing — "nicht abgeschlossen" and "nachträglich geändert" are two different faults
  with two different fixes.
- **The closure row records the whole day, not just the break verdict.** Snapshotted at close
  time: `worked_minutes`, `planned_minutes`, `first_start_at`, `last_end_at` beside
  `required_break_minutes` / `tracked_break_minutes`, plus the § 4 follow-up answers
  (`no_break_reason`, `is_one_off`, `prevention_measures`, `support_requested`), the deviation
  pair (`deviation_reason_code`, `deviation_reason`) and the derived `break_violated` boolean. The
  Einsatz owner's no-break notification carries the same day summary and follow-up answers, so
  "zu wenig Pause" now arrives with the fact that decides what to do — whether that was a 6-hour
  day or an 11-hour one.
- **No generic MCP surface — but it is a model type now.** The closure is
  `TimeTrackingDayClosure` (`0-422`) and is deliberately **not** on the MCP allowlist:
  `list-model-types` reports it as `not_available` (it is listed, with a reason — not
  omitted), and `query-model` / `get-model` / `manage-model` all refuse it. You still cannot read
  or withdraw one via `mcp__alluvo__*`; "which days are still open?" is answered in the employee's
  app or the web app. **Creating one is the exception** — not through a model tool, but through
  the record action on the *day* (`0-427`) described next. The one thing `0-422` *is* good for is
  as an automation-workflow trigger — "when an employee closes a day, do X"
  (`→ build-automation-agent`).

### Closing a day for the employee — `close_tracked_day_for_employee` on `TimesheetDay` (`0-427`)

A tracked day nobody ever closed used to be a dead end for you: "Tag abschließen" existed **only**
in the employee app, which resolves the employee from the logged-in user and so could never act for
anyone else. A day the employee tracked and then never closed — they left, lost the phone, simply
forgot — held its whole week's Stundenfreigabe hostage with no operator-side way out. Now you can
close it on their behalf, as a real MCP write via `manage-record-action` on `0-427`, two-stage like
every other action:

```
manage-record-action(model_type: "0-427", record_id: <day id>,
                     operation: "list")        # what this day needs, and why it may refuse
manage-record-action(model_type: "0-427", record_id: <day id>,
                     operation: "execute", action: "close_tracked_day_for_employee",
                     data: { no_break_reason: "…", is_one_off: true, support_requested: false,
                             deviation_reason_code: "handover_delayed",
                             deviation_reason: "Übergabe an die Spätschicht hat länger gedauert" },
                     confirmed: false)          # preview first, then confirmed: true
```

- **It relaxes nothing.** Both gates above run unchanged, because the action *delegates* to the
  same employee-side close rather than reimplementing it. A day breaching § 4 ArbZG still needs
  `no_break_reason` plus its follow-up (`is_one_off`, `support_requested`, and
  `prevention_measures` whenever `is_one_off` is false); a deviating day still needs both
  `deviation_reason` (≥ 10 characters, ≥ 2 real words) and a `deviation_reason_code` whose
  `direction` matches the deviation. A submission missing an answer is refused outright, never
  half-stored. **Never invent a reason to clear a gate** — the § 4 text is an ArbZG record and the
  deviation text is printed on the § 17c AÜG Tätigkeitsnachweis the client signs.
- **Ask the day what it needs first.** `operation: "list"` on the record returns only the fields
  *this* day actually demands: a compliant, plan-conforming day asks for nothing beyond a
  confirmation, and the § 4 and deviation questions appear only when their gate fires. Do not send
  fields the day did not ask for, and do not ask the operator questions the day did not raise.
- **The reason codes are direction-filtered server-side** from the tenant's Abweichungsgrund
  catalogue (below). Read the offered options from the `list` response rather than re-deriving the
  mapping — a day that ran long can never be filed as "früher gegangen".
- **Named refusals, each with a different fix.** The action reports *why* it is unavailable:
  already closed, a timer still running on that day, nothing tracked at all, a future date, the
  day not resolving to an employee, or the missing permission. Read the reason back to the
  operator instead of retrying.
- **Needs `timesheet_days.close_tracked_day`.** Like the rest of the `timesheet_days.*` family it
  exists but is granted to no role by default — a Disponent who cannot run it needs the
  permission, it is not a rollout gap. It is also blocked while impersonating.
- **The closure is stamped as yours (`closed_by_operator`).** Your prose is the *agency's* account
  of that day, not the employee's, and the flag keeps the two distinguishable on the record. It
  changes nothing about how the day is released or billed. Say this out loud before confirming:
  you are signing a declaration about hours you did not work.
- **Effects to state in the preview:** it creates the day closure; on a newly reported or changed
  break violation it notifies the day's Einsatz owners; and if you set `support_requested: true`
  it also raises a high-priority task for them.
- **The day reaches the Nachweis on its own.** Closing on the employee's behalf dispatches the same
  rebuild every Ist write does, so the period's day row, its Abweichung and the totals pick the day
  up without anyone opening the employee's app (see *Tracked time and the Tagesabschluss rebuild
  the period too*, below). It is queued — read the day back before quoting a number.
- **There is no operator route back.** Withdrawal ("Abschluss zurückziehen") stays employee-app
  only — it is not on `0-427`'s action list. So a day you closed with the wrong reason is undone
  by the employee, or not at all. Preview, and get the operator to read the text, before you
  confirm.
- **Prefer the employee closing it themselves whenever they still can.** This exists for the day
  they *cannot*; it is not a shortcut around asking them, and it replaces their account of the day
  with yours.

### Abweichungsgründe — a tenant-editable catalogue, maintainable over MCP

The categories are **not** a closed enum any more. They are rows of **`ShiftDeviationReason`**, a
tenant catalogue the operator can add to, relabel, reorder and deactivate — in the app under
Einstellungen, and over MCP through the generic model tools. There is deliberately **no dedicated
tool**: `query-model`, `search-model`, `get-model`, `get-model-schema` and `manage-model` all work
on it.

> **Resolve the model type with `list-model-types`; do not hardcode it.** `ShiftDeviationReason`
> is registered as `0-440` — but look the type up by name at runtime rather than pasting that id,
> the same way you would for any other catalogue. If `list-model-types` does not offer it yet, say
> the catalogue is not reachable over MCP rather than guessing an id.

**The fields**, confirmed with `get-model-schema` before any write:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `code` | the stable key the timesheet day stores — **not changeable after creation**, on any row |
| `label` | translatable (de/en), free to rewrite at any time |
| `direction` | `over` \| `under` \| `both` — which deviations the row may explain |
| `sort_order` | display order in the employee's picker |
| `is_active` | inactive rows are not offered; stored history keeps resolving |
| `is_system` | true on the eight platform-seeded rows |

Four rules to respect, each enforced server-side rather than merely by the UI:

- **`code` is fixed at creation.** Days store the code string, with no foreign key, so renaming it
  would silently orphan every closed day that carries it. An update touching `code` is refused.
  "Der Schlüssel ist falsch" is answered by deactivating the row and creating a new one, never by
  editing the key.
- **A system row can be relabelled and deactivated, but never deleted.** An operator asking you to
  remove one of the eight defaults gets a deactivation — `is_active: false` — and say so plainly
  rather than reporting a delete you did not do. A tenant's own row (`is_system: false`) can be
  deleted, but deactivating is still the better answer whenever days already reference it.
- **`direction` is the business rule, not decoration.** It is what stops a day that ran long being
  filed as "früher gegangen" on a § 17c AÜG document the client signs. Set it deliberately when
  creating a row, and never widen an existing row to `both` just to make a code selectable for a
  day it does not fit — that removes a correctness guard from a signed document.
- **Relabelling is retroactive in display, not in history.** The stored code does not move, so a
  rewritten label changes how *past* days read as well. Point that out before renaming a row an
  operator's reporting already leans on.

Writes are two-stage like everywhere else — preview with `confirmed: false`, show the operator the
row as it will be written, then `confirmed: true`:

```
manage-model(model_type: <ShiftDeviationReason id from list-model-types>,
             operation: "create",
             data: { code: "fahrdienst_verspaetet",
                     label: { de: "Fahrdienst verspätet", en: "Transport service delayed" },
                     direction: "under", sort_order: 90, is_active: true },
             confirmed: false)
```

Reading the catalogue is the prerequisite for anything that *names* categories to an operator —
the seeded eight are a starting point, not the tenant's list (`→ triage-data-quality` for
catalogue hygiene generally).

### Changing a closed day — the employee withdraws; you are not blocked

A closed day is locked **employee-side only**. Every self-service write path refuses it, so the
employee withdraws the closure ("Abschluss zurückziehen") and re-closes afterwards.

> **The closure freezes the day's *Schicht* too, not just its Zeiteinträge (since 2026-09-02).**
> „Dienst ändern“ / „Dienst entfällt“ on the Zeiterfassung is gated on the same two facts as
> everything else here — the employee's Tagesabschluss and the Kundenfreigabe — plus a `locked`
> Dienstplan-Periode (Stunden abgerechnet). Two things follow. **Withdrawing the closure reopens
> the roster as well**, so an employee who says "die Zeit stimmt, aber der Dienst war ein anderer"
> is told to withdraw, correct both, and re-close — one round-trip, not a Disposition ticket. And
> **your own `update-shift` is not the mirror image of that**: the operator path keeps the
> calendar lock and refuses a started or past Schicht regardless of whether the day is open
> (`→ build-dienstplan`, step 6). On a day that is open and past, the employee can do what you
> cannot.

> **The employee app has one day surface now: the wizard.** Erfassen, Bearbeiten and Abschluss all
> run through it — it reopens on **"Tag prüfen & abschließen"** prefilled with the *stored* day,
> ends on a review step, and the confirm rewrites the whole day in one transaction and closes it.
> The per-entry editor, the single-segment dialog and the standalone "Tag abschließen" button are
> **gone**; entry rows are read-only. Never coach an employee to "tippe den Eintrag an und bearbeite
> ihn" or to "drück auf Tag abschließen" — that surface does not exist. What is left beside the
> wizard: the live timer (Ein-/Ausstempeln, where enabled) and "Tag leeren" (delete the day's
> entries). **One exception keeps the direct close:** a day carrying entries from *more than one
> Einsatz* cannot be expressed as one span plus breaks, so the wizard stays out of it and the plain
> "Tag abschließen" button remains — that is the only day where the old button is correct.
> The atomic rewrite also refuses a day whose hours are released or approved, and refuses a day
> that is already closed (the withdraw-first rule below is unchanged).

- **Withdrawal is possible only while the day is still theirs — per day, plus one period-level cut.**
  Three things end it, each with a different next step:
  - **the client signed that day** — the employee is refused and pointed at the Disposition; from
    then on the correction is *your* job (see the section below), or a `revoke_release` on the day;
  - **an operator released the day in the Stundenkontrolle** (`operator_approved_at` on `0-427`) —
    the agency has put its own name to those hours, even though the day carries no client
    signature of its own;
  - **the period left the open stage** — it is with the employee for a counter-confirmation
    (`PendingEmployee`), in mediation, released or invoiced. Refusal: *„Dieser Tag wurde bereits
    freigegeben und kann nicht mehr zurückgezogen werden."*
  A period that is still open does **not** block it, including one already sitting with the client:
  a day the client deliberately left open can be withdrawn and re-closed normally. So do not send
  an employee away because "die Woche ist schon beim Kunden" — ask which of the three refusals they
  actually got. Button and write share one predicate, so a "Zurückziehen" the app offers will not
  come back 422.
- **Operator, MCP and zvoove writes are deliberately unaffected.** A closure is not part of
  `TimeEntry::isLocked()` and never locks Disposition out of a day the client has not signed.
- **Withdrawal has no operator route — the asymmetry is deliberate.** You can *close* a day for
  the employee (`close_tracked_day_for_employee`, above), but you cannot take a closure back:
  "Abschluss zurückziehen" is employee-app only and is not on `0-427`'s action list. So a day
  closed with the wrong reason — theirs or yours — is reopened by the employee, and a day the
  client has already signed by a `revoke_release` first. Never promise an operator you can undo
  a Tagesabschluss.
- **Your write does not reopen the day, but it can re-block the release.** The closure stands and
  its stored § 4 snapshot keeps describing the hours as they were at close time — nothing is
  rewritten. The release gate, however, re-runs the break check **live** at release time: if your
  edit pushed a reason-less closed day below the § 4 minimum, the employee's next release fails
  with the "nachträglich geändert" 422 above, and only a withdraw-and-re-close with a reason
  clears it. So after correcting a closed day, re-derive the day's break yourself (step 2)
  instead of assuming the employee's declaration still fits the hours — and if it no longer does,
  tell them, because they are the ones who will hit the wall.

## Released or approved hours are locked — a TimeEntry edit is never the fix

Not to be confused with the Tagesabschluss above: a *closed* day is locked for the employee
only and stays fully editable by you. This section is the harder lock — it binds operators too.

A `TimeEntry` (`0-6`) whose hours are closed is **immutable for everyone**, operators
included. Two closing events, one consequence:

- the entry carries a **company approval**, or
- **its own day was released** against a captured client signature.

> **The lock is per day and per Einsatz — never "the whole week".** A period with one signed day
> leaves every other day open, including for the employee (see *A Kundenfreigabe is an event*
> above), and a day signed off at one client does not lock the same date under a second Einsatz.
> The old week-level rule — first Teilfreigabe locks the period, and everything from the moment it
> reached the client onwards is frozen — is gone. Do not quote it, and do not tell an employee that a period sitting
> with the client puts its unsigned days out of their reach.

A `manage-model` update or delete on such an entry (and equally the schema form in the web
app) is refused by policy. That denial is the guard working, not a missing permission — no
retry, no `confirmed: true`, no broader permission set gets around it. Editing the row would
move hours out from under an existing approval or client signature, the same class of
integrity problem as fabricating a release (see the go-live section above). The employee app
enforces the same rule and stops offering the edit affordance on locked entries, so an
employee reporting "I can't change my hours any more" on a signed day is expected
behaviour, not a bug.

**The client is locked out of a signed day too.** A portal contact can adjust a day's agreed
start/end/break before releasing it, but not afterwards — even while the rest of the period is
still open. A client asking to change a day they already signed needs an operator to revoke that
day's release first (see `revoke_release` above), or the correction flow below.

Correcting closed hours runs through its own path, never by editing the rows underneath the
signature:

- **Client-proposed day correction** ("Korrektur der Dienstzeit beantragen" in the client
  portal): the client proposes new start/end/break for a single day with a reason; the
  **employee accepts or rejects** it. Accepting rewrites the day's tracked *and* agreed span
  with a full audit trail ("via Korrektur #N") and stamps the day as decided by a named human,
  which also freezes it against the rebuild (below); rejecting escalates the week to the operator
  for mediation. This flow has **no MCP write** — it belongs to the client and the employee.

  > **"Der Mitarbeiter war gar nicht da" is its own reason — never an empty correction.** The
  > reasons are `wrong_start`, `wrong_end`, `wrong_break`, `missing_shift`, `extra_shift`,
  > `day_not_worked` ("Mitarbeiter war nicht anwesend") and `other` ("Sonstiges", which requires
  > a note). **`day_not_worked` is the only one that may be submitted without a proposed start
  > and end** — every other reason with both times empty is refused with a 422 (*"A correction
  > must propose a start and an end, or state day_not_worked as its reason."*). So never tell a
  > client or an operator to file "Grund: Sonstiges, Zeiten leer lassen" for a no-show: that used
  > to store the same row as "korrigiere die Zeiten auf null", the employee accepted it without
  > ever seeing that stated, and it now simply fails validation. Accepting a `day_not_worked`
  > correction does write the day's times to nothing and the break to 0 — deliberately, because
  > the statement *is* "der Tag hat nicht stattgefunden" — and the resulting deviation, a full
  > planned shift short, is the honest one. It is a statement about attendance, not a reason for
  > it: if an Abwesenheit covers the day, record it as well (`→ record-absence`).
- **Operator**: mediate an escalated correction in the web app — HR & Payroll → **Stundenklärung**,
  or the "Zur Vermittlung" action on the period in the Stundennachweis list (see above) — or route
  the request to the client/employee. Do not attempt to shortcut it with a TimeEntry edit; it will
  be denied, and there is no MCP path into the mediation flow either.
- **Operator, before the period leaves the client's hands**: `revoke_release` on the day (see
  above) reopens it for normal correction — the right tool for a day the client signed too early or
  with wrong hours, while the period is still `open`. From `PendingEmployee`
  onwards it is refused, and the two routes above are what remains.

## Reading the employee's week back to them (Erfasst · Vereinbart · Abweichung)

The employee's Stundenfreigabe wizard and their hours-confirmation page share one day-row
component. When an operator quotes a number off an employee's screen, this is what it means:

- **Erfasst** — the tracked day, net of the recorded break (Ist span − Ist break).
- **Vereinbart** — the **planned** day: the sum of that day's Schichten, net of the § 4 ArbZG
  break. It is *not* the `agreed_*` fields. The builder writes `agreed_*` equal to the tracked
  time, so this column used to repeat Erfasst and read "-" whenever nothing was tracked, while
  the Abweichung beside it measured against a plan that was never shown.
- **Abweichung** — agreed net minus planned net, i.e. the delta the Vereinbart column now
  actually displays.
- **"Grund der Abweichung"** — on a day that deviates, the row carries the explanation the
  employee gave at Feierabend, printed as `<Kategorie> – <Satz>` (see *Tagesabschluss* below).
  The client sees the same line in the Kundenportal and on the Tätigkeitsnachweis, so an
  employee asking "was sieht der Kunde von meiner Begründung?" gets: exactly this, verbatim.

`agreed_*` itself is unchanged and remains the **billing basis** — only what the employee is
shown changed. Keep the two apart when an employee argues about a number: Vereinbart on their
screen is the Dienstplan, the billed figure is the agreed span (`→ build-dienstplan` to correct
the plan; a client or operator correction to the agreed span is what a counter-confirmation
compares).

> **A past day's Schicht cannot be re-planned — only a missing one added.** Editing,
> cancelling or deleting a Schicht dated before today is hard-blocked outright, so a
> disputed number on a day that has passed is settled in time tracking here, never by
> rewriting the plan behind it. What *is* possible on a past day is entering a Schicht that
> was never recorded (nachtragen), with the operator's explicit `past_date_acknowledged`
> confirmation and only inside the backfill window — the current month by default
> (`→ build-dienstplan`, step 3). Keep the two apart before promising a correction.
>
> **Once the month's Dienstplan-Periode is `locked`, even the nachtragen is refused** —
> `add-shift` comes back with *"the hours for this month are already approved or invoiced —
> correct them in the Stundenklärung, not in the Dienstplan"*. That is not an ArbZG block and
> no flag releases it: the message names the route, and it is this skill. A `pending_approval`
> month refuses a new Schicht too (withdraw it from approval first). So on an approved or
> invoiced month, settle the number here and stop looking for a way into the plan.

**A day is not a deviation until its planned shift has ended.** A day that is today or later
and whose planned Schicht still runs or lies ahead is flagged clean and contributes zero
Abweichung — opening the wizard on a Monday used to report the rest of the week as `missing`
with amber minus-rows and set `has_deviation` on the running week. The cut-off is the shift's
own **end**, not midnight, so a half-tracked day (or a timer still running) is not a shortfall
either. Once the planned end has passed the day is measured normally, including a day with no
tracking at all — that is the real `missing` case. A day with no plan at all (`extra` work) is
never deferred.

Consequence: `has_deviation` on a **current** week now means something real. Do not dismiss it
as "the week isn't over yet" — look at the days whose shift has already ended.

**A Kranktag is not a deviation — and a *submitted* Krankmeldung already makes it one.** A day
covered by a declared absence (`submitted`, `pending`, `needs_revision` or `approved` — not a
never-submitted `draft`, not `rejected`, not `cancelled`) keeps `deviation_minutes` at 0 and
`has_deviation` false, so the week no longer goes to `mediation` over a sick day while the
Krankmeldung waits for approval. Two edges to keep straight:

- **Only when NOTHING was tracked on that day.** If time was logged, the day was worked, and a
  late Krankmeldung does not zero a real Über-/Unterschreitung — that deviation stands and is
  investigated normally.
- **The Soll survives.** The day keeps its planned times (see the `replacement_needed` bullet in
  the Tagesabschluss section above), so the Nachweis documents "geplant · Krank" rather than a day
  that silently never existed. Only the deviation is neutralised. Whether the client ever *sees*
  that row is a separate question — `taetigkeitsnachweis_show_absence_days` is off by default and
  drops it (see *Wo ist der Kranktag hin?* above).
- **And it no longer triggers a counter-confirmation either.** `has_deviation` was already false
  on such a week, but the client-confirmation check compared the day's break separately and sent
  the week to `PendingEmployee` over the inherited Schicht-Pause alone. That is fixed — a sick
  week now goes straight to `approved` (see *Counter-confirmation is triggered by a client edit*
  above). Do not go looking for it in the `PendingEmployee` view.

**Tracked time and the Tagesabschluss rebuild the period too — not only absences.** A
`TimesheetDay` used to be written once when the period was materialised and then never revisited:
the only production caller of the rebuild was the employee's own Stundenfreigabe page, so a period
healed if and only if somebody opened that screen, and an operator reading a day shortly after it
was worked saw stale Ist times — "Keine Zeit erfasst, kein Grund angegeben" on a day the employee
had tracked and closed hours earlier. Every Ist writer now triggers the same rebuild the absence
path uses: the employee app, the zvoove import, an operator correction, a generic `manage-model`
write on a `TimeEntry` — and every Tagesabschluss, re-close and withdrawal too, since the day row
carries the Abweichungsgrund off the closure. Moving an entry across midnight refreshes the old
**and** the new day. So any "warte, bis der Mitarbeiter seine Stundenfreigabe öffnet" advice is
obsolete. Three things to keep straight: the rebuild is **queued**, so read the day back rather
than quoting it in the same breath; it touches only still-open periods and skips every day a
human has decided (below); and on a day nobody has signed or edited it re-derives `agreed_*` from
the tracking — so an agreed span written by anything that does *not* stamp the day as
human-decided (a raw `manage-model` write on the day row, say) is not durable, the next entry or
Feierabend on that day overwrites it.


**Absence changes rebuild the affected weeks by themselves.** Creating, approving, rejecting or
deleting an Abwesenheit re-derives every overlapping period still `open` — submitted to the client
or not — in both directions: submitting a Krankmeldung clears a gap already stored on the week, and
rejecting one brings the deviation back. Periods in `pending_employee`, `mediation` and beyond
are **deliberately never rewritten** — so a week already in `mediation` is not repaired by approving
the Krankmeldung after the fact. The route for those stays the Stundenklärung (mediation), and that
is what to offer the operator.

> **Inside a rebuilt period, a day a human decided is frozen.** The rebuild skips two kinds of
> day: one the client has **signed**, and one a named person has **edited** — the employee
> accepting a client correction, the client editing the agreed times in the portal, the client
> accepting an agency-proposed correction on an approved day. Their Soll,
> Ist, agreed span and deviation stand exactly as decided, while the period's still-open days are
> re-derived normally. Their stored figures still count toward the period's totals; frozen means
> "not re-derived", not "not counted". So an `open` period holding both decided and open days is
> partially rebuilt, and a number a person put their name to never moves on its own. If such a day
> genuinely holds the wrong values, revoke that day's release (above) or run another correction —
> do not expect a rebuild to correct it.
>
> **The edit half of that rule is new, and the old advice was the opposite.** A day under
> correction is by definition *not* client-signed, so the signature alone left the exact case it
> was written for unprotected: the client disputed a day, the employee accepted from the app, and
> the employee's own page re-derived it straight back to the Dienstplan one second later — five
> accepts, five rebuilds, no surviving change. Never tell anyone to "just re-open the page" or
> "wait for the rebuild" to make an accepted correction stick, and never explain a correction that
> appears to have vanished as expected behaviour: on current alluvo it sticks, and a day that
> reverted is worth reporting.
>
> **"Korrigiert" and "geprüft" are two different statements on the Tage card.** A day is flagged
> as corrected only when a binding value actually **differs** from what was tracked; a day a person
> reviewed and left exactly as it stood shows as touched by a human but not as corrected. So do not
> read "von X bearbeitet" as "X changed the hours", and do not send an operator hunting for a
> change that was never made.

## Adjacent capability: reimbursement review (MCP-capable)

Unlike Stundenzettel, submitted **reimbursements** (Spesen/Auslagen) CAN be reviewed via
MCP. `Reimbursement` is model type `0-20`:

- Find submissions: `query-model` on `0-20` filtered by `status: "submitted"` (statuses:
  `draft`, `submitted`, `approved`, `rejected`, `needs_revision`, `paid`).
- **Name a record by its `title`.** That is the identifier alluvo shows everywhere — a derived,
  never-empty string: "Stadt Köln · 14.08.2026" for an Auslage (vendor + Belegdatum),
  "Reisekosten Köln · 03.–05.08.2026" for Reisekosten. `search-model` on `0-20` returns it as
  the identity column. Do **not** quote `purpose`: it is NULL on every receipt-driven Auslage,
  which is exactly why it stopped being the identifier. Because `title` is derived and has no
  column behind it, you cannot filter or sort on it — use the real fields below.
- **Filters on `0-20`** — the five the operator sees as chips are `status`, `employee_id`,
  `type`, `needs_clarification`, `authorization_status`; also filterable are `grand_total`,
  `amount`, `assignment_id`, `reimbursement_category_id`, `approver_id`, `authorized_by_id`,
  `payout_method`, `approved_at`, `paid_at`, `purpose`, `destination`, `start_date`, `end_date`.
  **`start_date`/`end_date` are set by Reisekosten only** — filtering or sorting a mixed list on
  them silently drops every Auslage. Sort by `created_at` for "the most recent ones"; that is
  the list's default order.
- **The Beleg behind an Auslage** carries the two facts the reimbursement record itself does
  not have: `vendor_name` and `document_date` (the Belegdatum — the one date an Auslage has).
  `ReimbursementReceipt` (`0-26`) has **no direct generic MCP surface** — `search-model`/
  `query-model` on `0-26` are refused. Reach it as a relation instead:
  `query-model` on `0-20` with
  `include: { receipts: { fields: ["title", "vendor_name", "document_date", "amount_gross"] } }`.
  A receipt's own label is `title` as well, not `vendor_name` (the extraction leaves that null
  often enough that it is not a reliable name).
- Lifecycle runs **only** through `manage-record-action` on `0-20` — action names
  `approve`, `reject`, `request_revision` (the latter two take a `reason`), `mark_as_paid`
  (optional `payout_method`, `bank_account_id`), `submit` and `cancel`. Preview first
  (`confirmed: false` or omitted), then execute with `confirmed: true`. Those are the
  **record** actions. `0-20` also carries one **index** action — `generate-period-statements`
  (Sammel-Reisekostenabrechnung, own section below) — which runs on the model type as a whole
  and therefore needs `operation: "execute_index"`, not `execute`.
- **`submit` is open for Stellvertretung; `cancel` is not — and that asymmetry is deliberate.**
  The owner submits their own record on the create permission; anyone else needs the same
  scoped `reimbursements.edit` that editing the record needs, which is what lets you submit a
  Draft you filed for an employee who never saw it (`submit_beleg`, below). `cancel` stays
  **owner-only**: it resets `submitted` straight back to `draft`, and a draft is freely
  editable — a non-owner cancel would be a full bypass of the immutability of `submitted`
  (submitted → cancel → change amounts → resubmit) with no reviewer and no trail. Never offer
  cancel as an operator capability; the sanctioned way out of `submitted` for anyone but the
  owner is `reject` or `request_revision`.
- **`submit` validates required fields, and a receipt-filed Auslage fails them.** Beyond the
  state gate, `submit` refuses unless `type`, `reimbursement_category_id`, `purpose`,
  `start_date`, `end_date`, `amount` and `employee_id` are all set (plus `occasion_type` on
  Reisekosten), and unless every attached receipt has its document. An Auslage the extraction
  built carries none of `purpose`, `reimbursement_category_id`, `start_date`, `end_date` — so
  submitting one on an employee's behalf fails with "Cannot submit reimbursement: …" listing
  them. Fill them via `manage-model` first (the record is `draft`, so field updates are
  accepted), then run `submit`. The employee's own app submits over a different path that does
  not carry this validation, which is why a record can be submittable for them and not for you.
- **Every transition is state-gated — read the status before you promise an action.** Nine
  actions carry a hard precondition that no permission level bypasses (it is plain PHP, not a
  Gate check, so a platform admin does not skip it either):

  | Action | Runs only when |
  |---|---|
  | `submit` | status `draft`, `needs_revision` |
  | `cancel` | status `submitted`, `needs_revision` (→ back to `draft`) |
  | `approve` | status `submitted` |
  | `reject` | status `submitted` |
  | `request_revision` | status `submitted` |
  | `mark_as_paid` | status `approved` |
  | `confirm_authorization` | status `submitted` **and** `authorization_status: pending` |
  | `decline_authorization` | status `submitted` **and** `authorization_status: pending` |
  | `resolve_clarification` | `needs_clarification: true` |

  On the wrong status the action is **not gone from `list`** — it still appears with
  `**Available:** No` and an `**Unavailable reason:**` naming the block. Do not report a state
  block as a missing permission. `execute` on it is refused with
  "Action '<name>' is not available for record <id>." plus `Current record status: <status>`.
  So the fix is always to name the status back to the operator ("die Auslage ist bereits
  eingereicht"), never to retry or to route it to someone with more rights.

  **Three of them name the actual blocker instead of the generic state text** — quote the reason
  you got back rather than matching on one fixed string:

  | Action | Unavailable reason when the state gate blocks it |
  |---|---|
  | `confirm_authorization`, `decline_authorization` | "This reimbursement is not waiting on an authorization — there is nothing to confirm or decline." (DE: "Diese Erstattung wartet auf keine Freigabe — es gibt keine Freigabe zu bestätigen oder abzulehnen.") |
  | `resolve_clarification` | "This reimbursement has no open clarification to resolve." (DE: "Diese Erstattung hat keine offene Rückfrage, die geklärt werden könnte.") |
  | `mark_as_paid` | "Only an approved reimbursement can be marked as paid." (DE: "Nur eine genehmigte Erstattung kann als bezahlt markiert werden.") |

  The other six report the generic state text — "This action is not available in the record's
  current state." (DE: "Diese Aktion ist im aktuellen Status des Datensatzes nicht verfügbar.").
  All of these are *different* strings from the permission reason ("You do not have permission…"
  / "Dir fehlt die Berechtigung für diese Aktion.").

  **On those three, state is reported before permission.** The same precondition also sits in the
  policy, so a record with nothing pending fails the permission check too — and the reason you
  see still names the state. Take it at face value: a "not waiting on an authorization" answer to
  the named Freigeber is exactly that, not a hint that they lack rights.
- **The Freigabe pair is gated on `authorization_status`, not only on status.**
  `confirm_authorization` and `decline_authorization` run only while the Freigabe is still
  `pending`, because confirming an already-confirmed record re-routes it into the owner's queue
  and produces a second approval task. This is not a one-way door: every (re)submission resets
  `authorization_status` to `pending`, so a declined record that the employee resubmits is
  confirmable again. `resolve_clarification` is gated on the **flag**
  (`needs_clarification: true`), not on status — there is nothing to resolve on an unflagged
  record.
- You cannot approve your own reimbursement.
- **Two gates block `approve`** — check both before promising an approval, and read them off
  the record (`query-model` on `0-20` filters on `needs_clarification` and on
  `authorization_status`: `pending` / `confirmed` / `declined`):
  - `needs_clarification: true` → `approve` fails until the flag is cleared with
    `resolve_clarification`.
  - a named Freigeber (`authorized_by_id`) whose `authorization_status` is not yet
    `confirmed` → `approve` fails until that person confirms.
- **Naming a Freigeber is optional by default.** The Freigeber:in step is tenant-flagged
  (`EmployeeReimbursementFreigeber`) and **off** unless a tenant switched it on, so most records
  arrive with `authorized_by_id: null` and the authorization gate above simply does not apply —
  read it off the record instead of assuming an approver is missing. Where the flag *is* on, it
  makes a Freigeber mandatory only on an **Auslagen** (`EXPENSES`) record that has none on file
  yet; **Reisekosten** (`TRAVEL_EXPENSES`) stays optional throughout, and a resubmit after
  `decline_authorization` reuses the Freigeber already recorded rather than demanding a new one.
- **Clearing a clarification.** `resolve_clarification` (optional `note`) clears
  `needs_clarification` + `clarification_reason` and unblocks the record for `approve`/`reject`
  — it does **not** approve anything, run the approval afterwards. Needs the same permission as
  `approve`. The `note` is stored as the `review_note` prefixed with the resolver's name and
  **replaces** any prior review note, so quote what was clarified rather than a bare "ok".
- **The Freigeber sub-flow** (`confirm_authorization` / `decline_authorization`) is gated to
  the named Freigeber themselves or a tenant admin, on a `submitted` record whose
  `authorization_status` is `pending` — a Disponent reviewing someone else's queue cannot confirm
  on their behalf, so route it to that person instead of retrying.
  - **A record with no named Freigeber has nothing to confirm.** `authorization_status` is null
    whenever `authorized_by_id` is null, so neither action is available there — not to a tenant
    admin either. On such a record the answer is `approve` (or `reject`) directly; do not look for
    an authorization step that was never started.
  - `confirm_authorization` stamps `authorization_confirmed_at` and only *then* hands the
    reimbursement to the owner's approval queue (owner notification + approval task). That is
    why a reimbursement awaiting authorization is deliberately missing from the queue — it is
    not a lost record.
  - `decline_authorization` requires a `reason` and is **not** a rejection: it sets
    `authorization_status: declined` and moves the record to `needs_revision`, notifying the
    employee so they can correct and resubmit. Use `reject` for a final, non-correctable no.
- Writes via `manage-model` create **drafts only** (any other `status` is rejected/clamped);
  field updates are accepted only while the record is `draft`/`needs_revision`. Status
  transitions never happen via a field update — `status` sent on update is silently
  stripped; use the actions above.
- **The payout sum is `grand_total`, never `amount`.** `grand_total` is what actually gets paid
  out — per-diems, transport, receipts, items *and* the manual add-on folded together.
  `amount` is only that **manual add-on** ("Manueller Zuschlag") and is `0` on every Auslage
  the employee app produced, so quoting it reports "0,00 €" for real money. Both are in EUR
  (e.g. `42.50`), never cents.

The READ-ONLY note above applies to Stundenzettel; a reimbursement approval is a real
write — always show the operator the preview (record, action, reason) before confirming.

## Sammel-Reisekostenabrechnung — one statement PDF per employee (index action)

Once reimbursements are approved, payroll's next step is the **Sammel-Reisekostenabrechnung**:
one aggregated Reisekostenabrechnung PDF per employee covering everything up to a Stichtag,
filed in each Personalakte and mailed to the Lohnbuchhaltung as a ZIP. It is an **index action**
on `0-20` — it applies to the model type, not to one record — so it is not in the record-action
table above and needs `operation: "execute_index"`:

```
manage-record-action operation:"execute_index" model_type:"0-20"
  action:"generate-period-statements" confirmed:false          # preview
manage-record-action operation:"execute_index" model_type:"0-20"
  action:"generate-period-statements" data:{ cutoff_date: "2026-08-31" } confirmed:true
```

- **`cutoff_date` (YYYY-MM-DD) is required**, and a forward-dated Stichtag is deliberately
  allowed — "alles bis Monatsende" is what payroll types mid-month. The only ceiling is the end
  of next month, which catches a mistyped year. Selection stays limited to what is already
  approved, so a future Stichtag cannot pull in more than exists.
- **The preview is thin — it is not a dry run.** `confirmed: false` on an index action prints
  only the model type, the action and its label: there is no computed preview, and
  `cutoff_date` is **not** validated at that point. Still run it (two-stage is the sanctioned
  shape), but read the Stichtag back to the operator yourself instead of expecting the preview
  to check it.
- **Tenant-gated, and its absence proves nothing.** The action sits behind the
  `ReimbursementPeriodStatements` feature flag, **off by default**. On a tenant that has not
  been switched on, `operation: "list"` on `0-20` simply **omits** it from the Index-Actions
  section — never report "das gibt es nicht" off a `list` result. Executing it there fails with
  `Index action 'generate-period-statements' is not available (canBePerformed() returned false).`
  That one string covers **both** the flag being off and the operator lacking the
  `reimbursements.export` permission, and does not say which. Report it as "für diesen Mandanten
  nicht freigeschaltet oder Berechtigung fehlt" and route it to an admin — do not retry.

**What gets selected — Leistungsdatum, not the approval date.** A reimbursement is swept in when
all of these hold:

| Condition | Detail |
|---|---|
| Status | `approved` or `paid` only — a `submitted` record is never picked up |
| Not yet batched | it carries no `statement_batch_id` yet |
| Employee | `employee_id` is set |
| Leistungsdatum ≤ Stichtag | earliest receipt `service_start_date`, falling back to its `document_date`; a Reisekosten record with no receipts at all falls back to its own `start_date` |

There is deliberately **no `created_at` window**: a July receipt approved in November is still
picked up. Never tell an operator that a late-entered Beleg "fell out of the period" — it lands
in the next run under its own Leistungsdatum.

**Re-running is safe, and warning against it would be wrong.** The run claims each reimbursement
by writing `statement_batch_id` on it, and the selection skips anything already claimed. Firing
the same Stichtag twice therefore selects nothing the second time and files no duplicate, and a
straggler approved afterwards is picked up by the next run.

**It is asynchronous — the tool call is not the result.** The action opens the batch, queues it
and returns "Sammelabrechnung gestartet" immediately; the requester is notified when the run
finishes. Report it as *gestartet*, never as *fertig*, and never quote employee counts or totals
off the call.

**What each employee gets.** One `EmployeeDocument` of type `reimbursement_period_statement` in
the Personalakte, carrying two files — the statement PDF plus a machine-readable JSON sidecar —
named `Sammelabrechnung_<Nachname>_<Personalnummer>_<MMJJJJ>`. It is a GoBD archive document:
category Payroll, **10-year retention**, visibility **HrOnly** — the employee does **not** see
it in their app, so do not promise them a copy. It is a different type from the per-approval
`reimbursement_statement`; both can sit on the same Personalakte, and repeated runs add further
statements rather than replacing the previous one.

**The printed form has its own ten fixed rows** (`ReimbursementStatementPosition`: Tankkosten,
Öl/Schmierstoffe, Parkkosten, ÖPNV, Fortbildungskosten, Übernachtung, Verpflegung, Sonstiges,
plus Verpflegungsmehraufwand and Kilometerpauschale as flat rates with no Vorsteuer column).
These are deliberately **not** the receipt categories carried on `0-20` — never tell an operator
the PDF is grouped by Belegkategorie.

**Delivery is configured, and can silently reach nobody.** The ZIP goes to
`statement_recipient_emails` on `manage-settings` group **`reimbursements`** (comma- or
semicolon-separated; entries that are not valid addresses are dropped). **Empty is not an
error**: the batch still runs, the documents are still filed and the reimbursements are still
claimed — only the external mail is skipped. Check the setting *before* the run and say so,
otherwise the operator will believe the Lohnbuchhaltung was mailed. `statement_show_empty_positions`
(bool, default `false`) controls whether the fixed rows with no amount are printed at all.

```
manage-settings(group: "reimbursements", action: "get")
manage-settings(group: "reimbursements", action: "update",
                fields: { "statement_recipient_emails": "lohn@example.de" })
```

**Verifying the run — what you can and cannot read back.** The batch itself has **no MCP
surface**: there is no model type for it, no export record to query, and `statement_batch_id`
is not a filter on `0-20`. What you *can* check is the Personalakte —
`manage-employee-document` `action: "audit"` with an `employee_id` returns a
`reimbursement_period_statement` row
that flips to `present` once a statement is filed (it reflects the most recent one only and
does not say which run produced it). For per-employee failures, totals or the ZIP download,
point the operator at the notification / the recipient mail / the app. One employee failing
never aborts the run — the batch counts as failed only when every employee failed.

## Filing a Beleg for an employee — a real action you can run

An operator can file a receipt **on an employee's behalf**: `submit_beleg` ("Beleg einreichen"),
a record action on the Employee record (`0-2`), also in the "…" menu in the web app. It needs
`update` permission on that employee and runs the exact same extraction pipeline as the
employee's own app.

**Run it with `beleg_base64`, not `beleg`.** The action has two upload fields and you use the
second: `beleg` is the browser's file picker (image or PDF, max 10 MB) and cannot be filled
from a tool call; `beleg_base64` takes the same file **Base64-encoded** and exists for exactly
this path. Preview with `confirmed: false`, then execute with `confirmed: true`:

```
manage-record-action  model_type: "0-2"  record_id: <employee>  action: "submit_beleg"
                      data: { beleg_base64: "<base64>" }  confirmed: true
```

- The format is read off the file's own magic bytes, not off a name you supply — **JPEG, PNG
  and PDF only**; anything else is refused with "Nicht unterstütztes Dateiformat".
- Malformed input fails loudly rather than filing an empty job: no file and no Base64 →
  "Bitte lade eine Datei hoch."; undecodable Base64 → "Ungültiges Base64 …".
- **An employee with neither their own user account nor a manager who has one cannot receive a
  Beleg at all** — the action refuses up front ("Dieser Mitarbeitende hat weder ein eigenes
  Nutzerkonto noch eine vorgesetzte Person mit einem"). Nothing is written. Get the employee
  invited to the portal (`→ onboard-new-employee`) rather than retrying.
- The Base64 goes through the tool call as one string. A phone photo is easily several MB
  before encoding; where the file is large, direct the operator to the record page instead of
  pushing it through here.

**The call creates a Draft Auslage immediately — and it is empty at first.** Before queueing
the extraction, the action opens a `Reimbursement` (`0-20`, `type: auslagen`, `status: draft`,
`amount: 0`) on that employee and binds the upload to it. So `query-model` on `0-20` filtered
by `employee_id` + `status: "draft"` **does** show the record right after the call — with zero
receipts on it. The receipt itself lands only once the AI extraction finishes, seconds to
minutes later. Report "Entwurf angelegt, Auslesung läuft", never "Auslage mit Beleg erfasst",
and re-read with the `receipts` include before you quote a vendor or an amount.

**Three outcomes, and two of them leave the Draft empty.** After extraction:

| Outcome | What you find |
|---|---|
| Clean extraction | The receipt is filed onto the Draft automatically and `grand_total` recalculated. This is the normal case. |
| A **warning** — duplicate suspicion, or several separate receipts on one scan (`multiple_documents`) | The receipt is deliberately **not** filed. The Draft stands with zero receipts and the upload waits for a human. |
| Extraction failed, or the filing itself threw | Same: Draft with zero receipts. |

In all three not-landed cases the **acting operator is notified** ("Beleg konnte nicht …",
linking to the reimbursement), so name the possibility when you report the upload rather than
promising a finished Auslage. You no longer have to wait for that notification to find out
which case it was: the upload itself is queryable as model type `0-29` (next section) and
carries the `status`, the `error` and the `warnings`. A Draft that stayed empty is not
self-healing: it needs the Beleg re-filed or the draft cleaned up in the app — and the dead
upload behind it discarded.

**Doppelte Belege are warned about, and on the employee's own path not blocked.** The extraction
checks each upload against the employee's own receipts of the last 120 days — the identical
file (hash) and, failing that, the same expense read off a different photo (same document date
+ gross amount, sharpened by the document number where the extraction read one). A hit becomes
a warning in the Beleg-Assistent that the employee can override by confirming anyway; a
reimbursement that was already `rejected`/`needs_revision` is treated as resolved and never
counts as the duplicate. **The warning lives on the upload, not on the record** — there is
still no duplicate flag on `0-20`, so never report "kein Duplikat" off a `query-model` result on
the reimbursement. The upload itself is readable now, though: the queue behind the Beleg is
model type `0-29` (next section) and carries `content_sha256`. Check a suspected double
submission on both levels — `query-model` on `0-29` by `employee_id` for two uploads sharing a
`content_sha256` (byte-identical file), and `query-model` on `0-20` by `employee_id` with the
`receipts` include for two records whose receipts share a `document_date` and an `amount_gross`
(the same receipt photographed twice has two different hashes).

**On the operator path that same warning blocks.** There is no review screen behind
`submit_beleg` for anyone to override a warning on, so instead of filing the receipt and
flagging it, alluvo files nothing — the empty-Draft row of the table above. The job's own
`warnings` on `0-29` name which warning fired; run the two-level duplicate comparison above
before re-filing the same Beleg, and do not read the block as a failure to retry around.

**There is a second duplicate check at submit time — and it fires where the upload one cannot.**
When the employee submits from the app, alluvo asks whether this record is about to become a
**second live claim** on an already-claimed receipt: same employee, another reimbursement of the
last 120 days whose status is `submitted`/`approved`/`paid`, matched on the identical file hash
or else on the same `document_date` + `amount_gross` (sharpened by `document_number` when both
sides have one). A hit refuses the submission once, naming the offending claim (vendor, date,
amount); the employee confirms and the retry goes through. This is deliberately *not* the upload
check: that one keys off the upload job (none exists at submit time) and treats
`rejected`/`needs_revision` as resolved — which is exactly how three rejected duplicates were
walked back through the correction dialog and resubmitted unchanged, putting 26,10 € of double
claims back on the pile on 2026-08-19.

**Two things follow for you.** First, this gate lives on the **employee app's submit endpoint,
not on the `submit` action** — a `submit` run through `manage-record-action` bypasses it
entirely. So submitting on an employee's behalf gives you *no* duplicate warning; run the
`0-29` / `0-20` comparison above yourself first. Second, a record reaching your approval
queue is **not** certified unique: it either had no hit or the employee acknowledged one. Never
tell an operator a claim is duplicate-free because it was submitted.

**Whether the employee sees a draft depends on who is recorded as its creator.** The employee's
own list shows every non-draft record, but only the **drafts they created themselves**. Two
cases, and they differ:

- A draft **you** created with `manage-model` on `0-20` records you as creator, so it stays
  hidden from the employee until it is submitted. Never tell an operator "the employee can see
  it now" about one of these.
- A draft `submit_beleg` created is deliberately booked as if the employee had opened it
  themselves — the employee is its creator, the owner is their manager (falling back to the
  employee) — so it **does** appear in their list, and the submit/approval routing behaves
  exactly as for a self-filed Auslage.

**Submitting that Draft on the employee's behalf is allowed, but not one call.** The Draft the
extraction filled still lacks `purpose`, `reimbursement_category_id`, `start_date` and
`end_date`, which `submit` requires — set them with `manage-model` while the record is `draft`,
then run `submit` (see "`submit` validates required fields" above). Leaving the Draft standing
for the employee to submit themselves is the other legitimate answer, and usually the better
one: they see it in their list, and their own submit path runs the duplicate check yours skips.

The same acting-on-behalf-of rule now covers the whole extraction pipeline in the app — Beleg,
**Stundenzettel** and **Dienstplan** alike: an operator acts for an employee only where they may
edit that employee, and a missing permission is refused outright rather than quietly filing the
document onto the operator themselves.

## The upload queue behind a Beleg (`0-29`) — readable, and discardable

Every file an employee snaps in the app — Beleg, Reisekosten, Dienstplan, Stundenzettel — first
becomes an **ExtractionUploadJob**, model type `0-29`, and only turns into a record once the
extraction commits it. That queue used to be invisible over MCP, so an upload that never landed
looked like nothing at all. It is readable through the generic tools now.

**Read-only.** `query-model` / `get-model` / `search-model` / `count-model` work on `0-29`;
`manage-model` create/update is refused, because every field there is written by the employee
app or by a processor. The single write is the `discard_upload` action below.

- **List fields:** `original_filename`, `employee_id`, `kind`, `status`, `source`,
  `file_size_bytes`, `content_sha256`, `created_at` (default sort: newest first).
- **`get-model` adds** `basket_key` (the app's capture session), `committed_at`, `error`,
  `extracted_payload`, `warnings` and `committed_record_ids` — the last one names the records
  the commit produced, i.e. the link from an upload to its Reimbursement.
- **Filters:** `employee_id`, `status`, `kind`, `source`, `content_sha256`, `created_at` range.
- **`status`:** `pending` (queued or running) · `extracted` (read, waiting for the employee to
  confirm) · `failed` · `committed` (the record exists) · `discarded`.
- **`kind`:** `beleg` · `reisekosten` · `dienstplan` · `stundenzettel` · `payslip` (a
  candidate's Gehaltsabrechnung on the guest Personalfragebogen — the only kind whose job has
  no `employee_id`).
- **`source`:** `upload` (the employee's own app) · `manual` · `operator` (filed by an operator
  via `submit_beleg`; that job has no `basket_key`, so the employee never sees it in their app
  and nobody else is going to confirm it by hand).

**Two operator questions this makes answerable.**

1. *"Hat sie denselben Beleg zweimal hochgeladen?"* — `query-model` on `0-29` by `employee_id`
   and compare `content_sha256`. Two rows sharing it are the same bytes. The same receipt
   photographed twice has different bytes, so that case still needs the `document_date` +
   `amount_gross` comparison on `0-20` from the section above — run both, in that order.
2. *"Der Beleg / der Dienstplan fehlt, obwohl er hochgeladen wurde."* — a job stuck in `pending`
   or sitting in `failed` is visible instead of unexplainably absent. Read its `error` and
   `warnings` before you tell the operator anything about why the record is missing.

**Looking at the file itself.** `get-attachment` has a third source, `extraction_upload_job`,
whose `attachment_id` is the **job's own id** (not a ticket or Email attachment id). It returns
the raw uploaded file before any record exists — the only way to see a queued
Beleg/Dienstplan/Stundenzettel. Handling is the same as for the other sources (images inline,
PDFs as extracted text or up to four rendered pages, 5 MB cap), and so is the principle behind
the access rule: you must be able to view the job *and* its employee.

**Discarding a dead upload — a real, two-stage write.** `discard_upload` on `0-29` deletes the
stored file and sets the job to `discarded`. The row itself stays, so the queue keeps the record
that the file existed and was dropped on purpose. Preview, show it to the operator, then
confirm:

```
manage-record-action  operation: "execute"  model_type: "0-29"  record_id: <job>
                      action: "discard_upload"
manage-record-action  operation: "execute"  model_type: "0-29"  record_id: <job>
                      action: "discard_upload"  confirmed: true
```

- **It is destructive and not reversible** — the bytes are gone. Name the `original_filename`
  and the employee in the preview you show, and never discard on a hunch that something is
  "probably a duplicate": confirm against `content_sha256` first.
- A **`committed`** job cannot be discarded. `operation: "list"` reports it as
  `Available: No` with a state reason, because the receipt or Dienstplan it produced still
  points at that file. To undo a committed upload, delete the record it produced instead.
- `pending`, `extracted` and `failed` are all discardable — a failed extraction is exactly the
  case an operator needs to clear.
- It needs `delete` permission on the `extraction_upload_jobs` resource; reading needs `view`.
  A missing permission is refused outright, never worked around.

## Adjacent capability: commute allowance (Wegstreckenpauschale)

The monthly commute allowance is **not** a Reimbursement and has **no approval flow** — it is
live-derived and display-only, rendered on the employee's Salary tab in the web app. There is
**no MCP tool** for it: you cannot read the computed figure, approve it, or export it. Never
promise a payroll or DATEV export of this amount, and never file it as a `0-20` Reimbursement.

What you *can* do via MCP is set the entitlement on the employee's Salary:

- `commute_allowance_entitlement` (Salary, writable via `manage-model` create **and** update,
  preview → confirm): `contractual` (always paid, regardless of the tenant setting),
  `company_policy` (default — follows the tenant setting), `none` (no entitlement).
- The per-employee rate override is the **existing** `distance_allowance_rate` field
  (EUR per km, label "Entfernungspauschale"). There is no `commute_allowance_rate` field on
  Salary — do not invent one. Unset, the tenant default rate applies.

One thing you must direct the operator to the app for:

- **`double_commute_distance`** ("Doppelte Wegstrecke") on the Assignment — a real field, but
  Assignment is not `manage-model`-manageable. Set it in the Einsatz form.

**The tenant-wide toggle and default rate ARE MCP-writable** — `manage-settings` group
`reimbursements`, fields `commute_allowance_enabled` (bool) and `commute_allowance_rate_cents`
(Eurocents for the ONE-WAY commute, 0–200). The same group also carries `default_payout_method`,
`employee_requestable`, `car_km_rate_cents` (the § 9 EStG Dienstreise-Kilometerpauschale — not
the Pendlerpauschale), `commute_allowance_wage_type_id` and the two Sammelabrechnung keys above.
Its web page moved under Einstellungen → Apps → Reisekosten/Erstattungen. Still no MCP surface
for the *computed* monthly figure — that stays display-only.

The calculation basis is **attended shifts** (Dienstantritt): a shift counts when it is not
cancelled, its Dienstplan period is Published or Locked, and no approved AbsencePeriod of the
employee overlaps that date. Two shifts on one day count as two commutes. Use this definition
whenever you reason about "angetretene Dienste" — a shift lands on the payroll basis only once
the Dienstplan is published, and an approved Krankmeldung removes it. A company car
(EmployeeVehicle valid in the month) or a missing/ungeocoded home address zeroes the figure
out entirely.

## Output

- Table: employee · assignment · month · submitted hours · status
- Anomalies (ArbZG-relevant days, missing information)
- Note on where the approval happens: the client signs (portal / on site / Frist), the web app
  under HR & Payroll → Stundennachweise (or → Stundenklärung for a period in `mediation`) holds the
  period, and for an approved one the "Tätigkeitsnachweis öffnen" action
- Where a period is stuck because the client will not sign, or was approved in error: the
  period-level action to run (`approve_by_operator` / `revoke_approval` on `0-426`), what it
  changes, and — for an operator release — the resulting status read back, since a deviating
  period lands in `PendingEmployee`, not `Approved`
